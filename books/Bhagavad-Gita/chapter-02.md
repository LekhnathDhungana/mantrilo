# Chapter 2 – Sāṅkhya Yoga (The Yoga of Knowledge)

*Yoga of Knowledge*

### 2.1

सञ्जय उवाच ।  
तं तथा कृपयाविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम् ।  
विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः ॥ २-१॥

*sañjaya uvāca  
taṃ tathā kṛpayāviṣṭam aśrupūrṇākulekṣaṇam  
viṣīdantamidaṃ vākyamuvāca madhusūdanaḥ 2-1*

Sanjaya said: To Arjuna, who was overwhelmed in this way by pity and despair, his eyes brimming with tears and his mind in turmoil, Madhusudana spoke these words.

### 2.2

श्रीभगवानुवाच ।  
कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।  
अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥ २-२॥

*śrībhagavānuvāca  
kutastvā kaśmalamidaṃ viṣame samupasthitam  
anāryajuṣṭamasvargyamakīrtikaramarjuna 2-2*

The Blessed Lord said: Arjuna, where has this dangerous state come from? This dejection is unworthy of an Aryan; it shuts the door to heaven and brings disgrace.

### 2.3

क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते ।  
क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप ॥ २-३॥

*klaibyaṃ mā sma gamaḥ pārtha naitattvayyupapadyate  
kṣudraṃ hṛdayadaurbalyaṃ tyaktvottiṣṭha parantapa 2-3*

Do not give in to weakness, Partha. It does not suit you. Shake off this petty faint-heartedness and rise up, Parantapa (scorcher of foes)!

### 2.4

अर्जुन उवाच ।  
कथं भीष्ममहं सङ्ख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन ।  
इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥ २-४॥

*arjuna uvāca  
kathaṃ bhīṣmamahaṃ saṅkhye droṇaṃ ca madhusūdana  
iṣubhiḥ pratiyotsyāmi pūjārhāvarisūdana 2-4*

Arjuna said: Madhusudana, destroyer of enemies, how can I shoot arrows in battle at Bhishma and Drona, men who deserve my worship?

### 2.5

गुरूनहत्वा हि महानुभावान्  
श्रेयो भोक्तुं भैक्ष्यमपीह लोके ।  
हत्वार्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव  
भुञ्जीय भोगान् रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥ २-५॥

*gurūnahatvā hi mahānubhāvān  
śreyo bhoktuṃ bhaikṣyamapīha loke  
hatvārthakāmāṃstu gurūnihaiva  
bhuñjīya bhogān rudhirapradigdhān 2-5*

It would truly be better in this world to live on a beggar's food than to kill these most honorable teachers. If I kill them, then even here on earth every pleasure of wealth and desire I enjoy will be smeared with their blood.

### 2.6

न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो  
यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।  
यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषाम-  
स्तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥ २-६॥

*na caitadvidmaḥ kataranno garīyo  
yadvā jayema yadi vā no jayeyuḥ  
yāneva hatvā na jijīviṣāma-  
ste'vasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ 2-6*

I can hardly tell which outcome would be better: that we defeat them, or that they defeat us. The sons of Dhritarashtra, whom we would not even want to outlive after killing them, are standing right in front of us.

### 2.7

कार्पण्यदोषोपहतस्वभावः  
पृच्छामि त्वां धर्मसम्मूढचेताः ।  
यच्छ्रेयः स्यान्निश्चितं ब्रूहि तन्मे  
शिष्यस्तेऽहं शाधि मां त्वां प्रपन्नम् ॥ २-७॥

*kārpaṇyadoṣopahatasvabhāvaḥ  
pṛcchāmi tvāṃ dharmasammūḍhacetāḥ  
yacchreyaḥ syānniścitaṃ brūhi tanme  
śiṣyaste'haṃ śādhi māṃ tvāṃ prapannam 2-7*

My heart is crushed by the weakness of pity, and my mind is confused about what my duty is. I am asking You: tell me plainly what is good for me. I am Your disciple. I have taken refuge in You; teach me.

### 2.8

न हि प्रपश्यामि ममापनुद्याद्  
यच्छोकमुच्छोषणमिन्द्रियाणाम् ।  
अवाप्य भूमावसपत्नमृद्धं  
राज्यं सुराणामपि चाधिपत्यम् ॥ २-८॥

*na hi prapaśyāmi mamāpanudyād  
yacchokamucchoṣaṇamindriyāṇām  
avāpya bhūmāvasapatnamṛddhaṃ  
rājyaṃ surāṇāmapi cādhipatyam 2-8*

I cannot see anything that would take away this grief that is drying up my senses, even if I won a rich and unrivaled kingdom on earth, or even rulership over the gods.

### 2.9

सञ्जय उवाच ।  
एवमुक्त्वा हृषीकेशं गुडाकेशः परन्तप ।  
न योत्स्य इति गोविन्दमुक्त्वा तूष्णीं बभूव ह ॥ २-९॥

*sañjaya uvāca  
evamuktvā hṛṣīkeśaṃ guḍākeśaḥ parantapa  
na yotsya iti govindamuktvā tūṣṇīṃ babhūva ha 2-9*

Sanjaya said: After saying this to Hrishikesha, Gudakesha, destroyer of enemies, told Govinda, "I will not fight," and fell silent.

### 2.10

तमुवाच हृषीकेशः प्रहसन्निव भारत ।  
सेनयोरुभयोर्मध्ये विषीदन्तमिदं वचः ॥ २-१०॥

*tamuvāca hṛṣīkeśaḥ prahasanniva bhārata  
senayorubhayormadhye viṣīdantamidaṃ vacaḥ 2-10*

O Bharata, to him, despairing there between the two armies, Hrishikesha spoke these words with something like a smile.

### 2.11

श्रीभगवानुवाच ।  
अशोच्यानन्वशोचस्त्वं प्रज्ञावादांश्च भाषसे ।  
गतासूनगतासूंश्च नानुशोचन्ति पण्डिताः ॥ २-११॥

*śrībhagavānuvāca  
aśocyānanvaśocastvaṃ prajñāvādāṃśca bhāṣase  
gatāsūnagatāsūṃśca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ 2-11*

The Blessed Lord said: You are grieving over people who do not deserve grief, and yet you speak as if you were wise. The truly wise grieve neither for the living nor for the dead.

### 2.12

न त्वेवाहं जातु नासं न त्वं नेमे जनाधिपाः ।  
न चैव न भविष्यामः सर्वे वयमतः परम् ॥ २-१२॥

*na tvevāhaṃ jātu nāsaṃ na tvaṃ neme janādhipāḥ  
na caiva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ sarve vayamataḥ param 2-12*

There was never a time in the past when I did not exist, or when you did not, or when these kings did not. Nor will any of us ever cease to exist in the future.

### 2.13

देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा ।  
तथा देहान्तरप्राप्ति र्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥ २-१३॥

*dehino'sminyathā dehe kaumāraṃ yauvanaṃ jarā  
tathā dehāntaraprāptir dhīrastatra na muhyati 2-13*

Just as the embodied soul moves through childhood, youth and old age in this body, it also moves on into another body. A person of steady mind is not troubled by this.

### 2.14

मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः ।  
आगमापायिनोऽनित्या स्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत ॥ २-१४॥

*mātrāsparśāstu kaunteya śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkhadāḥ  
āgamāpāyino'nityā stāṃstitikṣasva bhārata 2-14*

Son of Kunti, the meeting of the senses with their objects produces heat and cold, pleasure and pain. These experiences come and go; they do not last. Bear them with courage, descendant of Bharata.

### 2.15

यं हि न व्यथयन्त्येते पुरुषं पुरुषर्षभ ।  
समदुःखसुखं धीरं सोऽमृतत्वाय कल्पते ॥ २-१५॥

*yaṃ hi na vyathayantyete puruṣaṃ puruṣarṣabha  
samaduḥkhasukhaṃ dhīraṃ so'mṛtatvāya kalpate 2-15*

The steadfast person who is not disturbed by these things, best of men, and who treats pleasure and pain alike, is ready to realize the immortality of the Self.

### 2.16

नासतो विद्यते भावो नाभावो विद्यते सतः ।  
उभयोरपि दृष्टोऽन्त स्त्वनयोस्तत्त्वदर्शिभिः ॥ २-१६॥

*nāsato vidyate bhāvo nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ  
ubhayorapi dṛṣṭo'ntas tvanayostattvadarśibhiḥ 2-16*

What is unreal has no existence, and what is Real never ceases to exist. Those who know the Truth (the seers of the Essence) have seen the truth about both.

### 2.17

अविनाशि तु तद्विद्धि येन सर्वमिदं ततम् ।  
विनाशमव्ययस्यास्य न कश्चित्कर्तुमर्हति ॥ २-१७॥

*avināśi tu tadviddhi yena sarvamidaṃ tatam  
vināśamavyayasyāsya na kaścitkartumarhati 2-17*

Understand that what pervades this whole universe is Indestructible. No one can bring about the destruction of That, the Imperishable.

### 2.18

अन्तवन्त इमे देहा नित्यस्योक्ताः शरीरिणः ।  
अनाशिनोऽप्रमेयस्य तस्माद्युध्यस्व भारत ॥ २-१८॥

*antavanta ime dehā nityasyoktāḥ śarīriṇaḥ  
anāśino'prameyasya tasmādyudhyasva bhārata 2-18*

These bodies that house the embodied Self are said to come to an end. But the Self is eternal, indestructible and beyond comprehension. So fight, Bharata.

### 2.19

य एनं वेत्ति हन्तारं यश्चैनं मन्यते हतम् ।  
उभौ तौ न विजानीतो नायं हन्ति न हन्यते ॥ २-१९॥

*ya enaṃ vetti hantāraṃ yaścainaṃ manyate hatam  
ubhau tau na vijānīto nāyaṃ hanti na hanyate 2-19*

Whoever thinks the Self kills, and whoever thinks It is killed, both lack understanding. The Self neither kills nor is killed.

### 2.20

न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्  
नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।  
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो  
न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ २-२०॥

*na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin  
nāyaṃ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ  
ajo nityaḥ śāśvato'yaṃ purāṇo  
na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre 2-20*

The Self is never born and never dies. Having once existed, It never stops existing. Unborn, eternal, unchanging and ancient, It is not killed when the body is killed.

### 2.21

वेदाविनाशिनं नित्यं य एनमजमव्ययम् ।  
कथं स पुरुषः पार्थ कं घातयति हन्ति कम् ॥ २-२१॥

*vedāvināśinaṃ nityaṃ ya enamajamavyayam  
kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ pārtha kaṃ ghātayati hanti kam 2-21*

Partha, how can anyone who knows the Self to be indestructible, eternal, unborn and inexhaustible kill anyone, or cause anyone to be killed?

### 2.22

वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय  
नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि ।  
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णा-  
न्यन्यानि संयाति नवानि देही ॥ २-२२॥

*vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya  
navāni gṛhṇāti naro'parāṇi  
tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇā-  
nyanyāni saṃyāti navāni dehī 2-22*

Just as a person discards worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, the embodied Self discards worn-out bodies and takes on new ones.

### 2.23

नैनं छिन्दन्ति शस्त्राणि नैनं दहति पावकः ।  
न चैनं क्लेदयन्त्यापो न शोषयति मारुतः ॥ २-२३॥

*nainaṃ chindanti śastrāṇi nainaṃ dahati pāvakaḥ  
na cainaṃ kledayantyāpo na śoṣayati mārutaḥ 2-23*

Weapons cannot cut the Self, fire cannot burn It, water cannot wet It, and wind cannot dry It.

### 2.24

अच्छेद्योऽयमदाह्योऽयमक्लेद्योऽशोष्य एव च ।  
नित्यः सर्वगतः स्थाणुरचलोऽयं सनातनः ॥ २-२४॥

*acchedyo'yamadāhyo'yamakledyo'śoṣya eva ca  
nityaḥ sarvagataḥ sthāṇuracalo'yaṃ sanātanaḥ 2-24*

This Self cannot be cut, burned, wetted or dried. It is eternal, present everywhere, stable, unmoving and ancient.

### 2.25

अव्यक्तोऽयमचिन्त्योऽयमविकार्योऽयमुच्यते ।  
तस्मादेवं विदित्वैनं नानुशोचितुमर्हसि ॥ २-२५॥

*avyakto'yamacintyo'yamavikāryo'yamucyate  
tasmādevaṃ viditvainaṃ nānuśocitumarhasi 2-25*

The Self is described as unmanifest, beyond thought, and unchangeable. Knowing It to be like this, you have no reason to grieve.

### 2.26

अथ चैनं नित्यजातं नित्यं वा मन्यसे मृतम् ।  
तथापि त्वं महाबाहो नैवं शोचितुमर्हसि ॥ २-२६॥

*atha cainaṃ nityajātaṃ nityaṃ vā manyase mṛtam  
tathāpi tvaṃ mahābāho naivaṃ śocitumarhasi 2-26*

And even if you believe the Self is born again and again and dies again and again, mighty-armed one, you still should not grieve.

### 2.27

जातस्य हि ध्रुवो मृत्युर्ध्रुवं जन्म मृतस्य च ।  
तस्मादपरिहार्येऽर्थे न त्वं शोचितुमर्हसि ॥ २-२७॥

*jātasya hi dhruvo mṛtyurdhruvaṃ janma mṛtasya ca  
tasmādaparihārye'rthe na tvaṃ śocitumarhasi 2-27*

Whatever is born is certain to die, and whatever dies is certain to be born again. You should not grieve over what cannot be avoided.

### 2.28

अव्यक्तादीनि भूतानि व्यक्तमध्यानि भारत ।  
अव्यक्तनिधनान्येव तत्र का परिदेवना ॥ २-२८॥

*avyaktādīni bhūtāni vyaktamadhyāni bhārata  
avyaktanidhanānyeva tatra kā paridevanā 2-28*

Bharata, beings are unmanifest before they appear and unmanifest again after they end; only in between do they seem to be manifest. What is there in this to mourn?

### 2.29

आश्चर्यवत्पश्यति कश्चिदेन-  
माश्चर्यवद्वदति तथैव चान्यः ।  
आश्चर्यवच्चैनमन्यः श‍ृणोति  
श्रुत्वाप्येनं वेद न चैव कश्चित् ॥ २-२९॥

*āścaryavatpaśyati kaścidena-  
māścaryavadvadati tathaiva cānyaḥ  
āścaryavaccainamanyaḥ śṛṇoti  
śrutvāpyenaṃ veda na caiva kaścit 2-29*

One person sees the Self as a marvel, another describes It as a marvel, another hears of It as a marvel; and yet, even after hearing, no one understands It at all.

### 2.30

देही नित्यमवध्योऽयं देहे सर्वस्य भारत ।  
तस्मात्सर्वाणि भूतानि न त्वं शोचितुमर्हसि ॥ २-३०॥

*dehī nityamavadhyo'yaṃ dehe sarvasya bhārata  
tasmātsarvāṇi bhūtāni na tvaṃ śocitumarhasi 2-30*

Bharata, the One who dwells in every body can never be destroyed. So you should not grieve for any living creature.

### 2.31

स्वधर्ममपि चावेक्ष्य न विकम्पितुमर्हसि ।  
धर्म्याद्धि युद्धाच्छ्रेयोऽन्यत्क्षत्रियस्य न विद्यते ॥ २-३१॥

*svadharmamapi cāvekṣya na vikampitumarhasi  
dharmyāddhi yuddhācchreyo'nyatkṣatriyasya na vidyate 2-31*

Besides, when you consider your own duty, you should not hesitate, because for a Kshatriya there is nothing greater than a righteous war.

### 2.32

यदृच्छया चोपपन्नं स्वर्गद्वारमपावृतम् ।  
सुखिनः क्षत्रियाः पार्थ लभन्ते युद्धमीदृशम् ॥ २-३२॥

*yadṛcchayā copapannaṃ svargadvāramapāvṛtam  
sukhinaḥ kṣatriyāḥ pārtha labhante yuddhamīdṛśam 2-32*

Partha, fortunate are the Kshatriyas who are called to a battle like this one, which arrives on its own as an open gateway to heaven.

### 2.33

अथ चेत्त्वमिमं धर्म्यं सङ्ग्रामं न करिष्यसि ।  
ततः स्वधर्मं कीर्तिं च हित्वा पापमवाप्स्यसि ॥ २-३३॥

*atha cettvamimaṃ dharmyaṃ saṅgrāmaṃ na kariṣyasi  
tataḥ svadharmaṃ kīrtiṃ ca hitvā pāpamavāpsyasi 2-33*

But if you refuse to fight this righteous war, you will have thrown away both your duty and your honor, and you will fall into sin.

### 2.34

अकीर्तिं चापि भूतानि कथयिष्यन्ति तेऽव्ययाम् ।  
सम्भावितस्य चाकीर्तिर्मरणादतिरिच्यते ॥ २-३४॥

*akīrtiṃ cāpi bhūtāni kathayiṣyanti te'vyayām  
sambhāvitasya cākīrtirmaraṇādatiricyate 2-34*

People will also tell of your lasting disgrace, and for someone who has been honored, disgrace is worse than death.

### 2.35

भयाद्रणादुपरतं मंस्यन्ते त्वां महारथाः ।  
येषां च त्वं बहुमतो भूत्वा यास्यसि लाघवम् ॥ २-३५॥

*bhayādraṇāduparataṃ maṃsyante tvāṃ mahārathāḥ  
yeṣāṃ ca tvaṃ bahumato bhūtvā yāsyasi lāghavam 2-35*

The great commanders will assume you left the battle out of fear, and those who once held you and your heroism in high regard will look down on you.

### 2.36

अवाच्यवादांश्च बहून्वदिष्यन्ति तवाहिताः ।  
निन्दन्तस्तव सामर्थ्यं ततो दुःखतरं नु किम् ॥ २-३६॥

*avācyavādāṃśca bahūnvadiṣyanti tavāhitāḥ  
nindantastava sāmarthyaṃ tato duḥkhataraṃ nu kim 2-36*

Your enemies will say many things that should never be said, mocking your abilities. What could be more painful than that?

### 2.37

हतो वा प्राप्स्यसि स्वर्गं जित्वा वा भोक्ष्यसे महीम् ।  
तस्मादुत्तिष्ठ कौन्तेय युद्धाय कृतनिश्चयः ॥ २-३७॥

*hato vā prāpsyasi svargaṃ jitvā vā bhokṣyase mahīm  
tasmāduttiṣṭha kaunteya yuddhāya kṛtaniścayaḥ 2-37*

If you are killed, you will reach heaven; if you win, you will enjoy the earth. So stand up, son of Kunti, and resolve to fight.

### 2.38

सुखदुःखे समे कृत्वा लाभालाभौ जयाजयौ ।  
ततो युद्धाय युज्यस्व नैवं पापमवाप्स्यसि ॥ २-३८॥

*sukhaduḥkhe same kṛtvā lābhālābhau jayājayau  
tato yuddhāya yujyasva naivaṃ pāpamavāpsyasi 2-38*

Treat pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat as the same, and then engage in battle for the sake of battle. In this way you will not fall into sin.

### 2.39

एषा तेऽभिहिता साङ्ख्ये बुद्धिर्योगे त्विमां श‍ृणु ।  
बुद्ध्या युक्तो यया पार्थ कर्मबन्धं प्रहास्यसि ॥ २-३९॥

*eṣā te'bhihitā sāṅkhye buddhiryoge tvimāṃ śṛṇu  
buddhyā yukto yayā pārtha karmabandhaṃ prahāsyasi 2-39*

What I have taught you so far is the wisdom of Sankhya. Now hear the wisdom of Yoga; once you know it, Partha, you will throw off the bonds of action.

### 2.40

नेहाभिक्रमनाशोऽस्ति प्रत्यवायो न विद्यते ।  
स्वल्पमप्यस्य धर्मस्य त्रायते महतो भयात् ॥ २-४०॥

*nehābhikramanāśo'sti pratyavāyo na vidyate  
svalpamapyasya dharmasya trāyate mahato bhayāt 2-40*

On this path no effort is wasted, and no harm (no contrary result) comes of it. Even a little of this knowledge, even a little practice of this Yoga, protects a person from the great fear.

### 2.41

व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिरेकेह कुरुनन्दन ।  
बहुशाखा ह्यनन्ताश्च बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम् ॥ २-४१॥

*vyavasāyātmikā buddhirekeha kurunandana  
bahuśākhā hyanantāśca buddhayo'vyavasāyinām 2-41*

Here, Kurunandana, joy of the Kurus, the resolve is single-pointed, while the thoughts of the irresolute branch out endlessly in every direction.

### 2.42

यामिमां पुष्पितां वाचं प्रवदन्त्यविपश्चितः ।  
वेदवादरताः पार्थ नान्यदस्तीति वादिनः ॥ २-४२॥

*yāmimāṃ puṣpitāṃ vācaṃ pravadantyavipaścitaḥ  
vedavādaratāḥ pārtha nānyadastīti vādinaḥ 2-42*

Partha, the unwise, who delight in the flattering words of the Vedas, make flowery speeches and declare that there is nothing beyond them.

### 2.43

कामात्मानः स्वर्गपरा जन्मकर्मफलप्रदाम् ।  
क्रियाविशेषबहुलां भोगैश्वर्यगतिं प्रति ॥ २-४३॥

*kāmātmānaḥ svargaparā janmakarmaphalapradām  
kriyāviśeṣabahulāṃ bhogaiśvaryagatiṃ prati 2-43*

Driven by desire and aiming at heaven, they speak these flowery words, which promise rebirth as the reward of action and lay down many specific rituals for gaining pleasure and power.

### 2.44

भोगैश्वर्यप्रसक्तानां तयापहृतचेतसाम् ।  
व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिः समाधौ न विधीयते ॥ २-४४॥

*bhogaiśvaryaprasaktānāṃ tayāpahṛtacetasām  
vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ samādhau na vidhīyate 2-44*

Those who cling to pleasure and power, and whose minds are carried away by such teaching, have no firm, single-pointed resolve, and they are not fit for steady meditation and samadhi.

### 2.45

त्रैगुण्यविषया वेदा निस्त्रैगुण्यो भवार्जुन ।  
निर्द्वन्द्वो नित्यसत्त्वस्थो निर्योगक्षेम आत्मवान् ॥ २-४५॥

*traiguṇyaviṣayā vedā nistraiguṇyo bhavārjuna  
nirdvandvo nityasattvastho niryogakṣema ātmavān 2-45*

The Vedas deal with the three qualities (gunas). Arjuna, rise above these three qualities, free yourself from the pairs of opposites, remain always in sattwa (goodness), let go of all concern with acquiring and holding on to things, and be established in the Self.

### 2.46

यावानर्थ उदपाने सर्वतः सम्प्लुतोदके ।  
तावान्सर्वेषु वेदेषु ब्राह्मणस्य विजानतः ॥ २-४६॥

*yāvānartha udapāne sarvataḥ samplutodake  
tāvānsarveṣu vedeṣu brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ 2-46*

To the Brahmana who has known the Self, all the Vedas are of as much use as a water tank in a place that is flooded on every side.

### 2.47

कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।  
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ २-४७॥

*karmaṇyevādhikāraste mā phaleṣu kadācana  
mā karmaphalaheturbhūrmā te saṅgo'stvakarmaṇi 2-47*

You have a right only to your work, never to its results. Do not let the fruit of action be your motive, and do not let yourself become attached to inaction either.

### 2.48

योगस्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा धनञ्जय ।  
सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योः समो भूत्वा समत्वं योग उच्यते ॥ २-४८॥

*yogasthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā dhanañjaya  
siddhyasiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā samatvaṃ yoga ucyate 2-48*

Dhananjaya, act while giving up attachment, staying firm in Yoga, and remaining balanced in success and failure. This evenness of mind is what is called Yoga.

### 2.49

दूरेण ह्यवरं कर्म बुद्धियोगाद्धनञ्जय ।  
बुद्धौ शरणमन्विच्छ कृपणाः फलहेतवः ॥ २-४९॥

*dūreṇa hyavaraṃ karma buddhiyogāddhanañjaya  
buddhau śaraṇamanviccha kṛpaṇāḥ phalahetavaḥ 2-49*

Dhananjaya, action is far inferior to the Yoga of wisdom. Take refuge in wisdom. Pitiable are those whose motive is the reward.

### 2.50

बुद्धियुक्तो जहातीह उभे सुकृतदुष्कृते ।  
तस्माद्योगाय युज्यस्व योगः कर्मसु कौशलम् ॥ २-५०॥

*buddhiyukto jahātīha ubhe sukṛtaduṣkṛte  
tasmādyogāya yujyasva yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam 2-50*

Someone who has the wisdom of evenness of mind casts off both good and bad deeds in this very life. So devote yourself to Yoga. Skill in action is Yoga.

### 2.51

कर्मजं बुद्धियुक्ता हि फलं त्यक्त्वा मनीषिणः ।  
जन्मबन्धविनिर्मुक्ताः पदं गच्छन्त्यनामयम् ॥ २-५१॥

*karmajaṃ buddhiyuktā hi phalaṃ tyaktvā manīṣiṇaḥ  
janmabandhavinirmuktāḥ padaṃ gacchantyanāmayam 2-51*

The wise, possessing knowledge and having given up the fruits of their actions, are freed from the chains of birth and reach the State beyond all evil.

### 2.52

यदा ते मोहकलिलं बुद्धिर्व्यतितरिष्यति ।  
तदा गन्तासि निर्वेदं श्रोतव्यस्य श्रुतस्य च ॥ २-५२॥

*yadā te mohakalilaṃ buddhirvyatitariṣyati  
tadā gantāsi nirvedaṃ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca 2-52*

When your understanding has passed beyond the swamp of delusion, you will become indifferent to everything you have heard and everything you have yet to hear.

### 2.53

श्रुतिविप्रतिपन्ना ते यदा स्थास्यति निश्चला ।  
समाधावचला बुद्धिस्तदा योगमवाप्स्यसि ॥ २-५३॥

*śrutivipratipannā te yadā sthāsyati niścalā  
samādhāvacalā buddhistadā yogamavāpsyasi 2-53*

When your understanding, no longer unsettled by what you have heard, stands firm and steady in the Self, you will attain realization of the Self.

### 2.54

अर्जुन उवाच ।  
स्थितप्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधिस्थस्य केशव ।  
स्थितधीः किं प्रभाषेत किमासीत व्रजेत किम् ॥ २-५४॥

*arjuna uvāca  
sthitaprajñasya kā bhāṣā samādhisthasya keśava  
sthitadhīḥ kiṃ prabhāṣeta kimāsīta vrajeta kim 2-54*

Arjuna said: Keshava, how would you describe someone whose wisdom is steady and who is absorbed in the superconscious state? How does a person of steady wisdom speak, how does he sit, how does he walk?

### 2.55

श्रीभगवानुवाच ।  
प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् ।  
आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥ २-५५॥

*śrībhagavānuvāca  
prajahāti yadā kāmānsarvānpārtha manogatān  
ātmanyevātmanā tuṣṭaḥ sthitaprajñastadocyate 2-55*

The Blessed Lord said: Partha, when a person completely gives up all the desires of the mind and is content in the Self through the Self alone, he is said to be one of steady wisdom.

### 2.56

दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः ।  
वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥ २-५६॥

*duḥkheṣvanudvignamanāḥ sukheṣu vigataspṛhaḥ  
vītarāgabhayakrodhaḥ sthitadhīrmunirucyate 2-56*

A person whose mind is not shaken by misfortune, who does not crave pleasures when things go well, and who is free of attachment, fear and anger, is called a sage of steady wisdom.

### 2.57

यः सर्वत्रानभिस्नेहस्तत्तत्प्राप्य शुभाशुभम् ।  
नाभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ २-५७॥

*yaḥ sarvatrānabhisnehastattatprāpya śubhāśubham  
nābhinandati na dveṣṭi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā 2-57*

One who is without attachment in every situation, who neither rejoices at what is good nor hates what is bad when it comes, has firmly established wisdom.

### 2.58

यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः ।  
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ २-५८॥

*yadā saṃharate cāyaṃ kūrmo'ṅgānīva sarvaśaḥ  
indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyastasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā 2-58*

When a person draws his senses back from their objects, just as a tortoise pulls its limbs in from every side, his wisdom becomes steady.

### 2.59

विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः ।  
रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥ २-५९॥

*viṣayā vinivartante nirāhārasya dehinaḥ  
rasavarjaṃ raso'pyasya paraṃ dṛṣṭvā nivartate 2-59*

For someone who abstains, the objects of the senses fall away, but the longing for them stays behind. Even that longing disappears once he has seen the Supreme.

### 2.60

यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः ।  
इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥ २-६०॥

*yatato hyapi kaunteya puruṣasya vipaścitaḥ  
indriyāṇi pramāthīni haranti prasabhaṃ manaḥ 2-60*

Son of Kunti, the restless senses forcibly drag away the mind even of a wise person who is striving to control them.

### 2.61

तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्त आसीत मत्परः ।  
वशे हि यस्येन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ २-६१॥

*tāni sarvāṇi saṃyamya yukta āsīta matparaḥ  
vaśe hi yasyendriyāṇi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā 2-61*

Once he has brought all the senses under control, he should sit firmly, with his mind fixed on Me. The person whose senses are mastered has wisdom that does not waver.

### 2.62

ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते ।  
सङ्गात्सञ्जायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥ २-६२॥

*dhyāyato viṣayānpuṃsaḥ saṅgasteṣūpajāyate  
saṅgātsañjāyate kāmaḥ kāmātkrodho'bhijāyate 2-62*

When someone dwells on sense objects, attachment to them grows. Attachment gives rise to desire, and desire gives rise to anger.

### 2.63

क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः ।  
स्मृतिभ्रंशाद् बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥ २-६३॥

*krodhādbhavati sammohaḥ sammohātsmṛtivibhramaḥ  
smṛtibhraṃśād buddhināśo buddhināśātpraṇaśyati 2-63*

Anger leads to delusion; delusion leads to loss of memory; loss of memory destroys the power of discrimination; and when discrimination is destroyed, the person is ruined.

### 2.64

रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् ।  
आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥ २-६४॥

*rāgadveṣaviyuktaistu viṣayānindriyaiścaran .  
ātmavaśyairvidheyātmā prasādamadhigacchati 2-64*

But the self-controlled person who moves among sense objects with the senses held in check, free of both liking and disliking, reaches peace.

### 2.65

प्रसादे सर्वदुःखानां हानिरस्योपजायते ।  
प्रसन्नचेतसो ह्याशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते ॥ २-६५॥

*prasāde sarvaduḥkhānāṃ hānirasyopajāyate  
prasannacetaso hyāśu buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate 2-65*

In that peace every sorrow comes to an end, because the understanding of a calm-minded person quickly becomes firm.

### 2.66

नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना ।  
न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम् ॥ २-६६॥

*nāsti buddhirayuktasya na cāyuktasya bhāvanā  
na cābhāvayataḥ śāntiraśāntasya kutaḥ sukham 2-66*

Someone whose mind is unsteady has no knowledge of the Self, and no meditation. Without meditation there is no peace, and without peace, how could there be happiness?

### 2.67

इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते ।  
तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥ २-६७॥

*indriyāṇāṃ hi caratāṃ yanmano'nuvidhīyate  
tadasya harati prajñāṃ vāyurnāvamivāmbhasi 2-67*

For when the mind runs after the roaming senses, it sweeps away a person's discrimination, just as the wind sweeps a boat across the water.

### 2.68

तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः ।  
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ २-६८॥

*tasmādyasya mahābāho nigṛhītāni sarvaśaḥ  
indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyastasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā 2-68*

Therefore, O mighty-armed one, the person whose senses are fully withdrawn from sense objects has knowledge that is firm.

### 2.69

या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी ।  
यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥ २-६९॥

*yā niśā sarvabhūtānāṃ tasyāṃ jāgarti saṃyamī  
yasyāṃ jāgrati bhūtāni sā niśā paśyato muneḥ 2-69*

What is night for all other beings is the time when the self-controlled person is awake; and the time when all beings are awake is night for the sage (muni) who truly sees.

### 2.70

आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं  
समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् ।  
तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे  
स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥ २-७०॥

*āpūryamāṇamacalapratiṣṭhaṃ  
samudramāpaḥ praviśanti yadvat  
tadvatkāmā yaṃ praviśanti sarve  
sa śāntimāpnoti na kāmakāmī 2-70*

The person into whom all desires flow, as rivers flow into the ocean that is filled from every side yet stays unmoved, reaches peace. The one who chases desires does not.

### 2.71

विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः ।  
निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥ २-७१॥

*vihāya kāmānyaḥ sarvānpumāṃścarati niḥspṛhaḥ  
nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sa śāntimadhigacchati 2-71*

The person who gives up all desires and lives without craving, without any sense of 'I' or 'mine', reaches peace.

### 2.72

एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति ।  
स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥ २-७२॥   
  
ॐ तत्सदिति श्रीमद्भगवद्गीतासूपनिषत्सु  
ब्रह्मविद्यायां योगशास्त्रे श्रीकृष्णार्जुनसंवादे  
साङ्ख्ययोगो नाम द्वितीयोऽध्यायः ॥ २॥

*eṣā brāhmī sthitiḥ pārtha naināṃ prāpya vimuhyati   
sthitvāsyāmantakāle'pi brahmanirvāṇamṛcchati 2-72  
  
oṃ tatsaditi śrīmadbhagavadgītāsūpaniṣatsu  
brahmavidyāyāṃ yogaśāstre śrīkṛṣṇārjunasaṃvāde  
sāṅkhyayogo nāma dvitīyo'dhyāyaḥ 2*

This is the state of Brahman, O son of Pritha. Whoever reaches it is no longer deluded. Established in it, even at the moment of death, a person attains oneness with Brahman.

So ends the second discourse, called "The Yoga of Knowledge," in the Upanishads of the glorious Bhagavad Gita, the science of the Eternal, the scripture of Yoga, the dialogue between Sri Krishna and Arjuna.
