# Brahma Sutras

### 1.1.1

<vt>Deliberation on Brahman</vt>  
श्री ब्रह्मसूत्राणि॥ ॥ अथ प्रथमोऽध्यायः॥ ॐ अथातो ब्रह्मजिज्ञासा ॐ ॥ १.१.१॥

*<vt>Deliberation on Brahman</vt>  
śrī brahmasūtrāṇi.. .. atha prathamo'dhyāyaḥ.. oṃ athāto brahmajijñāsā oṃ .. 1.1.1..*

From here on, therefore, Brahman is to be deliberated on.

And so, now, the inquiry into Brahman begins.

### 1.1.2

<vt>Origin etc., of the Universe</vt>  
ॐ जन्माद्यस्य यतः ॐ ॥ १.१.२॥

*<vt>Origin etc., of the Universe</vt>  
oṃ janmādyasya yataḥ oṃ .. 1.1.2..*

Brahman is that from which the birth and so on of this universe come.

Brahman is the source of the origin, sustenance and dissolution of this world.

### 1.1.3

<vt>Scripture as Source of Knowledge of Brahman</vt>  
ॐ शास्त्रयोनित्वात् ॐ ॥ १.१.३॥

*<vt>Scripture as Source of Knowledge of Brahman</vt>  
oṃ śāstrayonitvāt oṃ .. 1.1.3..*

Brahman is omniscient, being the source of the scriptures. (Or) Brahman is known from no other source, since the scriptures are the valid means of knowing It.

For scripture is the source of right knowledge.

### 1.1.4

<vt>Upanishads Reveal Brahman</vt>  
ॐ तत्तुसमन्वयात् ॐ ॥ १.१.४॥

*<vt>Upanishads Reveal Brahman</vt>  
oṃ tattusamanvayāt oṃ .. 1.1.4..*

But Brahman is known from the Upanishads, since It is what they fully intend to convey.

But Brahman is established as knowable only from the Scriptures and by no independent means, because It is the main purpose of all Vedantic texts.

### 1.1.5

<vt>The first Cause Possessed of Consciousness</vt>  
ॐ ईक्षतेर्नाशब्दम् ॐ ॥ १.१.५॥

*<vt>The first Cause Possessed of Consciousness</vt>  
oṃ īkṣaternāśabdam oṃ .. 1.1.5..*

The Pradhana of the Samkhyas is not the cause of the universe. The Upanishads do not mention it, as is clear from the fact of seeing (or thinking).

The Upanishads ascribe seeing (thinking) to the First Cause, so the Pradhana is not the first cause they indicate; it has no basis in scripture.

### 1.1.6

ॐ गौणश्चेन्नात्मशब्दात् ॐ ॥ १.१.६॥

*oṃ gauṇaścennātmaśabdāt oṃ .. 1.1.6..*

Suppose the seeing is meant in a secondary sense. Not so, because the word Self is used.

The word 'seeing' or thinking is not used in a secondary sense, since the word Atman is applied to the cause of the world.

### 1.1.7

ॐ तन्निष्ठस्य मोक्षोपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.१.७॥

*oṃ tanniṣṭhasya mokṣopadeśāt oṃ .. 1.1.7..*

Pradhana is not what the word "Self" means, since liberation is promised to one who holds fast to That.

The term Self cannot designate the Pradhana, since Salvation is declared for one devoted to that Sat.

### 1.1.8

ॐ हेयत्वावचनाच्च ॐ ॥ १.१.८॥

*oṃ heyatvāvacanācca oṃ .. 1.1.8..*

Pradhana is not spoken of even indirectly, because nothing later mentions rejecting it, and because that would conflict with the opening assertion.

The word 'Self' cannot denote the Pradhana, also because the scriptures never say that It (Sat) must be discarded.

### 1.1.9

ॐ स्वाप्ययात् ॐ ॥ १.१.९॥

*oṃ svāpyayāt oṃ .. 1.1.9..*

Because the individual merges into his own Self.

Since the individual merges in its own Self, the Self cannot be the Pradhana.

### 1.1.10

ॐ गतिसामान्यात् ॐ ॥ १.१.१०॥

*oṃ gatisāmānyāt oṃ .. 1.1.10..*

Because the knowledge drawn from the various Upanishads is uniform in holding Consciousness to be the cause.

The Vedanta texts agree in their view, so Brahman must be taken as that cause.

### 1.1.11

ॐ श्रुतत्वाच्च ॐ ॥ १.१.११॥

*oṃ śrutatvācca oṃ .. 1.1.11..*

And because the Upanishads reveal Brahman as such.

And because the Sruti states it directly, the all-knowing Brahman alone is the cause of the universe.

### 1.1.12

<vt>The Blissful One</vt>  
ॐ आनन्दमयोऽभ्यासात् ॐ ॥ १.१.१२॥

*<vt>The Blissful One</vt>  
oṃ ānandamayo'bhyāsāt oṃ .. 1.1.12..*

Repetition shows that the Blissful One is the supreme Self.

Anandamaya means Para Brahman, because the word 'bliss' is repeated to denote the Highest Self.

### 1.1.13

ॐ विकारशब्दान्नेतिचेन्न प्राचुर्यात् ॐ ॥ १.१.१३॥

*oṃ vikāraśabdānneticenna prācuryāt oṃ .. 1.1.13..*

Objection: the Blissful One is not Brahman, because the suffix used denotes modification. No: the suffix here expresses abundance.

Objection: Anandamaya, consisting of bliss, cannot denote the supreme Self, since it is a word for a modification, transformation or product. The objection fails, because the suffix 'maya' denotes abundance.

### 1.1.14

ॐ तद्धेतुव्यपदेशाच्च ॐ ॥ १.१.१४॥

*oṃ taddhetuvyapadeśācca oṃ .. 1.1.14..*

Also because Brahman is pointed out as the source of Bliss.

And because he is declared to be the cause of bliss, 'maya' denotes abundance or fulness.

### 1.1.15

ॐ मान्त्रवर्णिकमेव च गीयते ॐ ॥ १.१.१५॥

*oṃ māntravarṇikameva ca gīyate oṃ .. 1.1.15..*

And the same Brahman spoken of in the mantra is declared in the Brahmana portion that explains the mantra.

Further, the very Brahman referred to in the Mantra portion is proclaimed in the Brahmana passage as the Anandamaya.

### 1.1.16

ॐ नेतरोऽनुपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ १.१.१६॥

*oṃ netaro'nupapatteḥ oṃ .. 1.1.16..*

The other is not the supreme Self, since that would be illogical.

Brahman is meant here, not the other (the individual soul), because the latter assumption is impossible.

### 1.1.17

ॐ भेदव्यपदेशाच्च ॐ ॥ १.१.१७॥

*oṃ bhedavyapadeśācca oṃ .. 1.1.17..*

And because the difference between the individual soul and the supreme Self is asserted.

And since a difference is declared between the individual soul and the one named in 'The Self consisting of bliss' and similar passages, the individual soul cannot be the one meant.

### 1.1.18

ॐ कामाच्च नानुमानापेक्षा ॐ ॥ १.१.१८॥

*oṃ kāmācca nānumānāpekṣā oṃ .. 1.1.18..*

Inference cannot be relied on to arrive at Pradhana, given the mention of desire.

Because the scriptural passage speaks of wishing or willing, we cannot claim, even by inference, that Anandamaya means Pradhana.

### 1.1.19

ॐ अस्मिन्नस्य च तद्योगं शास्ति ॐ ॥ १.१.१९॥

*oṃ asminnasya ca tadyogaṃ śāsti oṃ .. 1.1.19..*

Moreover, scripture teaches the absolute identity of this one with this One.

Scripture also teaches that when knowledge is attained, this (the individual soul) is joined with that which consists of bliss (Anandamaya).

### 1.1.20

<vt>The Being Inside</vt>  
ॐ अन्तस्तद्धर्मोपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.१.२०॥

*<vt>The Being Inside</vt>  
oṃ antastaddharmopadeśāt oṃ .. 1.1.20..*

The Being inside is God, since His qualities are taught.

The being within the Sun and the eye is Brahman, because His attributes are taught there.

### 1.1.21

ॐ भेदव्यपदेशाच्चान्यः ॐ ॥ १.१.२१॥

*oṃ bhedavyapadeśāccānyaḥ oṃ .. 1.1.21..*

And God is different from the individual being, as difference is mentioned.

There is also another one, the Lord, distinct from the individual souls animating the Sun and so on, since a distinction is declared.

### 1.1.22

<vt>Space</vt>  
ॐ आकाशस्तल्लिङ्गात् ॐ ॥ १.१.२२॥

*<vt>Space</vt>  
oṃ ākāśastalliṅgāt oṃ .. 1.1.22..*

Space (Akasa) is Brahman, since Brahman's identifying mark is evident.

The word Akasa (ether) here means Brahman, because Brahman's characteristic marks are mentioned.

### 1.1.23

<vt>Prana</vt>  
ॐ अत एव प्राणः ॐ ॥ १.१.२३॥

*<vt>Prana</vt>  
oṃ ata eva prāṇaḥ oṃ .. 1.1.23..*

On that same ground, Prana is Brahman.

The breath, too, refers to Brahman for the same reason.

### 1.1.24

<vt>Light</vt>  
ॐ ज्योतिश्चरणाभिधानात् ॐ ॥ १.१.२४॥

*<vt>Light</vt>  
oṃ jyotiścaraṇābhidhānāt oṃ .. 1.1.24..*

Light is Brahman, because feet are mentioned.

The 'light' is Brahman, because feet are mentioned in a passage connected with the one about the light.

### 1.1.25

ॐ छन्दोऽभिधानान्नेतिचेन्न तथाचेतोऽर्पणनिगदात्तथा हि दर्शनम् ॐ ॥ १.१.२५॥

*oṃ chando'bhidhānānneticenna tathāceto'rpaṇanigadāttathā hi darśanam oṃ .. 1.1.25..*

Objection: Brahman is not spoken of, since the mention concerns a metre. No: this is how dedication of the mind is taught, and similar instances occur elsewhere.

Objection: the metre Gayatri is denoted, so Brahman is not. Reply: not so, because the metre is the means by which applying the mind to Brahman is declared, as other passages also show.

### 1.1.26

ॐ भूतादिपादव्यपदेशोपपत्तेश्चैवम् ॐ ॥ १.१.२६॥

*oṃ bhūtādipādavyapadeśopapatteścaivam oṃ .. 1.1.26..*

And this must be so, since only this allows all things and so on to be represented as a foot.

We must also conclude that Brahman is the topic of the previous passage, where Gayatri occurs, because only then is it possible to declare that the beings and so on are the feet.

### 1.1.27

ॐ उपदेशभेदान्नेति चेन्नोभयस्मिन्नप्यविरोधात् ॐ ॥ १.१.२७॥

*oṃ upadeśabhedānneti cennobhayasminnapyavirodhāt oṃ .. 1.1.27..*

Objection: the Brahman of the earlier text is not referred to here, since the instruction differs. No: in either case there is no contradiction.

Objection: the Brahman of the Gayatri passage cannot be recognised in the passage on 'light,' since the designation or specification differs. Reply: no, because neither designation stands in the way of that recognition.

### 1.1.28

<vt>Pratardana</vt>  
ॐ प्राणस्तथानुगमात् ॐ ॥ १.१.२८॥

*<vt>Pratardana</vt>  
oṃ prāṇastathānugamāt oṃ .. 1.1.28..*

Prana is Brahman, since it is so understood.

Prana is Brahman, as a connected reading of the passage referring to Prana shows.

### 1.1.29

ॐ नवक्तुरात्मोपदेशादिति चेदध्यात्मसंबन्धभूमा ह्यस्मिन् ॐ ॥ १.१.२९॥

*oṃ navakturātmopadeśāditi cedadhyātmasaṃbandhabhūmā hyasmin oṃ .. 1.1.29..*

Objection: Prana is not Brahman, since the instruction concerns the speaker's own self. No: references to the inmost Self abound here.

Objection: Brahman is not meant in these passages, since the speaker is instructing about himself. Reply: not so, because this chapter or Upanishad abounds in references to the Inner Self.

### 1.1.30

ॐ शास्त्रदृष्ट्या तूपदेशो वामदेववत् ॐ ॥ १.१.३०॥

*oṃ śāstradṛṣṭyā tūpadeśo vāmadevavat oṃ .. 1.1.30..*

But the instruction comes from a seer's vision that agrees with scripture, as with Vamadeva.

Indra's declaration about himself, that he is one with Brahman, is possible through intuition as attested by Sruti, as in the case of Vamadeva.

### 1.1.31

ॐ जीवमुख्यप्राणलिङ्गान्नेति चेन्नोपासत्रैविद्यादाश्रितत्वादिह तद्योगात् ॐ ॥ १.१.३१॥ प्रथमाध्यायस्य प्रथमः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ jīvamukhyaprāṇaliṅgānneti cennopāsatraividyādāśritatvādiha tadyogāt oṃ .. 1.1.31.. prathamādhyāyasya prathamaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

Objection: Brahman is not spoken of here, given the signs of the individual soul and the chief vital force. That cannot be, since it would lead to a threefold meditation. Besides, Prana is accepted elsewhere as meaning Brahman because Brahman's characteristics are present, and these are evident here.

Objection: Brahman is not meant, since characteristic marks of the individual soul and the chief vital air are mentioned. Reply: no, because that reading would enjoin a threefold meditation (Upasana), because Prana has been accepted elsewhere in the Sruti in the sense of Brahman, and because words denoting Brahman are used here too in reference to Prana.

### 1.2.1

<vt>The Entity Known Everywhere</vt>  
ॐ सर्वत्र प्रसिद्धोपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.२.१॥

*<vt>The Entity Known Everywhere</vt>  
oṃ sarvatra prasiddhopadeśāt oṃ .. 1.2.1..*

Brahman is the object to be meditated on, since what is well known everywhere is taught here (Chandogya Upanishad III-xiv-1,2).

That which consists of the mind ('Manomaya') is Brahman, because this text teaches the Brahman well known in the Upanishads as the cause of the world.

### 1.2.2

ॐ विवक्षितगुणोपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ १.२.२॥

*oṃ vivakṣitaguṇopapatteśca oṃ .. 1.2.2..*

And this follows because the intended qualities are justifiable in Brahman.

The qualities meant to be expressed are also possible in Brahman, so the passage refers to Brahman.

### 1.2.3

ॐ अनुपपत्तेस्तु न शारीरः ॐ ॥ १.२.३॥

*oṃ anupapattestu na śārīraḥ oṃ .. 1.2.3..*

And the embodied individual soul is surely not meant, since the qualities do not fit it.

Those qualities being impossible in the embodied soul, Manomaya and the like do not denote it.

### 1.2.4

ॐ कर्मकर्तृव्यपदेशाच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.४॥

*oṃ karmakartṛvyapadeśācca oṃ .. 1.2.4..*

And because object and subject are referred to.

The attainer and the attained are declared separately, so He who consists of the mind (Manomaya) refers to Brahman and not to the individual soul.

### 1.2.5

ॐ शब्दविशेषात् ॐ ॥ १.२.५॥

*oṃ śabdaviśeṣāt oṃ .. 1.2.5..*

Because of the difference in the case-endings of the two words.

Because the words differ.

### 1.2.6

ॐ स्मृतेश्च ॐ ॥ १.२.६॥

*oṃ smṛteśca oṃ .. 1.2.6..*

This follows from the Smriti also.

The Smriti, too, tells us that the embodied self or individual soul differs from the one referred to in the text under discussion.

### 1.2.7

ॐ अर्भकौकस्त्वात् तद्व्यपदेशाच्च नेति चेन्न निचाय्यत्वादेवं व्योमवच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.७॥

*oṃ arbhakaukastvāt tadvyapadeśācca neti cenna nicāyyatvādevaṃ vyomavacca oṃ .. 1.2.7..*

Objection: the supreme Self is not taught here, given the smallness of the abode and its being so described. No: this is for the sake of contemplation, as seen in the case of space.

Objection: the passage does not refer to Brahman, since the abode (the heart) is small and minuteness is denoted. Reply: no, because Brahman is to be meditated on in this way, and the case resembles that of ether.

### 1.2.8

ॐ संभोगप्राप्तिरिति चेन्न वैशेष्यात् ॐ ॥ १.२.८॥

*oṃ saṃbhogaprāptiriti cenna vaiśeṣyāt oṃ .. 1.2.8..*

Objection: God would then undergo the experience of happiness and sorrow, as a result of unity. Not so, for there is a difference.

Objection: Brahman, being omnipresent and so connected with the hearts of all individual souls, would also experience pleasure and pain. Reply: not so, because the two differ in nature.

### 1.2.9

<vt>The Eater</vt>  
ॐ अत्ता चराचरग्रहणात् ॐ ॥ १.२.९॥

*<vt>The Eater</vt>  
oṃ attā carācaragrahaṇāt oṃ .. 1.2.9..*

The eater is God, since all that moves and does not move is taken as food.

The Eater is Brahman, because the movable and the immovable, the whole world, are taken as His food.

### 1.2.10

ॐ प्रकरणाच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.१०॥

*oṃ prakaraṇācca oṃ .. 1.2.10..*

This also follows from the context.

The context, too, shows that the eater is Brahman.

### 1.2.11

<vt>The Two in the Cavity of the Heart</vt>  
ॐ गुहां प्रविष्टावात्मानौ हि तद्दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ १.२.११॥

*<vt>The Two in the Cavity of the Heart</vt>  
oṃ guhāṃ praviṣṭāvātmānau hi taddarśanāt oṃ .. 1.2.11..*

The two who have entered the cavity of the heart are the individual Self and the supreme Self, as other texts show.

The two who have entered the cavity of the heart are indeed the individual soul and the Supreme Soul, for that is what is seen.

### 1.2.12

ॐ विशेषणाच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.१२॥

*oṃ viśeṣaṇācca oṃ .. 1.2.12..*

And because a specification is made.

And because of the distinctive qualities of the two, given in later texts.

### 1.2.13

<vt>The Person in the Eye</vt>  
ॐ अन्तर उपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ १.२.१३॥

*<vt>The Person in the Eye</vt>  
oṃ antara upapatteḥ oṃ .. 1.2.13..*

The One inside is God, for that is logical.

The person within the eye is Brahman, because the attributes mentioned there suit only Brahman.

### 1.2.14

ॐ स्थानादिव्यपदेशाच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.१४॥

*oṃ sthānādivyapadeśācca oṃ .. 1.2.14..*

This also follows from the mention of place and so on.

And because place and so on are stated.

### 1.2.15

ॐ सुखविशिष्टाभिधानादेव च ॐ ॥ १.२.१५॥

*oṃ sukhaviśiṣṭābhidhānādeva ca oṃ .. 1.2.15..*

And also because the One possessed of bliss is referred to (in the Text, "The One that").

And because the passage refers to that which is marked by bliss (Brahman).

### 1.2.16

ॐ श्रुतोपनिषत्कगत्यभिधानाच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.१६॥

*oṃ śrutopaniṣatkagatyabhidhānācca oṃ .. 1.2.16..*

And because the path for one who has heard the secret teaching is described.

And because the way of one who has known the Truth of the Upanishads is stated.

### 1.2.17

ॐ अनवस्थितेरसंभवाच्च नेतरः ॐ ॥ १.२.१७॥

*oṃ anavasthiterasaṃbhavācca netaraḥ oṃ .. 1.2.17..*

No other can be the Person in the eye, because of impermanence and impossibility.

The person within the eye is the Supreme Self and no other, such as the individual soul. These others do not exist always, and the qualities of the person in the eye cannot be ascribed to any of them.

### 1.2.18

<vt>The Internal Ruler</vt>  
ॐ अन्तर्याम्यधिदैवादिषु तद्धर्मव्यपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.२.१८॥

*<vt>The Internal Ruler</vt>  
oṃ antaryāmyadhidaivādiṣu taddharmavyapadeśāt oṃ .. 1.2.18..*

The internal Ruler in the divine and other contexts is the supreme Self, since that Self's characteristics are described.

The internal ruler over the gods and so on is Brahman, because Brahman's attributes are mentioned.

### 1.2.19

ॐ न च स्मार्तं अतद्धर्माभिपालात् ॐ ॥ १.२.१९॥

*oṃ na ca smārtaṃ ataddharmābhipālāt oṃ .. 1.2.19..*

Nor is the internal Ruler the Pradhana known from the Samkhya Smriti, for qualities foreign to Pradhana are described.

The Internal Ruler is not what the Sankhya Smriti teaches (Pradhana), because qualities contrary to its nature are mentioned here.

### 1.2.20

ॐ शरीरश्चोभयेऽपि हि भेदेनैनमधीयते ॐ ॥ १.२.२०॥

*oṃ śarīraścobhaye'pi hi bhedenainamadhīyate oṃ .. 1.2.20..*

Nor is the embodied soul the internal Ruler, for followers of both recensions read of it as different.

The individual soul is not the Internal Ruler either, for both recensions, the Kanva and Madhyandina Sakhas of the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, speak of it as different from the Internal Ruler.

### 1.2.21

<vt>The One That is Unseen etc.</vt>  
ॐ अदृश्यत्वादिगुणको धर्मोक्तेः ॐ ॥ १.२.२१॥

*<vt>The One That is Unseen etc.</vt>  
oṃ adṛśyatvādiguṇako dharmokteḥ oṃ .. 1.2.21..*

The entity having such qualities as not being seen is Brahman, for Its characteristics are described.

The possessor of qualities such as indivisibility is Brahman, since Its attributes are declared.

### 1.2.22

ॐ विशेषणभेदव्यपदेशाभ्यां नेतरौ ॐ ॥ १.२.२२॥

*oṃ viśeṣaṇabhedavyapadeśābhyāṃ netarau oṃ .. 1.2.22..*

The other two (the individual soul and Pradhana) are not meant, for Brahman's distinctive characteristics and Its difference from the two are mentioned.

The other two (the individual soul and the Pradhana) are not the source of all beings, since distinctive attributes and differences are stated.

### 1.2.23

ॐ रूपोपन्यासाच्च ॐ ॥ १.२.२३॥

*oṃ rūpopanyāsācca oṃ .. 1.2.23..*

And because a form is presented.

And since its form is mentioned, the passage under discussion refers to Brahman.

### 1.2.24

<vt>Vaisvanara</vt>  
ॐ वैश्वानरः साधारणशब्दविशेषात् ॐ ॥ १.२.२४॥

*<vt>Vaisvanara</vt>  
oṃ vaiśvānaraḥ sādhāraṇaśabdaviśeṣāt oṃ .. 1.2.24..*

Vaisvanara (the Cosmic Person) is the supreme Lord, for though the two words (Self and Vaisvanara) can denote many things, they are used here specifically.

Vaisvanara is Brahman, because of the distinction that qualifies the common terms "Vaisvanara" and "Self."

### 1.2.25

ॐ स्मर्यमाणमनुमानं स्यादिति ॐ ॥ १.२.२५॥

*oṃ smaryamāṇamanumānaṃ syāditi oṃ .. 1.2.25..*

The form described in the Smriti is an identifying mark that Vaisvanara means the supreme Lord. So Vaisvanara is God.

For the cosmic form of the Supreme Lord described in the Smriti is an identifying mark or inference, from which we infer the meaning of this Sruti text.

### 1.2.26

ॐ शब्दादिभ्योऽन्तः प्रतिष्ठानान्नेति चेन्न तथा दृष्ट्युपदेशादसंभवात्पुरुषविध्मपि चैनमधीयते ॐ ॥ १.२.२६॥

*oṃ śabdādibhyo'ntaḥ pratiṣṭhānānneti cenna tathā dṛṣṭyupadeśād asaṃbhavātpuruṣavidhmapi cainamadhīyate oṃ .. 1.2.26..*

Objection: Vaisvanara is not the supreme Self, given the word used and other factors, and given residence inside. Not so, because the instruction is to conceive of Brahman as such, because the specification cannot apply to others, and because they name Him even as a person (Purusha).

Objection: Vaisvanara is not Brahman or the Highest Lord, since the term has a different settled meaning (gastric fire) and since abiding within is a characteristic of the gastric fire. Reply: no, because there is the instruction to conceive Brahman as the gastric fire, because the gastric fire cannot have heaven and so on as its head and other limbs, and because the Vajasaneyins describe Vaisvanara as man, a term that cannot apply to the gastric fire.

### 1.2.27

ॐ अत एव न देवता भूतं च ॐ ॥ १.२.२७॥

*oṃ ata eva na devatā bhūtaṃ ca oṃ .. 1.2.27..*

For these same reasons Vaisvanara is neither the deity nor the element.

For the same reasons Vaisvanara cannot be the deity fire or the element fire.

### 1.2.28

ॐ साक्षादप्यविरोधं जैमिनिः ॐ ॥ १.२.२८॥

*oṃ sākṣādapyavirodhaṃ jaiminiḥ oṃ .. 1.2.28..*

According to Jaimini, there is no contradiction even with direct meditation.

Jaimini declares there is no contradiction even if Brahman is directly taken, as Vaisvanara, to be the object of worship.

### 1.2.29

ॐ अभिव्यक्तेरित्याश्मरथ्यः ॐ ॥ १.२.२९॥

*oṃ abhivyakterityāśmarathyaḥ oṃ .. 1.2.29..*

According to Asmarathya, God is referred to as spatially limited from the point of view of manifestation.

Because of manifestation, says Aasmarathya.

### 1.2.30

ॐ अनुस्मृतेर्बादरिः ॐ ॥ १.२.३०॥

*oṃ anusmṛterbādariḥ oṃ .. 1.2.30..*

Badari holds that God is described as spatially limited because He is meditated on in that way.

The sage Badari says this is for the purpose of meditation or constant remembrance.

### 1.2.31

ॐ सम्पत्तेरिति जैमिनिः तथा हि दर्शयति ॐ ॥ १.२.३१॥

*oṃ sampatteriti jaiminiḥ tathā hi darśayati oṃ .. 1.2.31..*

Jaimini holds that the spatial limitation is justified by meditation based on superimposition, as another text shows.

Jaimini says the Supreme Lord may be called Pradesamatra (a span long) through imagined identity, because the Sruti states this.

### 1.2.32

ॐ आमनन्ति चैनमस्मिन् ॐ ॥ १.२.३२॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु प्रथमाध्यायस्य द्वितीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ āmananti cainamasmin oṃ .. 1.2.32.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu prathamādhyāyasya dvitīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

The followers of the Jabala branch also speak of this One (God) as located here, between the head and the chin.

The Jabalas further teach that the Supreme Lord is to be meditated on in this place, the space between the head and the chin.

### 1.3.1

<vt>The Abode of Heaven, Earth, etc.</vt>  
ॐ द्युभ्वाद्यायतनं स्वशब्दात् ॐ ॥ १.३.१॥

*<vt>The Abode of Heaven, Earth, etc.</vt>  
oṃ dyubhvādyāyatanaṃ svaśabdāt oṃ .. 1.3.1..*

The support of heaven, earth, and the rest is the supreme Self, since the word used denotes Itself.

Brahman is the abode of heaven, earth, and so on, because of the term 'own', that is, 'Self'.

### 1.3.2

ॐ मुक्तोपसृप्यव्यपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.३.२॥

*oṃ muktopasṛpyavyapadeśāt oṃ .. 1.3.2..*

For the teaching says that the free attain It.

For the scriptures declare that the liberated are to reach it.

### 1.3.3

ॐ नानुमानमतच्छब्दात् ॐ ॥ १.३.३॥

*oṃ nānumānamatacchabdāt oṃ .. 1.3.3..*

The support is no inferred entity, since no word conveys that sense.

The abode of heaven and the rest is not what is inferred, the Pradhana, because no term points to it.

### 1.3.4

ॐ प्राणभृच्च ॐ ॥ १.३.४॥

*oṃ prāṇabhṛcca oṃ .. 1.3.4..*

Nor is it a living creature.

Nor is it the individual soul.

### 1.3.5

ॐ भेदव्यपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.३.५॥

*oṃ bhedavyapadeśāt oṃ .. 1.3.5..*

And a difference is stated.

Also, a difference is declared between the individual soul and the abode of heaven and the rest.

### 1.3.6

ॐ प्रकरणात् ॐ ॥ १.३.६॥

*oṃ prakaraṇāt oṃ .. 1.3.6..*

Because of the context.

Because of the subject matter.

### 1.3.7

ॐ स्थित्यदनाभ्यां च ॐ ॥ १.३.७॥

*oṃ sthityadanābhyāṃ ca oṃ .. 1.3.7..*

And because of the facts of staying on and eating.

And because of two conditions, remaining unattached (typical of the Supreme Self) and eating (typical of the individual soul).

### 1.3.8

<vt>Bhuman (Infinite, Plenitude)</vt>  
ॐ भूमा सम्प्रसादादध्युपदेशात् ॐ ॥ १.३.८॥

*<vt>Bhuman (Infinite, Plenitude)</vt>  
oṃ bhūmā samprasādādadhyupadeśāt oṃ .. 1.3.8..*

Bhuman is the supreme Self, since It is taught as higher than samprasada (Prana, the vital force).

Bhuma is Brahman, since it is taught after the state of deep sleep, that is, after Prana, the vital air that stays awake even in that state.

### 1.3.9

ॐ धर्मोपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ १.३.९॥

*oṃ dharmopapatteśca oṃ .. 1.3.9..*

And the characteristics of Bhuman fit the supreme Self.

And the attributes that scripture gives to Bhuma apply properly only to Para Brahman.

### 1.3.10

<vt>Immutable</vt>  
ॐ अक्षरमम्बरान्तधृतेः ॐ ॥ १.३.१०॥

*<vt>Immutable</vt>  
oṃ akṣaramambarāntadhṛteḥ oṃ .. 1.3.10..*

Akasa is Brahman, since it supports all things up to and including space.

The Imperishable is Brahman, since it supports everything up to Akasa (ether).

### 1.3.11

ॐ सा च प्रशासनात् ॐ ॥ १.३.११॥

*oṃ sā ca praśāsanāt oṃ .. 1.3.11..*

Only God can provide that support, since His mighty rule is mentioned.

Because command is attributed to the Imperishable, this supporting can be the work only of the Supreme Self and not of the Pradhana.

### 1.3.12

ॐ अन्यभावव्यावृत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ १.३.१२॥

*oṃ anyabhāvavyāvṛtteśca oṃ .. 1.3.12..*

And because being any other entity is excluded.

And because the Sruti separates the Akshara from whatever has a nature different from Brahman.

### 1.3.13

<vt>The Object of Seeing</vt>  
ॐ ईक्षतिकर्मव्यपदेशात् सः ॐ ॥ १.३.१३॥

*<vt>The Object of Seeing</vt>  
oṃ īkṣatikarmavyapadeśāt saḥ oṃ .. 1.3.13..*

Since It is named as the object of the act of seeing (iksana), the supreme Self is meant.

The one to be meditated on is Brahman, since He is named as the object of sight.

### 1.3.14

<vt>Dahara (The Small Space)</vt>  
ॐ दहर उत्तरेभ्यः ॐ ॥ १.३.१४॥

*<vt>Dahara (The Small Space)</vt>  
oṃ dahara uttarebhyaḥ oṃ .. 1.3.14..*

The small space (dahara akasa) is Brahman, for the reasons that follow.

The small ether (Akasa) is Brahman, because of the arguments or expressions that come after.

### 1.3.15

ॐ गतिशब्दाभ्यां तथा हि दृष्टं लिङ्गं च ॐ ॥ १.३.१५॥

*oṃ gatiśabdābhyāṃ tathā hi dṛṣṭaṃ liṅgaṃ ca oṃ .. 1.3.15..*

The small Space is Brahman because of the going and the word Brahma-loka; other Upanishads show the same, and an indicatory mark is also present.

The small Akasa (ether) is Brahman because of the going into Brahman and the word Brahmaloka. Other Sruti texts likewise show individual souls going into Brahman, and this daily entry of souls into Brahman during deep sleep is an inferential sign by which 'Brahmaloka' may be properly interpreted.

### 1.3.16

ॐ धृतेश्च महिम्नोऽस्यास्मिन्नुपलब्धेः ॐ ॥ १.३.१६॥

*oṃ dhṛteśca mahimno'syāsminnupalabdheḥ oṃ .. 1.3.16..*

The small Space must also be God because it holds the worlds in place, a glory other texts ascribe to Him.

The small ether must be Brahman also because support is attributed to it; other scriptural passages find this greatness in Brahman alone.

### 1.3.17

ॐ प्रसिद्धेश्च ॐ ॥ १.३.१७॥

*oṃ prasiddheśca oṃ .. 1.3.17..*

And because of familiar usage.

The small Akasa is Brahman also because Akasa is well known to mean Brahman.

### 1.3.18

ॐ इतरपरामर्शात् स इति चेन्नासम्भवात् ॐ ॥ १.३.१८॥

*oṃ itaraparāmarśāt sa iti cennāsambhavāt oṃ .. 1.3.18..*

If it is argued that the small Space is the other one, the individual soul, since it is alluded to at the end: no, for that is impossible.

If it is said that the other one, the individual soul, is meant because a complementary passage refers to it, we say no, since that is impossible.

### 1.3.19

ॐ उत्तराच्चेदाविर्भूतस्वरूपस्तु ॐ ॥ १.३.१९॥

*oṃ uttarāccedāvirbhūtasvarūpastu oṃ .. 1.3.19..*

If it is argued that the small Space is the individual soul, given the later reference in the same chapter: no, there it is spoken of in its revealed true nature.

If later texts seem to mean the individual soul, we reply that they refer to it only insofar as its real nature has become manifest, that is, as non-different from Brahman.

### 1.3.20

ॐ अन्यार्थश्च परामर्शः ॐ ॥ १.३.२०॥

*oṃ anyārthaśca parāmarśaḥ oṃ .. 1.3.20..*

Moreover, the reference to the individual soul in the complementary passage serves another purpose.

And the reference to the individual soul is for a different purpose.

### 1.3.21

ॐ अल्पश्रुतेरिति चेत्तदुक्तम् ॐ ॥ १.३.२१॥

*oṃ alpaśruteriti cettaduktam oṃ .. 1.3.21..*

If it is argued from the Upanishad's mention of smallness that the small space must be the individual being: this was already refuted.

If it is said that Brahman cannot be meant because scripture declares the ether to be small, we reply that this has already been explained.

### 1.3.22

<vt>Acting in Accordance</vt>  
ॐ अनुकृतेस्तस्य च ॐ ॥ १.३.२२॥

*<vt>Acting in Accordance</vt>  
oṃ anukṛtestasya ca oṃ .. 1.3.22..*

The Light in the Mundaka Upanishad must be Brahman, because things shine in accordance with It and the word 'His' is used.

That after which the sun, moon, and the rest are said to shine is the Supreme Self, because of this shining after, and because by His light everything else is lit.

### 1.3.23

ॐ अपि स्मर्यते ॐ ॥ १.३.२३॥

*oṃ api smaryate oṃ .. 1.3.23..*

The Smriti also mentions this aspect.

The Smriti too speaks of Him, Brahman, as the universal light.

### 1.3.24

<vt>The Measured One</vt>  
ॐ शब्दादेव प्रमितः ॐ ॥ १.३.२४॥

*<vt>The Measured One</vt>  
oṃ śabdādeva pramitaḥ oṃ .. 1.3.24..*

The term itself shows that the measured One is the supreme Self.

The very word applied to it, 'Lord', shows that the person measured by the size of the thumb is Brahman.

### 1.3.25

ॐ हृद्यपेक्षया तु मनुष्याधिकारत्वात् ॐ ॥ १.३.२५॥

*oṃ hṛdyapekṣayā tu manuṣyādhikāratvāt oṃ .. 1.3.25..*

But the size is stated with reference to dwelling in the heart, since scripture addresses human beings.

The highest Brahman is said to be thumb-sized with reference to the heart, since only man is entitled to study the Vedas, practise meditation, and attain Self-realisation.

### 1.3.26

<vt>The Devas also are entitled to the study of Vedas.</vt>  
ॐ तदुपर्यपि बादरायणः संभवात् ॐ ॥ १.३.२६॥

*<vt>The Devas also are entitled to the study of Vedas.</vt>  
oṃ taduparyapi bādarāyaṇaḥ saṃbhavāt oṃ .. 1.3.26..*

Badarayana thinks beings higher than humans are also qualified for knowledge, since that is possible.

According to Badarayana, beings above men are also entitled to the study and practice of the Vedas, since this is possible.

### 1.3.27

ॐ विरोधः कर्मणीति चेदनेकप्रतिपत्तेर्दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ १.३.२७॥

*oṃ virodhaḥ karmaṇīti cedanekapratipatterdarśanāt oṃ .. 1.3.27..*

If it is objected that the gods' corporeality would create a contradiction regarding rites: no, for the Vedas record the taking on of many bodies.

If it is said that the gods having bodies contradicts sacrifices, we say no, since scripture shows the gods assuming many forms at one and the same time.

### 1.3.28

ॐ शब्द इतिचेन्नातः प्रभवात्प्रत्यक्षानुमानाभ्याम् ॐ ॥ १.३.२८॥

*oṃ śabda iticennātaḥ prabhavātpratyakṣānumānābhyām oṃ .. 1.3.28..*

If it is objected that this contradicts the validity of Vedic words: no, for the universe arises from them, as direct revelation and inference prove.

If it is said that a contradiction arises concerning the word, we say no, since the world originates from the word, as is known from direct perception (Sruti) and inference (Smriti).

### 1.3.29

ॐ अत एव च नित्यत्वम् ॐ ॥ १.३.२९॥

*oṃ ata eva ca nityatvam oṃ .. 1.3.29..*

This very fact also establishes the eternity of the Vedas.

The eternity of the Vedas follows for this same reason.

### 1.3.30

ॐ समाननामरूपत्वाच्चावृत्तावप्यविरोधो दर्शनात्स्मृतेश्च ॐ ॥ १.३.३०॥

*oṃ samānanāmarūpatvāccāvṛttāvapyavirodho darśanātsmṛteśca oṃ .. 1.3.30..*

There is no contradiction, since similar names and forms recur even as world cycles revolve, as the Vedas and the Smriti show.

Names and forms stay the same in each new cycle, so even the revolving of world cycles does not contradict the eternity of the Vedic words, as Sruti and Smriti show.

### 1.3.31

ॐ मध्वादिष्वसंभवादनधिकारं जैमिनिः ॐ ॥ १.३.३१॥

*oṃ madhvādiṣvasaṃbhavādanadhikāraṃ jaiminiḥ oṃ .. 1.3.31..*

Jaimini asserts that the gods and others lack competence for knowledge of Brahman, since they cannot be competent for Madhu-vidya and the like.

Jaimini's view is that since the gods cannot qualify for Madhu Vidya and the like, they are not qualified for Upasana or for Brahma Vidya, the knowledge of the Self.

### 1.3.32

ॐ ज्योतिषि भावाच्च ॐ ॥ १.३.३२॥

*oṃ jyotiṣi bhāvācca oṃ .. 1.3.32..*

Because the words are used for a sphere of light.

The gods are also not qualified for Vidyas because the words 'sun', 'moon', and so on, spoken of as gods, are used to mean mere spheres of light.

### 1.3.33

ॐ भावं तु बादरायणोऽस्ति हि ॐ ॥ १.३.३३॥

*oṃ bhāvaṃ tu bādarāyaṇo'sti hi oṃ .. 1.3.33..*

But Badarayana upholds the gods' competence, since what competence requires exists in them.

Badarayana, however, maintains that the gods are qualified for Brahma Vidya. There are passages indicating this, and the body, desires, and so on that qualify one for such knowledge do exist in the gods.

### 1.3.34

<vt>Topic 9: The right of the Sudras to the study of Vedas discussed</vt>  
ॐ शुगस्य तदनादरश्रवणात्तदाद्रवणात्सूच्यते हि ॐ ॥ १.३.३४॥

*<vt>Topic 9: The right of the Sudras to the study of Vedas discussed</vt>  
oṃ śugasya tadanādaraśravaṇāttadādravaṇātsūcyate hi oṃ .. 1.3.34..*

Grief came to Janasruti on hearing the swan's disparaging words, as his approaching Raikva shows; Raikva hints at this by using the word Sudra.

King Janasruti grieved on hearing contemptuous words said about him by the sage in the form of a swan. Because he approached Raikva overwhelmed by that grief, Raikva called him Sudra; Raikva's word points to the grief.

### 1.3.35

ॐ क्षत्रियत्वागतेश्चोत्तरत्र चैत्ररथेन लिङ्गात् ॐ ॥ १.३.३५॥

*oṃ kṣatriyatvāgateścottaratra caitrarathena liṅgāt oṃ .. 1.3.35..*

And his Kshatriyahood is known later, from the indicatory mark of his being mentioned with a descendant of Citraratha.

And Janasruti's Kshatriyahood is known from an inferential mark, his being mentioned later along with Chaitraratha, himself a Kshatriya.

### 1.3.36

ॐ संस्कारपरामर्शात्तदभावाभिलापाच्च ॐ ॥ १.३.३६॥

*oṃ saṃskāraparāmarśāttadabhāvābhilāpācca oṃ .. 1.3.36..*

Because purificatory rites are mentioned for others and declared absent for the Sudra.

Because purificatory ceremonies are mentioned for the twice-born and declared absent for the Sudra.

### 1.3.37

ॐ तदभावनिर्धारणे च प्रवृत्तेः ॐ ॥ १.३.३७॥

*oṃ tadabhāvanirdhāraṇe ca pravṛtteḥ oṃ .. 1.3.37..*

And because Gautama's inclination to initiate and instruct Satyakama arose once the absence of Sudrahood was ascertained.

And because Gautama is seen inclined to impart knowledge only after ascertaining that Jabala Satyakama was not a Sudra.

### 1.3.38

ॐ श्रवणाध्ययनार्थप्रतिषेधात् स्मृतेश्च ॐ ॥ १.३.३८॥

*oṃ śravaṇādhyayanārthapratiṣedhāt smṛteśca oṃ .. 1.3.38..*

And because the Smriti forbids the Sudra hearing, studying, and acquiring the meaning of the Vedas.

And since the Smriti forbids Sudras from hearing, studying, and understanding the Veda and performing Vedic rites, they are not entitled to the knowledge of Brahman.

### 1.3.39

<vt>Topic 10: The Prana in which everything trembles is Brahman</vt>  
ॐ कम्पनात् ॐ ॥ १.३.३९॥

*<vt>Topic 10: The Prana in which everything trembles is Brahman</vt>  
oṃ kampanāt oṃ .. 1.3.39..*

Prana is Brahman, because vibration is mentioned.

Prana is Brahman, because of the vibration or trembling spoken of the whole world.

### 1.3.40

<vt>Topic 11: The light is Brahman</vt>  
ॐ ज्योतिर्दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ १.३.४०॥

*<vt>Topic 11: The light is Brahman</vt>  
oṃ jyotirdarśanāt oṃ .. 1.3.40..*

Light is Brahman, since the Upanishad presents it as such.

The light is Brahman, since Brahman is seen in the scriptural passage.

### 1.3.41

<vt>Topic 12: The Akasa is Brahman</vt>

*oṃ asadvyapadeśānneti cenna dharmāntareṇa vākyaśeṣāt oṃ .. 2.1.17..*

If the effect did not exist before creation, being called "non-existent" in the Upanishad: no, for the complementary portion shows the word refers to a difference of characteristics.

Should it be said the effect does not exist before creation because it is described as that which is not, we reply 'not so,' because the latter part of the text shows the term 'that which is not' denotes another characteristic or attribute.

### 2.1.18

ॐ युक्तेः शब्दान्तराच्च ॐ ॥ २।१।१८॥

*oṃ yukteḥ śabdāntarācca oṃ .. 2.1.18..*

Reasoning and another Upanishadic text establish the effect's pre-existence and non-difference.

The same is clear from reasoning and from another Sruti text. This relation between cause and effect is established.

### 2.1.19

ॐ पटवच्च ॐ ॥ २।१।१९॥

*oṃ paṭavacca oṃ .. 2.1.19..*

The effect is also non-different from the cause, as with a piece of cloth.

Also as with a piece of cloth.

### 2.1.20

ॐ यथा प्राणादिः ॐ ॥ २।१।२०॥

*oṃ yathā prāṇādiḥ oṃ .. 2.1.20..*

It is just so with the outgoing breath and the like.

Also as with the different Pranas, or vital airs.

### 2.1.21

<vt>Non-performance of Good</vt>  
ॐ इतरव्यपदेशाद्धिताकरणादिदोषप्रसक्तिः ॐ ॥ २।१।२१॥

*<vt>Non-performance of Good</vt>  
oṃ itaravyapadeśāddhitākaraṇādidoṣaprasaktiḥ oṃ .. 2.1.21..*

Since the other, the individual soul, is described as identical with Brahman, faults such as not doing what is beneficial would follow.

Since the other, the individual soul, is stated to be non-different from Brahman, faults such as not doing what is beneficial would arise in Brahman.

### 2.1.22

ॐ अधिकं तु भेदनिर्देशात् ॐ ॥ २।१।२२॥

*oṃ adhikaṃ tu bhedanirdeśāt oṃ .. 2.1.22..*

But Brahman is greater than the embodied being, for a difference between the two is declared.

But Brahman, the Creator, is something more than the individual soul, since the Srutis state a difference between them.

### 2.1.23

ॐ अश्मादिवच्च तदनुपपत्तिः ॐ ॥ २।१।२३॥

*oṃ aśmādivacca tadanupapattiḥ oṃ .. 2.1.23..*

The analogy of stones and the like, and other reasons, make the opponent's view untenable.

The objection raised is untenable, for the case resembles that of stones and the like produced from the same earth.

### 2.1.24

<vt>Creation without Materials</vt>  
ॐ उपसंहारदर्शनान्नेति चेन्न क्षीरवद्धि ॐ ॥ २।१।२४॥

*<vt>Creation without Materials</vt>  
oṃ upasaṃhāradarśanānneti cenna kṣīravaddhi oṃ .. 2.1.24..*

If Brahman cannot be the cause because makers are seen to gather materials: no, for it is possible, as with milk.

Should you object that Brahman, having no instruments, cannot cause the universe, since an agent is seen to collect materials for any construction, we say no, because it is like milk turning into curds.

### 2.1.25

ॐ देवादिवदपि लोके ॐ ॥ २।१।२५॥

*oṃ devādivadapi loke oṃ .. 2.1.25..*

Brahman can also create without outside help, like gods and others in this world.

Brahman's creating the world is like the case of gods and other beings in ordinary experience.

### 2.1.26

<vt>Wholesale Transformation</vt>  
ॐ कृत्स्नप्रसक्तिर्निरवयवत्वशब्दकोपो वा ॐ ॥ २।१।२६॥

*<vt>Wholesale Transformation</vt>  
oṃ kṛtsnaprasaktirniravayavatvaśabdakopo vā oṃ .. 2.1.26..*

If Brahman changes into the world, either It transforms wholesale or the texts on partlessness are violated.

If Brahman is the material cause of the world, one must accept either that the whole of Brahman undergoes change or that the texts declaring It partless are violated.

### 2.1.27

ॐ श्रुतेस्तु शब्दमूलत्वात् ॐ ॥ २।१।२७॥

*oṃ śrutestu śabdamūlatvāt oṃ .. 2.1.27..*

But this must be accepted on the authority of the Upanishad, for Brahman is known from the Upanishads alone.

But this is not so, given the scriptural passages and because Brahman rests on scripture only.

### 2.1.28

ॐ आत्मनि चैवं विचित्राश्च हि ॐ ॥ २।१।२८॥

*oṃ ātmani caivaṃ vicitrāśca hi oṃ .. 2.1.28..*

For it happens this way in the individual soul too, and diverse creation occurs with gods and others.

Also because in the individual soul too, as with gods, magicians, and dreams, various creation exists. So too with Brahman.

### 2.1.29

ॐ स्वपक्षदोषाच्च ॐ ॥ २।१।२९॥

*oṃ svapakṣadoṣācca oṃ .. 2.1.29..*

And because the opponent's own view is equally flawed.

Also because the opponent's own view faces these very objections.

### 2.1.30

<vt>Possession of All Powers</vt>  
ॐ सर्वोपेता च तद्दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ २।१।३०॥

*<vt>Possession of All Powers</vt>  
oṃ sarvopetā ca taddarśanāt oṃ .. 2.1.30..*

Moreover, the Deity possesses all powers, as the Upanishads reveal.

Brahman is also endowed with all powers, as the scriptures show.

### 2.1.31

ॐ विकरणत्वान्नेतिचेत्तदुक्तम् ॐ ॥ २।१।३१॥

*oṃ vikaraṇatvānneticettaduktam oṃ .. 2.1.31..*

The argument that Brahman cannot act for lack of organs has been answered earlier.

Should it be said Brahman cannot create because It has no organs, we reply that this has already been explained.

### 2.1.32

<vt>Need of Motive</vt>  
ॐ न प्रयोजनत्त्वात् ॐ ॥ २।१।३२॥

*<vt>Need of Motive</vt>  
oṃ na prayojanattvāt oṃ .. 2.1.32..*

Brahman is not the cause, since creation would need a motive.

Brahman is not the creator of the universe, since every activity has a motive.

### 2.1.33

ॐ लोकवत्तु लीलाकैवल्यम् ॐ ॥ २।१।३३॥

*oṃ lokavattu līlākaivalyam oṃ .. 2.1.33..*

But for Brahman creation is mere pastime, as seen in the world.

But Brahman's creative activity is mere sport, such as is seen in ordinary life.

### 2.1.34

<vt>Partiality and Cruelty</vt>  
ॐ वैष्यम्यनैर्घृण्ये न सापेक्षत्वात्तथा हि दर्शयति ॐ ॥ २।१।३४॥

*<vt>Partiality and Cruelty</vt>  
oṃ vaiṣyamyanairghṛṇye na sāpekṣatvāttathā hi darśayati oṃ .. 2.1.34..*

God cannot be charged with partiality and cruelty, for He takes other factors into account. So the Vedas show.

Partiality and cruelty cannot be ascribed to Brahman, since He takes other reasons into account, namely the merit and demerit of souls. So scripture declares.

### 2.1.35

ॐ कर्माविभागादिति चेन्नानादित्वात् ॐ ॥ २।१।३५॥

*oṃ karmāvibhāgāditi cennānāditvāt oṃ .. 2.1.35..*

If Karma, merit and demerit, cannot be considered at the beginning because the fruits of work are still undifferentiated: no, for transmigration has no beginning.

Should it be objected that the Lord's regard for merit and demerit is impossible, since they are not distinct before creation, we say no, because the world has no beginning.

### 2.1.36

ॐ उपपद्यते चाप्युपलभ्यते च ॐ ॥ २।१।३६॥

*oṃ upapadyate cāpyupalabhyate ca oṃ .. 2.1.36..*

This is logical, and it is found in the scriptures.

That the world, and Karma too, has no beginning is reasonable and is also seen from the scriptures.

### 2.1.37

<vt>Propriety of All the Characteristics</vt>  
ॐ सर्वधर्मोपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ २।१।३७॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु द्वितीयाध्यायस्य प्रथमः पादः समाप्तः

*<vt>Propriety of All the Characteristics</vt>  
oṃ sarvadharmopapatteśca oṃ .. 2.1.37.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu dvitīyādhyāyasya prathamaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

Brahman is the cause, since all the characteristics of a cause properly belong to It.

Since all the qualities needed to create the world are reasonably found only in Brahman, He must be admitted to be the cause of the universe.

### 2.2.1

<vt>Samkhya View Refuted (Impossibility of Design)</vt>  
ॐ रचनानुपपत्तेश्च नानुमानम् ॐ ॥ २.२.१॥

*<vt>Samkhya View Refuted (Impossibility of Design)</vt>  
oṃ racanānupapatteśca nānumānam oṃ .. 2.2.1..*

The inferred Pradhana is not the cause, since the design cannot be explained by it, and for other reasons.

That which the Sankhyas infer, the Pradhana, cannot be the world's cause, because the design or orderly arrangement found in creation could not then be accounted for.

### 2.2.2

ॐ प्रवृत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ २.२.२॥

*oṃ pravṛtteśca oṃ .. 2.2.2..*

The inferred Pradhana cannot be the cause, since the tendency to create cannot logically arise in it.

Also because activity is impossible for it.

### 2.2.3

ॐ पयोऽम्बुवच्चेत्तत्रापि ॐ ॥ २.२.३॥

*oṃ payo'mbuvaccettatrāpi oṃ .. 2.2.3..*

If Pradhana is claimed to act spontaneously like milk and water, even there intelligence is the guide.

Should it be said the Pradhana spontaneously modifies herself into the various products like milk or water, with no intelligence guiding her, we reply that there too it is due to intelligence.

### 2.2.4

ॐ व्यतिरेकानवस्थितेश्चानपेक्षत्वात् ॐ ॥ २.२.४॥

*oṃ vyatirekānavasthiteścānapekṣatvāt oṃ .. 2.2.4..*

Pradhana is not the cause, since nothing exists outside it, leaving it nothing to rely on for impulse to act or to stop.

The Pradhana cannot be active, because it depends on nothing, there being no external agent besides it.

### 2.2.5

ॐ अन्यत्राभावाच्च न तृणादिवत् ॐ ॥ २.२.५॥

*oṃ anyatrābhāvācca na tṛṇādivat oṃ .. 2.2.5..*

Pradhana cannot change automatically like grass and the like into milk in a cow, for that change does not occur elsewhere, as in a bull.

Nor can it be said the Pradhana modifies itself spontaneously like grass and the like turning into milk, because that does not occur elsewhere than in female animals.

### 2.2.6

ॐ अभ्युपगमेऽप्यर्थाभावात् ॐ ॥ २.२.६॥

*oṃ abhyupagame'pyarthābhāvāt oṃ .. 2.2.6..*

Even granting spontaneous modification of Pradhana, it is not the cause, since it lacks any purpose.

Even if we grant the Sankhya position on the Pradhana's spontaneous modification, it cannot be the universe's cause, because no purpose is present.

### 2.2.7

ॐ पुरुषाश्मवदितिचेत्तथापि ॐ ॥ २.२.७॥

*oṃ puruṣāśmavaditicettathāpi oṃ .. 2.2.7..*

If the soul can stimulate Pradhana, like a lame man riding a blind man or a lodestone moving iron, the defect still persists.

Should it be said that the Purusha, or Soul, can direct or move the Pradhana as a lame man directs a blind man, or as a magnet moves iron, the difficulty is still not overcome.

### 2.2.8

ॐ अङ्गित्वानुपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ २.२.८॥

*oṃ aṅgitvānupapatteḥ oṃ .. 2.2.8..*

Further, Pradhana cannot act, since no relation of principal and subordinate is possible among the gunas constituting it.

Again, the Pradhana cannot be active, because a relation of principal and subordinate matter is impossible among the three Gunas.

### 2.2.9

ॐ अन्यथानुमितौ च ज्ञशक्तिवियोगात् ॐ ॥ २.२.९॥

*oṃ anyathānumitau ca jñaśaktiviyogāt oṃ .. 2.2.9..*

Even if the inference is pursued another way, the defect persists, since Pradhana lacks the power of intelligence.

Even if it is inferred otherwise, the Pradhana lacks the power of intelligence, so the other objections to its being the universe's cause remain in force.

### 2.2.10

ॐ विप्रतिषेधाच्चासमञ्जसम् ॐ ॥ २.२.१०॥

*oṃ vipratiṣedhāccāsamañjasam oṃ .. 2.2.10..*

The contradictions it involves also make (the Samkhya doctrine) incoherent.

Its contradictions further make (the Sankhya doctrine) objectionable.

### 2.2.11

<vt>Vaisesika Objection Refuted</vt>  
ॐ महद्दीर्घवद्धा ह्रस्वपरिमण्डलाभ्याम् ॐ ॥ २.२.११॥

*<vt>Vaisesika Objection Refuted</vt>  
oṃ mahaddīrghavaddhā hrasvaparimaṇḍalābhyām oṃ .. 2.2.11..*

(The universe may come from Brahman) just as the great and long (triads etc.) come from the short (dyad) or the inextensive (atom).

Just as the great and long arise from the short and atomic, (the world may arise from Brahman).

### 2.2.12

<vt>Atoms Not the Cause of Universe</vt>  
ॐ उभयथापि न कर्मातस्तदभावः ॐ ॥ २.२.१२॥

*<vt>Atoms Not the Cause of Universe</vt>  
oṃ ubhayathāpi na karmātastadabhāvaḥ oṃ .. 2.2.12..*

(Whether adrista guides the atoms or conjunction aids them), action is impossible either way, so neither creation nor dissolution can occur.

Either way (whether the Adrishta, the unseen principle, inheres in the atoms or in the soul), the atoms cannot act; so that (creation through the union of atoms) is ruled out.

### 2.2.13

ॐ समवायाभ्युपगमाच्च साम्यादनवस्थितेः ॐ ॥ २.२.१३॥

*oṃ samavāyābhyupagamācca sāmyādanavasthiteḥ oṃ .. 2.2.13..*

Assuming inherence also (rules out creation and dissolution), since the same reasoning leads to infinite regress.

Admitting Samavaya produces, by the same reasoning, a regressus ad infinitum (so the Vaiseshika theory fails).

### 2.2.14

ॐ नित्यमेव च भावात् ॐ ॥ २.२.१४॥

*oṃ nityameva ca bhāvāt oṃ .. 2.2.14..*

(Activity etc.) would persist eternally, which is a further reason (to reject the atomic theory).

(Activity or non-activity) would exist permanently, (so the atomic theory cannot be admitted).

### 2.2.15

ॐ रूपादिमत्त्वाच्च विपर्ययो दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ २.२.१५॥

*oṃ rūpādimattvācca viparyayo darśanāt oṃ .. 2.2.15..*

Having color etc., the atoms would become the reverse (of their stated nature), as experience shows.

If atoms have color etc., the opposite (of what the Vaiseshikas hold) would follow, for that is what is observed.

### 2.2.16

ॐ उभयथा च दोषात् ॐ ॥ २.२.१६॥

*oṃ ubhayathā ca doṣāt oṃ .. 2.2.16..*

(The atomic theory also fails) because it is flawed from either standpoint.

Flaws arise in both cases, (so the atomic theory cannot stand).

### 2.2.17

ॐ अपरिग्रहाच्चात्यन्तमनपेक्षा ॐ ॥ २.२.१७॥

*oṃ aparigrahāccātyantamanapekṣā oṃ .. 2.2.17..*

Since (the worthy) do not accept it, this (theory of the atom as cause) should be wholly disregarded.

(Authoritative sages like Manu and others) do not accept (the atomic theory), so it must be rejected entirely.

### 2.2.18

<vt>Refutation of Buddhist Realists</vt>  
ॐ समुदायोभयहेतुकेऽपि तदप्राप्तिः ॐ ॥ २.२.१८॥

*<vt>Refutation of Buddhist Realists</vt>  
oṃ samudāyobhayahetuke'pi tadaprāptiḥ oṃ .. 2.2.18..*

Even supposing integration comes from either of the causes, it will not be accomplished.

Even if the (two kinds of) aggregates come from their two causes, (the two aggregates) would not be established.

### 2.2.19

ॐ इतरेतरप्रत्ययत्वादिति चेन्नौत्पत्तिमात्रनिमित्तत्वात् ॐ ॥ २.२.१९॥

*oṃ itaretarapratyayatvāditi cennautpattimātranimittatvāt oṃ .. 2.2.19..*

It may be argued that combination is possible since (nescience and the rest) can cause one another (in a series). No: (nescience etc.) can each cause only the origin of the one immediately following.

If (the forming of aggregates is explained) through (nescience) in a relation of mutual causality, we say no: they are only the efficient cause of the origin (of the next links, not of the aggregation).

### 2.2.20

ॐ उत्तरोत्पादे च पूर्वनिरोधात् ॐ ॥ २.२.२०॥

*oṃ uttarotpāde ca pūrvanirodhāt oṃ .. 2.2.20..*

The earlier is canceled when the later arises, (so nescience and the rest cannot each cause the next in the series).

(Nor can nescience etc. be causally related), since the preceding thing ceases to be when the subsequent one originates.

### 2.2.21

ॐ असति प्रतिज्ञोपरोधो यौगपद्यमन्यथा ॐ ॥ २.२.२१॥

*oṃ asati pratijñoparodho yaugapadyamanyathā oṃ .. 2.2.21..*

(If the effect is said to arise) with no cause, your claim (of causation) collapses. Otherwise (if the earlier moment's entity lasts until the later one arises), cause and effect exist together.

Assuming the non-existence (of the cause, while the effect still occurs) contradicts the admitted proposition. Otherwise, (cause and effect) would be simultaneous.

### 2.2.22

ॐ प्रतिसङ्ख्याप्रतिसङ्ख्यानिरोधाप्राप्तिः अविच्छेदात् ॐ ॥ २.२.२२॥

*oṃ pratisaṅkhyāpratisaṅkhyānirodhāprāptiḥ avicchedāt oṃ .. 2.2.22..*

Neither pratisamkhya-nirodha (artificial annihilation) nor apratisamkhya-nirodha (natural annihilation) is possible, since no cessation can occur (of the current or of the individuals forming it).

There is no interruption, so conscious and unconscious destruction are both impossible.

### 2.2.23

ॐ उभयथा च दोषात् ॐ ॥ २.२.२३॥

*oṃ ubhayathā ca doṣāt oṃ .. 2.2.23..*

(The Buddhist view also fails) because of the flaw arising from either standpoint.

Objections present themselves in either case.

### 2.2.24

ॐ आकाशे चाविशेषात् ॐ ॥ २.२.२४॥

*oṃ ākāśe cāviśeṣāt oṃ .. 2.2.24..*

(Non-existence cannot be claimed) of Akasa either, since (it) lacks dissimilarity (with destruction).

The cause of Akasa (ether) is also no different (from the two other kinds of destruction), so it too cannot be a non-entity.

### 2.2.25

ॐ अनुस्मृतेश्च ॐ ॥ २.२.२५॥

*oṃ anusmṛteśca oṃ .. 2.2.25..*

Remembrance (memory) also (requires admitting a permanent soul).

Memory shows that things are not momentary.

### 2.2.26

ॐ नासतोऽदृष्टत्वात् ॐ ॥ २.२.२६॥

*oṃ nāsato'dṛṣṭatvāt oṃ .. 2.2.26..*

Something does not arise from nothing; experience does not bear that out.

(Existence or entity does) not (arise) from non-existence or non-entity, for this is never seen.

### 2.2.27

ॐ उदासीनानामपि चैवं सिद्धिः ॐ ॥ २.२.२७॥

*oṃ udāsīnānāmapi caivaṃ siddhiḥ oṃ .. 2.2.27..*

(If something can arise from nothing), then by the same logic even the indifferent would succeed.

(If existence came from non-existence), even the indifferent and inactive would reach the goal.

### 2.2.28

<vt>Buddhist Idealism Refuted</vt>  
ॐ नाभाव उपलब्धेः ॐ ॥ २.२.२८॥

*<vt>Buddhist Idealism Refuted</vt>  
oṃ nābhāva upalabdheḥ oṃ .. 2.2.28..*

(External objects are) not non-existent, since they are perceived.

The non-existence (of external things) cannot be upheld, given (our) consciousness (of them).

### 2.2.29

ॐ वैधर्म्याच्च न स्वप्नादिवत् ॐ ॥ २.२.२९॥

*oṃ vaidharmyācca na svapnādivat oṃ .. 2.2.29..*

Differing in nature, (the waking state is) not (false) like dream etc.

Consciousness differs in nature (between waking and dreaming), so (waking experience) is not like dreams and the like.

### 2.2.30

ॐ न भावोऽनुपलब्धेः ॐ ॥ २.२.३०॥

*oṃ na bhāvo'nupalabdheḥ oṃ .. 2.2.30..*

(Tendencies) cannot exist, since (on your view) external things are not perceived.

(According to the Bauddhas), the existence (of Samskaras or mental impressions) is impossible, since (external things) are not perceived.

### 2.2.31

ॐ क्षणिकत्वाच्च ॐ ॥ २.२.३१॥

*oṃ kṣaṇikatvācca oṃ .. 2.2.31..*

Being momentary, (ego-consciousness cannot be the abode).

The momentariness (of the Alayavijnana or ego-consciousness keeps it from being the abode of the Samskaras or mental impressions).

### 2.2.32

ॐ सर्वथानुपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ २.२.३२॥

*oṃ sarvathānupapatteśca oṃ .. 2.2.32..*

Moreover, (this view stands condemned) as untenable from every angle.

(The Bauddha system) is illogical in every way, (so it cannot be accepted).

### 2.2.33

<vt>Jaina View Refuted</vt>  
ॐ नैकस्मिन्नसम्भवात् ॐ ॥ २.२.३३॥

*<vt>Jaina View Refuted</vt>  
oṃ naikasminnasambhavāt oṃ .. 2.2.33..*

(The Jaina view is) wrong, since (contradictory attributes) cannot be present in one and the same thing.

(Contradictory attributes) cannot exist in one thing at one time, (so the Jaina doctrine is) not (acceptable).

### 2.2.34

ॐ एवं चात्माकार्त्स्न्यम् ॐ ॥ २.२.३४॥

*oṃ evaṃ cātmākārtsnyam oṃ .. 2.2.34..*

Likewise (the defect arises that) the soul is not all-pervasive (or has only a medium size).

In the same way (the Jaina doctrine entails) that the soul is not universal.

### 2.2.35

ॐ न च पर्यायादप्यविरोधः विकारादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ २.२.३५॥

*oṃ na ca paryāyādapyavirodhaḥ vikārādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.2.35..*

Assuming a sequence (in the increase and decrease of parts) does not avoid the contradiction either, for defects like mutability remain.

Nor does the succession (of parts joining and leaving the soul in different bodies) remove the contradiction, because of the change, etc., (of the soul).

### 2.2.36

ॐ अन्त्यावस्थितेश्चोभयनित्यत्वादविशेषत् ॐ ॥ २.२.३६॥

*oṃ antyāvasthiteścobhayanityatvādaviśeṣat oṃ .. 2.2.36..*

The final size (of the soul) is permanent, so the other two must be as well; hence (the sizes) do not differ.

The final (size of the soul on release) is permanent and so, as a result, are the two (earlier sizes); so (the soul's size) never differs.

### 2.2.37

<vt>God Is Not a Mere Superintendent</vt>  
ॐ पत्युरसामञ्जस्यात् ॐ ॥ २.२.३७॥

*<vt>God Is Not a Mere Superintendent</vt>  
oṃ patyurasāmañjasyāt oṃ .. 2.2.37..*

The Lord cannot be creator, as that leads to incongruity.

That doctrine is inconsistent, (so) the Lord (cannot be the efficient or operative cause of the world).

### 2.2.38

ॐ संबन्धानुपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ २.२.३८॥

*oṃ saṃbandhānupapatteśca oṃ .. 2.2.38..*

(The incongruity) also arises because no relationship is possible.

Also, no relation (between the Lord and the Pradhana or the souls) is possible.

### 2.2.39

ॐ अधिष्ठानानुपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ २.२.३९॥

*oṃ adhiṣṭhānānupapatteśca oṃ .. 2.2.39..*

(Nature) cannot come under (His) direction, (so the position fails). (Or) (God cannot be proved), since He can have no physical support (adhisthana).

Rulership (by the Lord) is also impossible.

### 2.2.40

ॐ करणवच्चेन्न भोगादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ २.२.४०॥

*oṃ karaṇavaccenna bhogādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.2.40..*

If God is said to direct Nature as (a person directs) the organs, no: God would then have experiences (of happiness, sorrow etc.). (Or) If God is assumed to have a body with sense-organs, (we say) this is impossible because of the (resulting) experiences etc.

If (the Lord is said to rule the Pradhana etc.) as (the Jiva rules) the senses (also unperceived), we say no, because of enjoyment, etc.

### 2.2.41

ॐ अन्तवत्त्वमसर्वज्ञता वा ॐ ॥ २.२.४१॥

*oṃ antavattvamasarvajñatā vā oṃ .. 2.2.41..*

God would be subject to finitude or would lose omniscience.

(Their doctrine would make the Lord) subject to destruction or not omniscient.

### 2.2.42

<vt>Bhagavata View Refuted</vt>  
ॐ उत्पत्त्यसम्भवात् ॐ ॥ २.२.४२॥

*<vt>Bhagavata View Refuted</vt>  
oṃ utpattyasambhavāt oṃ .. 2.2.42..*

(The Bhagavata view that Samkarsana and others arise in succession from Vasudeva and others is wrong), since no origin (for the soul) is possible.

(The individual soul) cannot originate (from the Highest Lord), (so the Bhagavata or Pancharatra doctrine is unacceptable).

### 2.2.43

ॐ न च कर्तुः करणम् ॐ ॥ २.२.४३॥

*oṃ na ca kartuḥ karaṇam oṃ .. 2.2.43..*

(This view is also wrong because) an instrument cannot arise from its agent (who uses it).

(It is) also not (observed that) an instrument (is produced) from the agent.

### 2.2.44

ॐ विज्ञानादिभावे वा तदप्रतिषेधः ॐ ॥ २.२.४४॥

*oṃ vijñānādibhāve vā tadapratiṣedhaḥ oṃ .. 2.2.44..*

Or even if (Vasudeva and others are assumed to) have knowledge, (majesty etc.), the defect remains unremedied.

Or if (the four Vyuhas are said to) have infinite knowledge etc., that still does not answer (the objection raised in Sutra 42).

### 2.2.45

ॐ विप्रतिषेधाच्च ॐ ॥ २.२.४५॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु द्वितीयाध्यायस्य द्वितीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ vipratiṣedhācca oṃ .. 2.2.45.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu dvitīyādhyāyasya dvitīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

Moreover, (this scripture) contains many contradictions and opposes the Vedas.

Contradictions also (make the Pancharatra doctrine untenable).

### 2.3.1

<vt>Origin of Space</vt>  
ॐ न वियदश्रुतेः ॐ ॥ २.३.१॥

*<vt>Origin of Space</vt>  
oṃ na viyadaśruteḥ oṃ .. 2.3.1..*

Space is not (created), since (some of) the Upanishads say nothing of this.

(The Purvapakshin, or objector, says that) ether (Akasa) does not (originate), since Sruti does not say it does.

### 2.3.2

ॐ अस्ति तु ॐ ॥ २.३.२॥

*oṃ asti tu oṃ .. 2.3.2..*

But (the origin of space) is mentioned.

But (a Sruti text does state that Akasa is created).

### 2.3.3

ॐ गौण्यसम्भवात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३॥

*oṃ gauṇyasambhavāt oṃ .. 2.3.3..*

(The Upanishadic passage on the creation of space) is meant in a secondary sense, as real creation is impossible.

(The Sruti text on the origin of Akasa) has a secondary sense, since (Akasa's origination) is impossible.

### 2.3.4

ॐ शब्दाच्च ॐ ॥ २.३.४॥

*oṃ śabdācca oṃ .. 2.3.4..*

Vedic texts (bear this out) as well.

Sruti texts also (show Akasa to be eternal).

### 2.3.5

ॐ स्याच्चैकस्य ब्रह्मशब्दवत् ॐ ॥ २.३.५॥

*oṃ syāccaikasya brahmaśabdavat oṃ .. 2.3.5..*

One word ("originated") can carry (primary and secondary senses), like the word Brahman.

The single word ('sprang' – Sambhutah) can be used in secondary and primary senses, like the word Brahman.

### 2.3.6

ॐ प्रतिज्ञाहानिरव्यतिरेकाच्छब्देभ्यः ॐ ॥ २.३.६॥

*oṃ pratijñāhāniravyatirekācchabdebhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.6..*

The (Vedic) claim (that "all things become known when the one is known") holds only if every effect is non-different from Brahman, and Vedic texts confirm this.

The proposition (that knowing one thing makes everything else known) is preserved only through the non-difference (of the whole world from Brahman), as stated in the Veda or Sruti texts (declaring the non-difference of cause and effects).

### 2.3.7

ॐ यावद्विकारं तु विभागो लोकवत् ॐ ॥ २.३.७॥

*oṃ yāvadvikāraṃ tu vibhāgo lokavat oṃ .. 2.3.7..*

But (space is a product), since wherever there is an effect there is separateness, as the world shows.

But wherever effects exist, separateness exists, as seen in the world (in ordinary life).

### 2.3.8

<vt>Origin of Air</vt>  
ॐ एतेन मातरिश्वा व्याख्यातः ॐ ॥ २.३.८॥

*<vt>Origin of Air</vt>  
oṃ etena mātariśvā vyākhyātaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.8..*

This explains air as well.

The foregoing explanation of Akasa as a product also explains (that) air (is an effect).

### 2.3.9

<vt>Origin of Brahman Denied</vt>  
ॐ असम्भवस्तु सतोऽनुपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ २.३.९॥

*<vt>Origin of Brahman Denied</vt>  
oṃ asambhavastu sato'nupapatteḥ oṃ .. 2.3.9..*

But an (origin) for Existence (Brahman) is impossible, being illogical.

But that which is (Brahman) has no origin, since (such an origin) is impossible.

### 2.3.10

<vt>Origin of Fire</vt>  
ॐ तेजोऽतस्तथा ह्याह ॐ ॥ २.३.१०॥

*<vt>Origin of Fire</vt>  
oṃ tejo'tastathā hyāha oṃ .. 2.3.10..*

Fire arises from this (air), as the Upanishad states.

Fire (is produced) from this (air); so indeed (declares the Sruti).

### 2.3.11

<vt>Origin of Water</vt>  
ॐ आपः ॐ ॥ २.३.११॥

*<vt>Origin of Water</vt>  
oṃ āpaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.11..*

Water (arose from this fire).

Water (comes from fire).

### 2.3.12

<vt>Origin of Earth</vt>  
ॐ पृथिव्यधिकाररूपशब्दान्तरादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ २.३.१२॥

*<vt>Origin of Earth</vt>  
oṃ pṛthivyadhikārarūpaśabdāntarādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.12..*

(The word "food" means) earth, given the topic, color, and other Vedic texts.

The subject matter, color, and other Sruti texts show that the earth (is meant by the word 'Anna').

### 2.3.13

<vt>Creation from God's Deliberation</vt>  
ॐ तदभिध्यानादेव तु तल्लिङ्गात् सः ॐ ॥ २.३.१३॥

*<vt>Creation from God's Deliberation</vt>  
oṃ tadabhidhyānādeva tu talliṅgāt saḥ oṃ .. 2.3.13..*

He alone, through deep meditation on each thing, (created it), as His indicatory marks show.

But the indicating mark, the reflection attributed to the elements, shows that He (the Lord is the creative principle within the elements).

### 2.3.14

<vt>Reverse Order of Dissolution</vt>  
ॐ विपर्ययेण तु क्रमोऽत उपपद्यते च ॐ ॥ २.३.१४॥

*<vt>Reverse Order of Dissolution</vt>  
oṃ viparyayeṇa tu kramo'ta upapadyate ca oṃ .. 2.3.14..*

The order of dissolution runs in reverse of this order of creation. That is logical too.

The order (in which the elements withdraw into Brahman during Pralaya or dissolution) is the reverse of (the order of creation), and this is reasonable.

### 2.3.15

<vt>The Origin of Mind and Intellect</vt>  
ॐ अन्तरा विज्ञानमनसी क्रमेण तल्लिङ्गादिति चेन्नाविशेषात् ॐ ॥ २.३.१५॥

*<vt>The Origin of Mind and Intellect</vt>  
oṃ antarā vijñānamanasī krameṇa talliṅgāditi cennāviśeṣāt oṃ .. 2.3.15..*

It may be argued that intellect and mind must fit in somewhere at an intermediate stage, since marks of their existence are evident. Not so: their presence makes no difference (it does not disturb the order of creation or dissolution).

It may be said that the intellect and mind (are mentioned) between (Brahman and the elements, so their origin and reabsorption belong) somewhere in the series, as inferential signs (breaking the order of the elements' creation). We say not so, since (intellect and mind are) non-different (from the elements).

### 2.3.16

<vt>Birth and Death</vt>  
ॐ चराचरव्यपाश्रयस्तु स्यात् तद्व्यपदेशो भाक्तः तद्भावभावित्वात् ॐ ॥ २.३.१६॥

*<vt>Birth and Death</vt>  
oṃ carācaravyapāśrayastu syāt tadvyapadeśo bhāktaḥ tadbhāvabhāvitvāt oṃ .. 2.3.16..*

Talk of birth and death applies primarily to the moving and the motionless. For the soul it is secondary, since such words apply only when a body is present.

But mention of (the individual soul's birth and death) fits only the bodies of moving and non-moving beings. Applied to the soul it is secondary or metaphorical, since those terms depend on the existence of (the body).

### 2.3.17

<vt>Origin of the Soul</vt>  
ॐ नात्मा अश्रुतेर्नित्यत्वाच्च ताभ्यः ॐ ॥ २.३.१७॥

*<vt>Origin of the Soul</vt>  
oṃ nātmā aśruternityatvācca tābhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.17..*

The individual soul has no origin: the Upanishads do not mention one, they teach its eternality, and (there are other reasons).

The individual soul is not (produced), (for) the scriptures do not say (so), and according to them (the Sruti texts) it is eternal.

### 2.3.18

<vt>Eternally Conscious Soul</vt>  
ॐ ज्ञोऽत एव ॐ ॥ २.३.१८॥

*<vt>Eternally Conscious Soul</vt>  
oṃ jño'ta eva oṃ .. 2.3.18..*

For this very reason (being free of origin and dissolution), the soul is eternally a cognizer.

For this very reason (that it is not created), (the individual soul is) intelligence (itself).

### 2.3.19

<vt>Soul's Dimensions</vt>  
ॐ युक्तेश्च ॐ ॥ २.३.१९॥

*<vt>Soul's Dimensions</vt>  
oṃ yukteśca oṃ .. 2.3.19..*

(The individual soul must be atomic in size, since the Vedas mention) its leaving the body, going (to the next world along a course), and coming back (from there).

(Because scriptures declare) that (the soul) passes out, goes, and returns, (it is not infinite in size; it is atomic).

### 2.3.20

ॐ उत्क्रान्तिगत्यागतीनाम् ॐ ॥ २.३.२०॥

*oṃ utkrāntigatyāgatīnām oṃ .. 2.3.20..*

(The soul's atomicity is confirmed) because these latter two facts (following a course and coming back) relate to one's own soul.

The latter two (going and returning) are connected with their soul (agent), (so the soul is atomic).

### 2.3.21

ॐ स्वात्मना चोत्तरयोः ॐ ॥ २.३.२१॥

*oṃ svātmanā cottarayoḥ oṃ .. 2.3.21..*

If it is objected that the soul is not atomic, since its size is described otherwise, we reply no: that context concerns the other (the supreme Self).

If (the soul is said to be) not atomic because the scriptures describe it otherwise (as all-pervading), we say not so, for (those passages) are about the one other than the individual soul (the Supreme Brahman or Highest Self).

### 2.3.22

ॐ नाणुरतत् श्रुतेरिति चेन्न इतराधिकारात् ॐ ॥ २.३.२२॥

*oṃ nāṇuratat śruteriti cenna itarādhikārāt oṃ .. 2.3.22..*

The individual soul is also atomic because the Upanishads use the word directly and mention infinitesimality.

Direct statements (in the Sruti texts about atomic size) and infinitesimal measure also (show the soul to be atomic).

### 2.3.23

ॐ स्वशब्दोन्मानाभ्यां च ॐ ॥ २.३.२३॥

*oṃ svaśabdonmānābhyāṃ ca oṃ .. 2.3.23..*

(The soul's being atomic and feeling throughout the body) is no contradiction, as with (a drop of) sandal paste.

No contradiction arises, as with sandal paste.

### 2.3.24

ॐ अविरोधश्चन्दनवत् ॐ ॥ २.३.२४॥

*oṃ avirodhaścandanavat oṃ .. 2.3.24..*

If it is objected that (the sandal paste example works) because of its specific location, (not evident for the soul), we say no: the Upanishads admit (a specific location) for the soul, for it lives in the heart.

If (the two cases are said not to be parallel) because of a specific abode (present for the sandal ointment, absent for the soul), we deny it, since (scripture) acknowledges (a special place for the soul), namely, within the heart.

### 2.3.25

ॐ अवस्थितिवैशेष्यादिति चेन्नाभ्युपगमाद्धृद्धि हि ॐ ॥ २.३.२५॥

*oṃ avasthitivaiśeṣyāditi cennābhyupagamāddhṛddhi hi oṃ .. 2.3.25..*

Or the soul may pervade the whole body through its attribute of sentience, as commonly seen in the world.

Or because of its quality, intelligence, as ordinary experience shows (a lamp, for instance, reaches out by its light).

### 2.3.26

ॐ गुणाद्वाऽऽलोकवत् ॐ ॥ २.३.२६॥

*oṃ guṇādvā''lokavat oṃ .. 2.3.26..*

Sentience can exist separately, as smell does.

The quality of intelligence extends beyond the soul it belongs to, as odour extends beyond a fragrant object.

### 2.3.27

ॐ व्यतिरेको गन्धवत् तथा च दर्शयति ॐ ॥ २.३.२७॥

*oṃ vyatireko gandhavat tathā ca darśayati oṃ .. 2.3.27..*

The Upanishad teaches this too.

The Sruti also declares it so.

### 2.3.28

ॐ पृथगुपदेशात् ॐ ॥ २.३.२८॥

*oṃ pṛthagupadeśāt oṃ .. 2.3.28..*

The soul and its intelligence are separate, since the Upanishads teach them separately.

Because the Sruti teaches separately that the soul pervades the body through its quality of intelligence.

### 2.3.29

ॐ तद्गुणसारत्वात्तु तद्व्यपदेशः प्राज्ञवत् ॐ ॥ २.३.२९॥

*oṃ tadguṇasāratvāttu tadvyapadeśaḥ prājñavat oṃ .. 2.3.29..*

But the soul receives such names because the modes of the intellect dominate it, as with the supreme Self.

But the statement of the soul's atomic size arises because its essence takes on the qualities of the Buddhi, as with the intelligent Lord (Saguna Brahman).

### 2.3.30

ॐ यावदात्मभावित्वाच्च न दोषस्तद्दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३०॥

*oṃ yāvadātmabhāvitvācca na doṣastaddarśanāt oṃ .. 2.3.30..*

The soul's contact with the intellect lasts as long as its worldly state continues, so there is no defect. This is what the scriptures show.

The previous Sutra is free of fault, since the soul's conjunction with the intellect exists as long as the soul in its relative aspect exists. Scripture shows this.

### 2.3.31

ॐ पुंस्त्वादिवत्त्वस्य सतोऽभिव्यक्तियोगात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३१॥

*oṃ puṃstvādivattvasya sato'bhivyaktiyogāt oṃ .. 2.3.31..*

Rather, the contact with the intellect and the rest lies latent in sleep and dissolution and can become manifest in waking and creation, as manhood emerges from boyhood.

Because it is fitting that a connection existing potentially should become manifest, like virile power and the like.

### 2.3.32

ॐ नित्योपलब्ध्यनुपलब्धिप्रसङ्गोऽन्यतरनियमो वाऽन्यथा ॐ ॥ २.३.३२॥

*oṃ nityopalabdhyanupalabdhiprasaṅgo'nyataraniyamo vā'nyathā oṃ .. 2.3.32..*

Otherwise, without an internal organ, there would be either constant perception or none, or the power of either the soul or the organs would have to be suddenly barred, limited, or lost.

If no intellect existed, there would be either constant perception or constant non-perception, or else a limitation of either the soul or the senses.

### 2.3.33

<vt>Soul as Agent</vt>  
ॐ कर्ता शास्त्रार्थवत्त्वात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३३॥

*<vt>Soul as Agent</vt>  
oṃ kartā śāstrārthavattvāt oṃ .. 2.3.33..*

The individual soul must be an agent; only then do the scriptures have a purpose.

The soul is an agent, for only so does scripture have meaning.

### 2.3.34

ॐ विहारोपदेशात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३४॥

*oṃ vihāropadeśāt oṃ .. 2.3.34..*

The soul is an agent, for there are teachings about its roaming.

Also because the Sruti teaches that it wanders about.

### 2.3.35

ॐ उपादानात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३५॥

*oṃ upādānāt oṃ .. 2.3.35..*

The soul is an agent, because it takes up the organs.

It is also a doer, since it takes the organs.

### 2.3.36

ॐ व्यपदेशाच्च क्रियायां न चेन्निर्देशविपर्ययः ॐ ॥ २.३.३६॥

*oṃ vyapadeśācca kriyāyāṃ na cennirdeśaviparyayaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.36..*

The soul is an agent because it is named so with regard to action; otherwise the indication would be contrary.

The soul is an agent also because it is so designated with regard to actions; were it not, the designation would differ.

### 2.3.37

ॐ उपलब्धिवदनियमः ॐ ॥ २.३.३७॥

*oṃ upalabdhivadaniyamaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.37..*

Just as perception shows no uniformity, neither does action.

Here too there is no fixed rule, as with perception.

### 2.3.38

ॐ शक्तिविपर्ययात् ॐ ॥ २.३.३८॥

*oṃ śaktiviparyayāt oṃ .. 2.3.38..*

The soul must be an agent; if the intellect were, power would be reversed.

Because the power of the Buddhi would be reversed.

### 2.3.39

ॐ समाध्यभावाछ ॐ ॥ २.३.३९॥

*oṃ samādhyabhāvācha oṃ .. 2.3.39..*

The soul must also be an agent because the contrary view would rule out deep meditation on God.

And because Samadhi would be impossible.

### 2.3.40

<vt>The Soul under Two Conditions</vt>  
ॐ यथा च तक्षोभयथा ॐ ॥ २.३.४०॥

*<vt>The Soul under Two Conditions</vt>  
oṃ yathā ca takṣobhayathā oṃ .. 2.3.40..*

Rather, it is like the carpenter, who exists in both conditions.

Just as the carpenter is both.

### 2.3.41

<vt>Soul's Agentship Derived from God</vt>  
ॐ परात्तु तच्छ्रुतेः ॐ ॥ २.३.४१॥

*<vt>Soul's Agentship Derived from God</vt>  
oṃ parāttu tacchruteḥ oṃ .. 2.3.41..*

But the individual soul's agency comes from God, as the Vedic texts state.

But even the soul's agency comes from the Supreme Lord; so the Sruti declares.

### 2.3.42

ॐ कृतप्रयत्नोपेक्षस्तु विहितप्रतिषिद्धावैयर्थ्यादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ २.३.४२॥

*oṃ kṛtaprayatnopekṣastu vihitapratiṣiddhāvaiyarthyādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.3.42..*

God, however, depends on the efforts made, so that injunctions and prohibitions do not become meaningless and no other defects arise.

But the Lord's making the soul act depends on the works it has done; otherwise scriptural injunctions and prohibitions would be useless.

### 2.3.43

<vt>Relation of Soul and God</vt>  
ॐ अंशो नानाव्यपदेशादन्यथा चापि दाशकितवादित्वमधीयत एके ॐ ॥ २.३.४३॥

*<vt>Relation of Soul and God</vt>  
oṃ aṃśo nānāvyapadeśādanyathā cāpi dāśakitavāditvamadhīyata eke oṃ .. 2.3.43..*

Individual souls are parts of God, since they are mentioned as different, and since some read otherwise, of Brahman's identity with fishermen, slaves, gamblers, and others.

The soul is a part of the Lord, since difference between the two is declared and non-difference from Brahman also; for some Vedic texts speak of Brahman as being fishermen, knaves, and so on.

### 2.3.44

ॐ मन्त्रवर्णात् ॐ ॥ २.३.४४॥

*oṃ mantravarṇāt oṃ .. 2.3.44..*

The words of the mantras also lead to this.

The words of the Mantra also show that the soul is a part of the Lord.

### 2.3.45

ॐ अपि स्मर्यते ॐ ॥ २.३.४५॥

*oṃ api smaryate oṃ .. 2.3.45..*

The Smriti (Gita) says this too.

The Smriti states it as well.

### 2.3.46

ॐ प्रकाशादिवन्नैवं परः ॐ ॥ २.३.४६॥

*oṃ prakāśādivannaivaṃ paraḥ oṃ .. 2.3.46..*

The Supreme Self is not touched by the individual soul's suffering, just as light and the like are unaffected by what conditions them.

Unlike the individual soul, the Supreme Lord is unaffected by pleasure and pain, just as light is unaffected by the shaking of its reflections.

### 2.3.47

ॐ स्मरन्ति च ॐ ॥ २.३.४७॥

*oṃ smaranti ca oṃ .. 2.3.47..*

The Smritis say so, and the Upanishads declare the same.

The Smritis also state this.

### 2.3.48

ॐ अनुज्ञापरिहारौ देहसम्बन्धाज्ज्योतिरादिवत् ॐ ॥ २.३.४८॥

*oṃ anujñāparihārau dehasambandhājjyotirādivat oṃ .. 2.3.48..*

Injunction and prohibition take effect through association with a body, as with light and the like.

Injunctions and prohibitions are possible because of the Self's connection with the body, as with light and so on.

### 2.3.49

ॐ असन्ततेश्चाव्यतिकरः ॐ ॥ २.३.४९॥

*oṃ asantateścāvyatikaraḥ oṃ .. 2.3.49..*

Actions and results do not intermix, since the soul has no connection with all bodies.

Because the soul does not extend beyond its own body, the results of actions are not confused.

### 2.3.50

ॐ आभास एव च ॐ ॥ २.३.५०॥

*oṃ ābhāsa eva ca oṃ .. 2.3.50..*

And the individual soul is surely only a reflection of the supreme Self.

And the individual soul is only a reflection of Paramatman, the Supreme Lord.

### 2.3.51

ॐ अदृष्टानियमात् ॐ ॥ २.३.५१॥

*oṃ adṛṣṭāniyamāt oṃ .. 2.3.51..*

Even the unseen potential results of works cannot regulate individual allocation, since they themselves cannot be so allocated.

Since the unseen principle has no fixity, works and their effects would be confused for those who hold there are many souls, each all-pervading.

### 2.3.52

ॐ अभिसन्ध्यादिष्वपि चैवम् ॐ ॥ २.३.५२॥

*oṃ abhisandhyādiṣvapi caivam oṃ .. 2.3.52..*

The same defect arises with resolves and the like.

This also applies to resolutions and so on.

### 2.3.53

ॐ प्रदेशादिति चेन्नान्तर्भावात् ॐ ॥ २.३.५३॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु द्वितीयाध्यायस्य तृतीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ pradeśāditi cennāntarbhāvāt oṃ .. 2.3.53.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu dvitīyādhyāyasya tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

If it is said that pleasure and pain can be allocated by the separate part of each soul in each body, no; for all omnipresent souls are included in all bodies.

If it is said that the distinction of pleasure, pain, and the like comes from difference of place, we say no, since the self is in all bodies.

### 2.4.1

<vt>Origin of the pranas</vt>  
ॐ तथा प्राणाः ॐ ॥ २.४.१॥

*<vt>Origin of the pranas</vt>  
oṃ tathā prāṇāḥ oṃ .. 2.4.1..*

The organs, likewise, are produced from the supreme Self.

In the same way the vital airs are produced from Brahman.

### 2.4.2

ॐ गौण्यसम्भवात् ॐ ॥ २.४.२॥

*oṃ gauṇyasambhavāt oṃ .. 2.4.2..*

The origin of the organs must be accepted, since the text about origin cannot have a secondary sense.

Because a secondary origin of the Pranas is impossible.

### 2.4.3

ॐ तत्प्राक्श्रुतेश्च ॐ ॥ २।४।३॥

*oṃ tatprākśruteśca oṃ .. 2.4.3..*

Also because the term "is born" is used earlier in the Upanishad, in its primary sense, regarding Prana.

Because the word indicating origin is mentioned first in connection with the Pranas.

### 2.4.4

ॐ तत्पूर्वकत्वाद्वाचः ॐ ॥ २।४।४॥

*oṃ tatpūrvakatvādvācaḥ oṃ .. 2.4.4..*

The Pranas must have originated from Brahman, since they come before speech.

Because fire and the other elements come before speech.

### 2.4.5

<vt>Number of Pranas</vt>  
ॐ सप्त गतेर्विशेषितत्वाच्च ॐ ॥ २।४।५॥

*<vt>Number of Pranas</vt>  
oṃ sapta gaterviśeṣitatvācca oṃ .. 2.4.5..*

The Pranas number seven, for they are so known and so specified.

The Pranas (organs) are seven, as scriptural passages convey and as their specification shows.

### 2.4.6

ॐ हस्तादयस्तु स्थितेऽतो नैवम् ॐ ॥ २।४।६॥

*oṃ hastādayastu sthite'to naivam oṃ .. 2.4.6..*

But the hands and the rest also exist; since this excess is established, the claim does not hold.

But besides the seven Pranas mentioned there are the hands and the rest. Since this is settled, we must not conclude that there are only seven Pranas.

### 2.4.7

<vt>Atomic Pranas</vt>  
ॐ अणवश्च ॐ ॥ २।४।७॥

*<vt>Atomic Pranas</vt>  
oṃ aṇavaśca oṃ .. 2.4.7..*

The organs are atomic, that is, subtle and limited in size.

And they are minute.

### 2.4.8

<vt>Chief Prana: Its Creation</vt>  
ॐ श्रेष्ठश्च ॐ ॥ २।४।८॥

*<vt>Chief Prana: Its Creation</vt>  
oṃ śreṣṭhaśca oṃ .. 2.4.8..*

The foremost Prana, too, is a product of Brahman.

The best, the chief vital air or Prana, is produced as well.

### 2.4.9

<vt>Nature of Prana</vt>  
ॐ न वायुक्रिये पृथगुपदेशात् ॐ ॥ २।४।९॥

*<vt>Nature of Prana</vt>  
oṃ na vāyukriye pṛthagupadeśāt oṃ .. 2.4.9..*

Prana is neither air nor a function, for it is taught separately.

The chief Prana is neither air nor function, since it is mentioned separately.

### 2.4.10

ॐ चक्षुरादिवत्तु तत्सहशिष्ट्यादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ २।४।१०॥

*oṃ cakṣurādivattu tatsahaśiṣṭyādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 2.4.10..*

But Prana is not independent, like the organs of vision and the rest, since instruction is imparted along with them and for other reasons.

But Prana is subordinate to the soul, like the eyes and the rest, since it is taught together with them and for other reasons.

### 2.4.11

ॐ अकरणत्वाच्च न दोषस्तथा हि दर्शयति ॐ ॥ २।४।११॥

*oṃ akaraṇatvācca na doṣastathā hi darśayati oṃ .. 2.4.11..*

No fault arises, since Prana is not a sense-organ. The Upanishads show this.

Since it is not an instrument, the objection is invalid; so scripture declares.

### 2.4.12

ॐ पञ्चवृत्तिर्मनोवद्व्यपदिश्यते ॐ ॥ २।४।१२॥

*oṃ pañcavṛttirmanovadvyapadiśyate oṃ .. 2.4.12..*

Prana is taught to have five states, as the mind does.

It is taught to have a fivefold function, like the mind.

### 2.4.13

<vt>Prana Is Atomic</vt>  
ॐ अणुश्च ॐ ॥ २।४।१३॥

*<vt>Prana Is Atomic</vt>  
oṃ aṇuśca oṃ .. 2.4.13..*

The chief Prana is atomic, that is, subtle and limited in size.

The chief Prana is also minute.

### 2.4.14

<vt>Presiding Deities</vt>  
ॐ ज्योतिराद्यधिष्ठानं तु तदामननात् ॐ ॥ २।४।१४॥

*<vt>Presiding Deities</vt>  
oṃ jyotirādyadhiṣṭhānaṃ tu tadāmananāt oṃ .. 2.4.14..*

But Fire and other deities preside, for so the scriptures teach.

But Fire and the others preside over the organs, as the Sruti states.

### 2.4.15

ॐ प्राणवता शब्दात् ॐ ॥ २।४।१५॥

*oṃ prāṇavatā śabdāt oṃ .. 2.4.15..*

The organs are connected with their possessor, as the Vedic texts make known.

The soul, not the gods, is the enjoyer, because scripture tells us the organs are connected with the one who possesses them.

### 2.4.16

ॐ तस्य च नितयत्वात् ॐ ॥ २।४।१६॥

*oṃ tasya ca nitayatvāt oṃ .. 2.4.16..*

And because of that soul's constant relation with the body.

And because the soul is permanent in the body, it is the enjoyer, not the gods.

### 2.4.17

<vt>Prana and Pranas</vt>  
ॐ त इन्द्रियाणि तद्व्यपदेशादन्यत्र श्रेष्ठात् ॐ ॥ २।४।१७॥

*<vt>Prana and Pranas</vt>  
oṃ ta indriyāṇi tadvyapadeśādanyatra śreṣṭhāt oṃ .. 2.4.17..*

Apart from the chief Prana, the other eleven Pranas are the organs, for they are so designated.

The other Pranas, excepting the best (the chief Prana), are senses, since scripture so designates them.

### 2.4.18

ॐ भेदश्रुतेः ॐ ॥ २।४।१८॥

*oṃ bhedaśruteḥ oṃ .. 2.4.18..*

Because the Upanishads mention a difference.

Because scripture states a difference.

### 2.4.19

ॐ वैलक्षण्याच्च ॐ ॥ २।४।१९॥

*oṃ vailakṣaṇyācca oṃ .. 2.4.19..*

The organs also differ from Prana in their characteristics.

And because their characteristics differ.

### 2.4.20

<vt>Creation of Gross Objects</vt>  
ॐ संज्ञामूर्तिक्लृप्तिस्तु त्रिवृत्कुर्वत उपदेशात् ॐ ॥ २।४।२०॥

*<vt>Creation of Gross Objects</vt>  
oṃ saṃjñāmūrtiklṛptistu trivṛtkurvata upadeśāt oṃ .. 2.4.20..*

But designation and shape are arranged by Him who made the elements tripartite, as the Upanishad teaches.

But names and forms are created by Him who does the tripartite creation, as the scriptures teach.

### 2.4.21

ॐ मांसादि भौमं यथाशब्दमितरयोश्च ॐ ॥ २।४।२१॥

*oṃ māṃsādi bhaumaṃ yathāśabdamitarayośca oṃ .. 2.4.21..*

Flesh and the like come from earth, as the Upanishads show; other things evolve from the other two as well.

According to scripture, flesh and the like originate from earth, and so too with the other elements, fire and water.

### 2.4.22

ॐ वैशेष्यात्तु तद्वादस्तद्वादः ॐ ॥ २।४।२२॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु द्वितीयाध्यायस्य चतुर्थः पादः समाप्तः ॥ इति द्वितीयोऽध्यायः॥

*oṃ vaiśeṣyāttu tadvādastadvādaḥ oṃ .. 2.4.22.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu dvitīyādhyāyasya caturthaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ .. iti dvitīyo'dhyāyaḥ..*

But each is named according to what preponderates in it; each is named according to what preponderates in it.

But the gross elements are named after the particular element that preponderates in them.

### 3.1.1

<vt>Departure from the Body</vt>  
॥ अथ तृतीयोऽध्यायः॥ ॐ तदन्तरप्रतिपत्तौ रंहति सम्परिष्वक्तः प्रश्ननिरूपणाभ्याम् ॐ ॥ ३.१.१॥

*<vt>Departure from the Body</vt>  
.. atha tṛtīyo'dhyāyaḥ.. oṃ tadantarapratipattau raṃhati sampariṣvaktaḥ praśnanirūpaṇābhyām oṃ .. 3.1.1..*

To obtain the next body, the individual soul departs enveloped by the subtle elements, as the question and its answer make known.

To obtain another body the soul goes enveloped by subtle elements, as appears from the question and explanation in scripture (Chhandogya).

### 3.1.2

ॐ त्र्यात्मकत्वात्तु भूयस्त्वात् ॐ ॥ ३.१.२॥

*oṃ tryātmakatvāttu bhūyastvāt oṃ .. 3.1.2..*

But water alone does not envelop the soul, since water has three components; it is mentioned because it preponderates.

Since water consists of three elements, the soul is enveloped by all of them, not by water alone; only water is named in the text because it preponderates in the human body.

### 3.1.3

ॐ प्राणगतेश्च ॐ ॥ ३.१.३॥

*oṃ prāṇagateśca oṃ .. 3.1.3..*

Since the organs go out, it follows that the elements go out too.

And because the Pranas (sense organs) go out with the soul, the elements accompany it as well.

### 3.1.4

ॐ अग्न्यादिगतिश्रुतेरिति चेन्न भाक्तत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३.१.४॥

*oṃ agnyādigatiśruteriti cenna bhāktatvāt oṃ .. 3.1.4..*

If it is objected that the organs do not accompany the soul at death, since the Upanishadic texts mention their entry into Fire and other deities, we say no; that is said in a secondary sense.

If it is said that the Pranas or organs do not follow the soul, because scripture speaks of entering Agni and so on, we say no, since those statements are secondary or metaphorical.

### 3.1.5

ॐ प्रथमेऽश्रवणादिति चेन्न ता एव ह्युपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ ३.१.५॥

*oṃ prathame'śravaṇāditi cenna tā eva hyupapatteḥ oṃ .. 3.1.5..*

If it is objected that water does not come to be known as man, since it is not heard of at first, we say no; on logical grounds, water itself is meant.

If it is objected that water is not mentioned in the first of the oblations, we say no, for the word "Sraddha" truly means water, that being its most fitting sense in the passage.

### 3.1.6

ॐ अश्रुतत्वादिति चेन्नेष्टादिकारिणां प्रतीतेः ॐ ॥ ३.१.६॥

*oṃ aśrutatvāditi cenneṣṭādikāriṇāṃ pratīteḥ oṃ .. 3.1.6..*

If it is argued that the soul does not depart enveloped by water, since the Upanishads do not say so, we say no; this is seen in those who perform sacrifices and the like, as what happens to sacrificers confirms.

If it is said that the soul does not depart enveloped by water and so on, because the Sruti does not state the soul, we say no, for scripture conveys that the Jivas who perform sacrifices and other good works alone go to heaven.

### 3.1.7

ॐ भाक्तं वाऽनात्मवित्त्वात् तथा हि दर्शयति ॐ ॥ ३.१.७॥

*oṃ bhāktaṃ vā'nātmavittvāt tathā hi darśayati oṃ .. 3.1.7..*

Rather, the statement that souls become the food of the gods is metaphorical, because they have not realized the Self. The Upanishad shows the same.

But the souls' being the food of the gods in heaven is a secondary or metaphorical usage, because they do not know the Self, as the Sruti declares.

### 3.1.8

<vt>Return of the Souls</vt>  
ॐ कृतात्ययेऽनुशयवान् दृष्टस्मृतिभ्याम् ॐ ॥ ३.१.८॥

*<vt>Return of the Souls</vt>  
oṃ kṛtātyaye'nuśayavān dṛṣṭasmṛtibhyām oṃ .. 3.1.8..*

When its actions are exhausted, the soul returns with its residual karma, as the Upanishads and Smritis make known, along the path by which it went and also by a different one.

When good work is exhausted, the soul returns to earth with a remainder of Karmas, as direct statements in Sruti and Smriti show, by the same route it ascended after death and by a different one too.

### 3.1.9

ॐ चरणादिति चेन्नोपलक्षणार्थेति कार्ष्णाजनिः ॐ ॥ ३।१।९॥

*oṃ caraṇāditi cennopalakṣaṇārtheti kārṣṇājaniḥ oṃ .. 3.1.9..*

If it is contended that rebirth comes from conduct rather than residual karma, Karsnajini says no; that Upanishadic passage is used to suggest residual karma.

If it is objected that because of conduct the remnant of Karma (Anusaya) need not be assumed for rebirth on earth, we say no, for the word "conduct" indirectly signifies the remainder. So Karshnajini thinks.

### 3.1.10

ॐ आनर्थक्यमिति चेन्न तदपेक्षत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३।१।१०॥

*oṃ ānarthakyamiti cenna tadapekṣatvāt oṃ .. 3.1.10..*

Objection: conduct would then serve no purpose. Not so, because karma depends on conduct.

Should it be said that good conduct would become pointless under this reading, we deny it, since Karma depends on good conduct.

### 3.1.11

ॐ सुकृतदुष्कृते एवेति तु बादरिः ॐ ॥ ३।१।११॥

*oṃ sukṛtaduṣkṛte eveti tu bādariḥ oṃ .. 3.1.11..*

The teacher Badari, however, holds that the word carana means good and bad works themselves.

The sage Baadari holds that conduct (Charana) simply means good and evil works.

### 3.1.12

<vt>Fate of Evil-doers</vt>  
ॐ अनिष्टादिकारिणामपि च श्रुतम् ॐ ॥ ३।१।१२॥

*<vt>Fate of Evil-doers</vt>  
oṃ aniṣṭādikāriṇāmapi ca śrutam oṃ .. 3.1.12..*

The Vedic texts show that the moon is the destination even of those who commit unholy acts and the like.

The Sruti states that those who do not perform sacrifices and so on also go to the world of the moon.

### 3.1.13

ॐ संयमने त्वनुभूयेतरेषामारोहावरोहौ तद्गतिदर्शनात् ॐ ॥ ३।१।१३॥

*oṃ saṃyamane tvanubhūyetareṣāmārohāvarohau tadgatidarśanāt oṃ .. 3.1.13..*

Vedantin: Others, however, descend after suffering in the abode of Death. Such is their ascent and descent, for the Upanishads describe this course.

Those who have not performed sacrifices and the like ascend to the abode of Yama and, after experiencing the results of their evil deeds, return to earth; the Sruti describes this course.

### 3.1.14

ॐ स्मरन्ति च ॐ ॥ ३।१।१४॥

*oṃ smaranti ca oṃ .. 3.1.14..*

The Smritis mention this too.

The Smritis say the same.

### 3.1.15

ॐ अपि सप्त ॐ ॥ ३।१।१५॥

*oṃ api sapta oṃ .. 3.1.15..*

And the Puranas mention seven hells.

Further, there are seven hells.

### 3.1.16

ॐ तत्रापि च तद्व्यापारादविरोधः ॐ ॥ ३।१।१६॥

*oṃ tatrāpi ca tadvyāpārādavirodhaḥ oṃ .. 3.1.16..*

Since Death's rule reaches even there, no contradiction arises.

Because Yama governs even in those hells, there is no contradiction.

### 3.1.17

ॐ विद्याकर्मणोरिति तु प्रकृतत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३।१।१७॥

*oṃ vidyākarmaṇoriti tu prakṛtatvāt oṃ .. 3.1.17..*

The phrase "of these two paths" means "of knowledge (that is, meditation) and action," since these are the subject under discussion.

The reference is to the two roads of knowledge and work, as those two are being discussed.

### 3.1.18

ॐ न तृतीये तथोपलब्धेः ॐ ॥ ३।१।१८॥

*oṃ na tṛtīye tathopalabdheḥ oṃ .. 3.1.18..*

The rule about the number of oblations does not apply to the third state, as the Upanishad makes evident.

This does not hold for a third place, as the scriptures declare.

### 3.1.19

ॐ स्मर्यतेऽपि च लोके ॐ ॥ ३।१।१९॥

*oṃ smaryate'pi ca loke oṃ .. 3.1.19..*

Further, the Smritis and the Mahabharata and similar works record births without parentage, and popular belief agrees.

The Smritis also record cases in this world of birth without the course of five oblations.

### 3.1.20

ॐ दर्शनाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।१।२०॥

*oṃ darśanācca oṃ .. 3.1.20..*

It is also observed that creatures are born without the five oblations.

Observation also shows this.

### 3.1.21

ॐ तृतीये शब्दावरोधः संशोकजस्य ॐ ॥ ३।१।२१॥

*oṃ tṛtīye śabdāvarodhaḥ saṃśokajasya oṃ .. 3.1.21..*

Life arising from moisture falls under the third term, plant life.

The third term, plant life, covers what arises from heat and moisture.

### 3.1.22

<vt>Similarity with Space etc., during Descent</vt>  
ॐ तत्स्वाभाव्यापत्तिरुपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ ३।१।२२॥

*<vt>Similarity with Space etc., during Descent</vt>  
oṃ tatsvābhāvyāpattirupapatteḥ oṃ .. 3.1.22..*

The descending soul becomes similar to space, air, and so on, since only this is reasonable.

Coming down from the sphere of the moon, the soul takes on a nature similar to ether, air, and so on, since only this is possible.

### 3.1.23

<vt>Intervening Period of Descent</vt>  
ॐ नातिचिरेण विशेषात् ॐ ॥ ३।१।२३॥

*<vt>Intervening Period of Descent</vt>  
oṃ nāticireṇa viśeṣāt oṃ .. 3.1.23..*

The soul's descent from one state to another does not involve long intervals, on the authority of a specific Upanishadic statement.

The soul passes through the stages of its descent in a short time, because of the special statement.

### 3.1.24

<vt>The Souls in Plants and Thereafter</vt>  
ॐ अन्याधिष्ठिते पूर्ववदभिलापात् ॐ ॥ ३।१।२४॥

*<vt>The Souls in Plants and Thereafter</vt>  
oṃ anyādhiṣṭhite pūrvavadabhilāpāt oṃ .. 3.1.24..*

As in the earlier stages, so in the later ones: the soul merely resides in paddy and the like, already occupied by other souls, for so it is declared.

The descending soul enters plants animated by other souls, as in the previous cases, according to scriptural declaration.

### 3.1.25

ॐ अशुद्धमिति चेन्न शब्दात् ॐ ॥ ३।१।२५॥

*oṃ aśuddhamiti cenna śabdāt oṃ .. 3.1.25..*

If rites involving the killing of animals are called unholy, we say no, since the scriptures sanction them.

Should sacrificial work be called unholy, we deny it, on the authority of scripture.

### 3.1.26

ॐ रेतः सिग्योगोऽथ ॐ ॥ ३।१।२६॥

*oṃ retaḥ sigyogo'tha oṃ .. 3.1.26..*

The soul then becomes connected with an inseminator.

The soul then becomes connected with the one who performs the act of generation.

### 3.1.27

ॐ योनेः शरीरम् ॐ ॥ ३।१।२७॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु तृतीयाध्यायस्य प्रथमः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ yoneḥ śarīram oṃ .. 3.1.27.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu tṛtīyādhyāyasya prathamaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

From the womb comes a new body for the descending soul.

A new body arises from the womb.

### 3.2.1

<vt>Topic -1: The Soul in the dream state</vt>  
ॐ सन्ध्ये सृष्टिराह हि ॐ ॥ ३.२.१॥

*<vt>Topic -1: The Soul in the dream state</vt>  
oṃ sandhye sṛṣṭirāha hi oṃ .. 3.2.1..*

In the intermediate stage of dream there is real creation, for the Upanishad says so.

Between waking and deep sleep a real creation occurs, because the Sruti says so.

### 3.2.2

ॐ निर्मातारं चैके पुत्रादयश्च ॐ ॥ ३.२.२॥

*oṃ nirmātāraṃ caike putrādayaśca oṃ .. 3.2.2..*

Some, following a particular branch, regard the Self as the creator of desired things, such as sons and others.

Some, namely the Kathakas, followers of one Sakha, state that the Supreme Lord is the Creator, sons and the like being the lovely things He creates.

### 3.2.3

ॐ मायामात्रं तु कार्त्स्न्येनानभिव्यक्तस्वरूपत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३.२.३॥

*oṃ māyāmātraṃ tu kārtsnyenānabhivyaktasvarūpatvāt oṃ .. 3.2.3..*

Dream creation, however, is mere Maya, because it does not fully manifest the totality of attributes found in waking.

The dream world is mere illusion, because its nature does not display the totality of the attributes of reality.

### 3.2.4

ॐ सूचकश्च हि श्रुतेः आचक्षते च तद्विदः ॐ ॥ ३.२.४॥

*oṃ sūcakaśca hi śruteḥ ācakṣate ca tadvidaḥ oṃ .. 3.2.4..*

A dream is also an omen, as the Upanishads teach and experts affirm.

Though the dream world is illusory, it still points to the future, as the Sruti shows and dream experts also declare.

### 3.2.5

ॐ पराभिध्यानात्तु तिरोहितं ततो ह्यस्य बन्धविपर्ययौ ॐ ॥ ३.२.५॥

*oṃ parābhidhyānāttu tirohitaṃ tato hyasya bandhaviparyayau oṃ .. 3.2.5..*

Through meditation on the supreme Lord, what remains hidden becomes manifest, since the soul's bondage and freedom come from Him.

Through meditation on the Supreme Lord, what ignorance hides, the equality of the Lord and the soul, becomes manifest, because the soul's bondage and freedom come from the Lord.

### 3.2.6

ॐ देहयोगाद्वासोऽपि ॐ ॥ ३.२.६॥

*oṃ dehayogādvāso'pi oṃ .. 3.2.6..*

Or rather, that covering also arises from connection with the body.

The concealment of the soul's rulership also results from its connection with the body.

### 3.2.7

<vt>Topic -2: The Soul in dreamless sleep.</vt>  
ॐ तदभावो नाडीषु तच्छ्रुतेः आत्मनि च ॐ ॥ ३.२.७॥

*<vt>Topic -2: The Soul in dreamless sleep.</vt>  
oṃ tadabhāvo nāḍīṣu tacchruteḥ ātmani ca oṃ .. 3.2.7..*

The absence of dream, that is, dreamless sleep, occurs in the nerves and the Self, as the Upanishads teach.

Dreamless sleep takes place in the nerves (Nadis, or psychic currents) and in the self, as is known from the Sruti, the statement of scripture.

### 3.2.8

ॐ अतः प्रबोधोऽस्मात् ॐ ॥ ३.२.८॥

*oṃ ataḥ prabodho'smāt oṃ .. 3.2.8..*

For the same reason, the soul wakes from this supreme Self.

Hence waking is from Brahman.

### 3.2.9

<vt>Topic -3: The same Soul returns from deep sleep</vt>  
ॐ स एव तु कर्मानुस्मृतिशब्दविधिभ्यः ॐ ॥ ३.२.९॥

*<vt>Topic -3: The same Soul returns from deep sleep</vt>  
oṃ sa eva tu karmānusmṛtiśabdavidhibhyaḥ oṃ .. 3.2.9..*

But the very same soul returns from sleep, by reason of action, remembrance, scriptural authority, and injunction.

The same soul returns from Brahman after deep sleep, because of work, remembrance, scriptural text, and precept.

### 3.2.10

<vt>Topic -4: The nature of swoon</vt>  
ॐ मुग्धेऽर्थसम्पत्तिर्परिशेषात् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१०॥

*<vt>Topic -4: The nature of swoon</vt>  
oṃ mugdhe'rthasampattirpariśeṣāt oṃ .. 3.2.10..*

One in a swoon reaches the state of sleep only partially, this being the last remaining alternative.

In one who has fainted there is half union, since this is the only hypothesis left.

### 3.2.11

<vt>Topic -5: The nature of Brahman</vt>  
ॐ न स्थानतोऽपि परस्योभयलिङ्गं सर्वत्र हि ॐ ॥ ३.२.११॥

*<vt>Topic -5: The nature of Brahman</vt>  
oṃ na sthānato'pi parasyobhayaliṅgaṃ sarvatra hi oṃ .. 3.2.11..*

Brahman cannot have a twofold character even by reason of place, for everywhere It is taught to be without attributes.

Even a difference of place cannot give the Highest a twofold character, for scripture everywhere teaches It to be without difference.

### 3.2.12

ॐ न भेदादिति चेन्न प्रत्येकमतद्वचनात् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१२॥

*oṃ na bhedāditi cenna pratyekamatadvacanāt oṃ .. 3.2.12..*

If it is argued that Brahman cannot have one character because the scriptures mention differences, we say no, for the scriptures negate each difference individually.

Should it be objected that scripture teaches difference, we reply that it is not so, because the Sruti declares the opposite with reference to each such form.

### 3.2.13

ॐ अपि चैवमेके ॐ ॥ ३.२.१३॥

*oṃ api caivameke oṃ .. 3.2.13..*

Moreover, followers of certain branches declare this.

Further, some teach this.

### 3.2.14

ॐ अरूपवदेव हि तत्प्रधानत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१४॥

*oṃ arūpavadeva hi tatpradhānatvāt oṃ .. 3.2.14..*

Brahman is certainly formless alone, for that is the dominant theme of Upanishadic teaching.

Truly Brahman is only formless, since that is the main intent of all texts about Brahman.

### 3.2.15

ॐ प्रकाशवच्चावैयर्थ्यम् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१५॥

*oṃ prakāśavaccāvaiyarthyam oṃ .. 3.2.15..*

Like light, Brahman may be taken to have different appearances, so that the scriptures do not lose their purpose.

As light seems to take on forms through contact with things that have form, so Brahman takes form in connection with Upadhis, or limiting adjuncts, because texts ascribing form to Brahman are not meaningless.

### 3.2.16

ॐ आह च तन्मात्रम् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१६॥

*oṃ āha ca tanmātram oṃ .. 3.2.16..*

The Upanishad also declares Brahman to be Consciousness alone.

The Sruti declares that Brahman is intelligence only.

### 3.2.17

ॐ दर्शयति चाथोऽपि स्मर्यते ॐ ॥ ३.२.१७॥

*oṃ darśayati cātho'pi smaryate oṃ .. 3.2.17..*

The Vedas also reveal this, and the Smritis likewise mention it.

Scripture also shows this, and Smriti states the same.

### 3.2.18

ॐ अत एव चोपमा सूर्यकादिवत् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१८॥

*oṃ ata eva copamā sūryakādivat oṃ .. 3.2.18..*

Hence too the illustrations of the sun's reflection and the like.

For this very reason, comparisons such as the images of the sun are used with respect to Brahman.

### 3.2.19

ॐ अम्बुवदग्रहणात्तु न तथात्वम् ॐ ॥ ३.२.१९॥

*oṃ ambuvadagrahaṇāttu na tathātvam oṃ .. 3.2.19..*

But that parallel fails, since nothing is perceived to be like the water.

The two things compared are not alike, since in the case of Brahman no second thing is apprehended or experienced as water is.

### 3.2.20

ॐ वृद्धिह्रासभाक्त्वमन्तर्भावात् उभयसामञ्जस्यादेवम् ॐ ॥ ३.२.२०॥

*oṃ vṛddhihrāsabhāktvamantarbhāvāt ubhayasāmañjasyādevam oṃ .. 3.2.20..*

Having entered the limiting adjuncts, Brahman seems to share in their increase and decrease. The illustration is apt, since illustration and thing illustrated correspond from this point of view.

Being inside Its limiting adjuncts, the highest Brahman participates in their increase and decrease; because the two things compared are thus suited to each other, the comparison holds good.

### 3.2.21

ॐ दर्शनाच्च ॐ ॥ ३.२.२१॥

*oṃ darśanācca oṃ .. 3.2.21..*

This also accords with Vedic revelation.

And because scripture declares it.

### 3.2.22

<vt>Topic -6: The Neti-neti text explained</vt>  
ॐ प्रकृतैतावत्त्वं हि प्रतिषेधति ततो ब्रवीति च भूयः ॐ ॥ ३.२.२२॥

*<vt>Topic -6: The Neti-neti text explained</vt>  
oṃ prakṛtaitāvattvaṃ hi pratiṣedhati tato bravīti ca bhūyaḥ oṃ .. 3.2.22..*

The Upanishad certainly denies the limitation under discussion, and then speaks of something more.

What has been mentioned so far is denied by the words "not this, not this," and the Sruti afterwards says something beyond it.

### 3.2.23

ॐ तदव्यक्तमाह हि ॐ ॥ ३.२.२३॥

*oṃ tadavyaktamāha hi oṃ .. 3.2.23..*

That Brahman is unmanifest, for the Upanishad says so.

Brahman is not manifest, for so scripture says.

### 3.2.24

ॐ अपि संराधने प्रत्यक्षानुमानाभ्याम् ॐ ॥ ३.२.२४॥

*oṃ api saṃrādhane pratyakṣānumānābhyām oṃ .. 3.2.24..*

Moreover, Brahman is realized in Samadhi, as direct revelation and inference show.

Further, Brahman is experienced in devout meditation, as we know from the Sruti and Smriti.

### 3.2.25

ॐ प्रकाशवच्चावैशेष्यं ॐ ॥ ३.२.२५॥

*oṃ prakāśavaccāvaiśeṣyaṃ prakāśaśca karmaṇyabhyāsāt oṃ .. 3.2.25..*

The effulgent Self appears different during activity, as light and the like do; yet intrinsically there is non-difference, as the repetition of "That thou art" makes evident.

Just as physical light and the like have no real difference, so there is none between Brahman and Its manifestation in activity, because the Sruti repeatedly instructs this.

### 3.2.26

ॐ अतोऽनन्तेन तथा हि लिङ्गम् ॐ ॥ ३.२.२७॥

*oṃ ato'nantena tathā hi liṅgam oṃ .. 3.2.26..*

Hence the individual attains unity with the Infinite, for such is the indicatory mark in the Upanishad.

Therefore the individual soul becomes one with the Infinite, as scripture indicates.

### 3.2.27

ॐ उभयव्यपदेशात्तु अहिकुण्डलवत् ॐ ॥ ३.२.२८॥

*oṃ ubhayavyapadeśāttu ahikuṇḍalavat oṃ .. 3.2.27..*

But since both difference and non-difference are mentioned, the relation between the supreme Self and the individual is like that of a snake and its coil.

Because the Sruti teaches both difference and non-difference, the relation of the highest Brahman to the individual soul is to be viewed like that of a snake to its coils.

### 3.2.28

ॐ प्रकाशाश्रयवद्वा तेजस्त्वात् ॐ ॥ ३।२।२८॥

*oṃ prakāśāśrayavadvā tejastvāt oṃ .. 3.2.28..*

Or they are like light and its source, both being effulgence alone.

Or like the relation of light and its substratum, since both are luminous.

### 3.2.29

ॐ पूर्ववद्वा ॐ ॥ ३।२।२९॥

*oṃ pūrvavadvā oṃ .. 3.2.29..*

Or the relation between the individual and the supreme Self is as already shown.

Or the relation between Jiva and Brahman is as given before.

### 3.2.30

ॐ प्रतिषेधाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।२।३०॥

*oṃ pratiṣedhācca oṃ .. 3.2.30..*

And because of the denial.

And because of the denial.

### 3.2.31

<vt>Topic -7: Brahman is one without a second</vt>  
ॐ परमतस्सेतून् मानसंबन्ध भेदव्यपदेशेभ्यः ॐ ॥ ३।२।३१॥

*<vt>Topic -7: Brahman is one without a second</vt>  
oṃ paramatah setūnmānasaṃbandha bhedavyapadeśebhyaḥ oṃ .. 3.2.31..*

Some entity is superior to this Brahman, because embankment, measure, connection, and difference are mentioned.

Something is superior to this Brahman, because terms denoting a bank, measure, connection, and difference are used of It.

### 3.2.32

ॐ दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ ३।२।३२॥

*oṃ darśanāt oṃ .. 3.2.32..*

But the Self is called an embankment because of similarity.

But Brahman is called a bank and so on because of similarity.

### 3.2.33

ॐ बुद्ध्यर्थः पादवत् ॐ ॥ ३।२।३३॥

*oṃ buddhyarthaḥ pādavat oṃ .. 3.2.33..*

Brahman's magnitude is spoken of for the sake of intellectual grasp, like the feet of the mind or of space, or the quarters of the karsapana.

The statement that Brahman has size is for easy comprehension, that is, Upasana or devout meditation, just like the four feet.

### 3.2.34

ॐ स्थानविशेषात्प्रकाशादिवत् ॐ ॥ ३।२।३४॥

*oṃ sthānaviśeṣātprakāśādivat oṃ .. 3.2.34..*

Connection and difference are spoken of with regard to Brahman from the standpoint of limiting adjuncts, as with light and the like.

Statements about connection and difference in Brahman are due to special places, as with light and the like.

### 3.2.35

ॐ उपपत्तेश्च ॐ ॥ ३।२।३५॥

*oṃ upapatteśca oṃ .. 3.2.35..*

And because only this position is logically justified.

And it is reasonable.

### 3.2.36

ॐ तथाऽन्यप्रतिषेधात् ॐ ॥ ३।२।३६॥

*oṃ tathā'nyapratiṣedhāt oṃ .. 3.2.36..*

Likewise, from the denial of everything else, it follows that there is nothing but Brahman.

Similarly, the express denial of all other things shows there is nothing but Brahman.

### 3.2.37

ॐ अनेन सर्वगतत्वमायामयशब्दादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ ३।२।३७॥

*oṃ anena sarvagata tvamāyāmaya śabdādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 3.2.37..*

By this the omnipresence of the Self is established, on the strength of Upanishadic words such as those of extension, and of other sources, namely Smriti and logic.

By this Brahman's Omnipresence is established, in keeping with scriptural statements about Its extent.

### 3.2.38

<vt>Topic -8: The Lord is the giver of the fruits of actions</vt>  
ॐ फलमत उपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ ३।२।३८॥

*<vt>Topic -8: The Lord is the giver of the fruits of actions</vt>  
oṃ phalamata upapatteḥ oṃ .. 3.2.38..*

The fruit of action comes from Him, this being the logical position.

The fruits of actions come from the Lord, for that is reasonable.

### 3.2.39

ॐ श्रुतत्वाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।२।३९॥

*oṃ śrutatvācca oṃ .. 3.2.39..*

God ordains results for the further reason that the Upanishads say so.

And because the Sruti teaches so.

### 3.2.40

ॐ धर्मं जैमिनिः अत एव ॐ ॥ ३।२।४०॥

*oṃ dharmaṃ jaiminiḥ ata eva oṃ .. 3.2.40..*

For these very reasons Jaimini holds that virtuous deeds yield the results.

For the same reasons, scriptural authority and reasoning, as stated in Sutras 38 and 39, Jaimini holds that religious merit brings about the fruits of actions.

### 3.2.41

ॐ पूर्वं तु बादरायणो हेतुव्यपदेशात् ॐ ॥ ३।२।४१॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु तृतीयाध्यायस्य द्वितीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ pūrvaṃ tu bādarāyaṇo hetuvyapadeśāt oṃ .. 3.2.41.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu tṛtīyādhyāyasya dvitīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

But Badarayana holds that the earlier One, God, bestows results, because He is named as the cause of action itself.

But Baadarayana holds the former view, that the Lord causes the fruits of action, since He is declared to be the cause of the actions themselves.

### 3.3.1

<vt>Sameness of Vidyas</vt>  
ॐ सर्ववेदान्तप्रत्ययं चोदनाद्यविशेषात् ॐ ॥ ३.३.१॥

*<vt>Sameness of Vidyas</vt>  
oṃ sarvavedāntapratyayaṃ codanādyaviśeṣāt oṃ .. 3.3.1..*

Any particular conception for meditation (vijnana) taught across the Upanishads is the same, because the injunction and the rest are the same.

The Vidyas, or Upasanas, described in the various Vedanta texts are identical, because their injunction and so on (connection, form, and name) do not differ.

### 3.3.2

ॐ भेदान्नेति चेदेकस्यामपि ॐ ॥ ३.३.२॥

*oṃ bhedānneti cedekasyāmapi oṃ .. 3.3.2..*

It may be said the vijnanas cannot be one because their details differ. Not so: details can vary even within one vijnana.

The objection that the Vidyas are separate because of minor differences is rejected, since such differences can occur even within one Vidya.

### 3.3.3

ॐ स्वाध्यायस्य तथात्वेन हि समाचारेऽधिकाराच्च ॐ ॥ ३.३.३॥

*oṃ svādhyāyasya tathātvena hi samācāre'dhikārācca oṃ .. 3.3.3..*

The rite of carrying fire on the head belongs to Vedic study, as the Samachara says so and as competence requires. This regulation is like the one about libations.

The rite of carrying fire on the head is tied to the Veda study (of the Atharvanikas), for the Samachara presents it that way. It is also a qualification (for Atharva Veda students), as with the (seven) oblations (Saurya etc.).

### 3.3.4

ॐ दर्शयति च ॐ ॥ ३।३।४॥

*oṃ darśayati ca oṃ .. 3.3.4..*

The scripture, moreover, reveals this.

The scripture teaches this as well.

### 3.3.5

<vt>Combination of Traits</vt>  
ॐ उपसंहारोऽर्थाभेदाद्विधिशेषवत्समाने च ॐ ॥ ३।३।५॥

*<vt>Combination of Traits</vt>  
oṃ upasaṃhāro'rthābhedādvidhiśeṣavatsamāne ca oṃ .. 3.3.5..*

In similar meditations, (all) the traits are to be combined, since their application does not differ, as with the subsidiaries of an injunction.

In Upasanas of one class (found in different Sakhas), the particulars from all Sakhas are to be combined, since the object of meditation does not differ. The subsidiary rites of a main sacrifice (given in different Sakhas) are combined likewise.

### 3.3.6

<vt>Difference of Meditations</vt>  
ॐ अन्यथात्वं च शब्दादिति चेन्नाविशेषात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।६॥

*<vt>Difference of Meditations</vt>  
oṃ anyathātvaṃ ca śabdāditi cennāviśeṣāt oṃ .. 3.3.6..*

It may be said the (Udgitha) meditations (of the Chandogya and Brihadaranyaka Upanishads) differ because their texts differ. Not so, for there is no difference.

The claim that the Udgitha Vidyas of the Brihadaranyaka and Chhandogya Upanishads differ because their texts differ is denied: in essentials they are not different.

### 3.3.7

ॐ न वा प्रकरणभेदात्परोवरीयस्त्वादिवत् ॐ ॥ ३।३।७॥

*oṃ na vā prakaraṇabhedātparovarīyastvādivat oṃ .. 3.3.7..*

Rather, they are not one, because the subject matter differs, as when (the Udgitha is meditated on) as higher than the high (and greater than the great).

Rather, the Vidyas are not one, since their subject matter differs, just as meditation on the Udgitha as the highest and greatest (Brahman) differs from meditation on the Udgitha as abiding in the eye and so on.

### 3.3.8

ॐ संज्ञातश्चेत्तदुक्तमस्ति तु तदपि ॐ ॥ ३।३।८॥

*oṃ saṃjñātaścettaduktamasti tu tadapi oṃ .. 3.3.8..*

If the (two meditations are held to be one) because the name is the same, that has already been answered. Sameness of name is found (even with quite different things).

If the Vidyas are said to be one because of an identical name, that has been explained already. An identical name is also found with admittedly separate Vidyas.

### 3.3.9

<vt>Specifications of Om</vt>  
ॐ व्याप्तेश्च समञ्जसम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।९॥

*<vt>Specifications of Om</vt>  
oṃ vyāpteśca samañjasam oṃ .. 3.3.9..*

Since Om pervades all the Vedas, qualifying it by the word Udgitha is appropriate.

Because Om extends (over the whole of the Vedas), specifying it (by the term 'Udgitha') is fitting.

### 3.3.10

<vt>Sameness of the Meditation on Prana</vt>  
ॐ सर्वाभेदादन्यत्रेमे ॐ ॥ ३।३।१०॥

*<vt>Sameness of the Meditation on Prana</vt>  
oṃ sarvābhedādanyatreme oṃ .. 3.3.10..*

Since all (the meditations on Prana) are the same, the traits (found here in one) are to be added elsewhere.

Since the Vidya does not differ anywhere (in the texts of the various Sakhas where the Prana-Vidya occurs), the qualities (given in two of them are to be inserted) in the other places (such as the Kaushitaki Upanishad).

### 3.3.11

<vt>Combination and Non-combination of Attributes of Brahman</vt>  
ॐ आनन्दादयः प्रधानस्य ॐ ॥ ३।३।११॥

*<vt>Combination and Non-combination of Attributes of Brahman</vt>  
oṃ ānandādayaḥ pradhānasya oṃ .. 3.3.11..*

Bliss and the other characteristics of the principal entity (Brahman) are to be combined.

Bliss and the other attributes (showing the true nature) of the Principal, the Supreme Self or Brahman, (are to be gathered from all places in the meditation on Brahman).

### 3.3.12

ॐ प्रियशिरस्त्वाद्यप्राप्तिरुपचयापचयौ हि भेदे ॐ ॥ ३।३।१२॥

*oṃ priyaśirastvādyaprāptirupacayāpacayau hi bhede oṃ .. 3.3.12..*

Attributes like having joy as the head are not to be added everywhere. They admit of more and less, (which is) possible only where there is difference (duality).

(Qualities like) joy being His head are not to be taken everywhere. Increase and decrease (are possible only) where there is difference (and not in Brahman, where there is non-difference).

### 3.3.13

ॐ इतरे त्वर्थसामान्यात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।१३॥

*oṃ itare tvarthasāmānyāt oṃ .. 3.3.13..*

But the other characteristics are to be understood everywhere, since their purport is identical.

But other attributes (such as Bliss are to be combined), since their purport is identical.

### 3.3.14

<vt>Purusha as the Highest in Katha</vt>  
ॐ आध्यानाय प्रयोजनाभावात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।१४॥

*<vt>Purusha as the Highest in Katha</vt>  
oṃ ādhyānāya prayojanābhāvāt oṃ .. 3.3.14..*

What the Katha Upanishad says is meant for deep meditation on Purusha, (not to set out a gradation), since that would serve no purpose.

(Katha Upanishad I.3.10 presents the Self as the highest) for the sake of pious meditation, since (knowing that objects are higher than the senses and so on) has no use.

### 3.3.15

ॐ आत्मशब्दाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।३।१५॥

*oṃ ātmaśabdācca oṃ .. 3.3.15..*

And (this must be the conclusion) because the word Self is used.

And because of the word Atman.

### 3.3.16

<vt>The Supreme Self in Aitareya</vt>  
ॐ आत्मगृहीतिरितरवदुत्तरात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।१६॥

*<vt>The Supreme Self in Aitareya</vt>  
oṃ ātmagṛhītiritaravaduttarāt oṃ .. 3.3.16..*

The supreme Self is meant in the Aitareya Upanishad, as elsewhere (in other creation texts), because of the later qualification. (Or) The Self is meant (in the Chandogya Upanishad), as in the other (Brihadaranyaka) Upanishad, because of the later (teaching on identity).

(In Aitareya Upanishad I.1) the Supreme Self is meant, as in other (creation) texts, because of the later qualification.

### 3.3.17

ॐ अन्वयादिति चेत्स्यादवधारणात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।१७॥

*oṃ anvayāditi cetsyādavadhāraṇāt oṃ .. 3.3.17..*

If it is objected that the trend of the sentences shows the Supreme Self is not meant, (the reply is that) It must be, given the definite statement (that the Self alone existed in the beginning). (Or) If the rule that opening and conclusion must agree is said to imply that the Self is not meant, we reply that It must be, given the definite statement.

If the context is said to show (the Supreme Self is not meant, we reply that) It is meant, given the definite statement (that the Atman alone existed in the beginning).

### 3.3.18

<vt>Acamana and Meditation on Prana</vt>  
ॐ कार्याख्यानादपूर्वम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।१८॥

*<vt>Acamana and Meditation on Prana</vt>  
oṃ kāryākhyānādapūrvam oṃ .. 3.3.18..*

Since acamana is mentioned as an already recognized duty, it appears (in the Upanishad) in connection with a new injunction (of meditation on Prana).

Since (the rinsing of the mouth with water mentioned in the Prana Vidya) merely restates an act (already ordained by the Smriti), the Sruti here enjoins what has not been ordained elsewhere.

### 3.3.19

<vt>Same Meditation in the Same Branch</vt>  
ॐ समान एवञ्चाभेदात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।१९॥

*<vt>Same Meditation in the Same Branch</vt>  
oṃ samāna evañcābhedāt oṃ .. 3.3.19..*

(Meditations) within one branch are likewise the same (and their traits are to be combined), since the object does not differ.

Within the same (Sakha) too the Vidya is one, since (the object of meditation) does not differ.

### 3.3.20

<vt>No Combination in Meditation on Satya-Brahman</vt>  
ॐ संबन्धादेवमन्यत्रापि ॐ ॥ ३।३।२०॥

*<vt>No Combination in Meditation on Satya-Brahman</vt>  
oṃ saṃbandhādevamanyatrāpi oṃ .. 3.3.20..*

Elsewhere too (in meditation on Satya-Brahman), (the attributes are to be combined) as here (in the Sandilya-Vidya), because of the very connection (with one object of meditation).

So too in other cases, because (the particulars) are connected (with one and the same Vidya).

### 3.3.21

ॐ न वा विशेषात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।२१॥

*oṃ na vā viśeṣāt oṃ .. 3.3.21..*

Rather, they are not to be combined, because of a distinction.

Rather not (so), because (the places) differ.

### 3.3.22

ॐ दर्शयति च ॐ ॥ ३।३।२२॥

*oṃ darśayati ca oṃ .. 3.3.22..*

The scripture also points to this.

(The scripture) declares (that) too.

### 3.3.23

<vt>Attributes of Brahman in Ranayaniya not to be combined elsewhere</vt>  
ॐ सम्भृतिद्युव्याप्त्यपि चातः ॐ ॥ ३।३।२३॥

*<vt>Attributes of Brahman in Ranayaniya not to be combined elsewhere</vt>  
oṃ sambhṛtidyuvyāptyapi cātaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.23..*

For the same reason (association with special abodes), (Brahman's attributes such as) unchallenged powers and pervading heaven are also not to be added to other meditations.

For the same reason (as in the previous Sutra), supporting (the world) and pervading the sky (ascribed to Brahman in the Ranayaniya Khila) are also (not to be included in other Vidyas or Upasanas of Brahman).

### 3.3.24

<vt>Purusha-Vidya in Chandogya and Taittiriya</vt>  
ॐ पुरुषविद्यायामिव चेतरेषामनाम्नानात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।२४॥

*<vt>Purusha-Vidya in Chandogya and Taittiriya</vt>  
oṃ puruṣavidyāyāmiva cetareṣāmanāmnānāt oṃ .. 3.3.24..*

The characteristics of the Purusha-Vidya are not to be added to the Taittiriyaka, since they are not recited there as they are in the Purusha-Vidya of other branches.

And since (the qualities) in the Purusha-Vidya (of the Chhandogya) are not mentioned (in that) of the others (the Taittiriya), (the two Purusha-Vidyas are not one and are not to be combined).

### 3.3.25

<vt>Non-combination of Disparate Traits</vt>  
ॐ वेधाद्यर्थभेदात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।२५॥

*<vt>Non-combination of Disparate Traits</vt>  
oṃ vedhādyarthabhedāt oṃ .. 3.3.25..*

Piercing and the like are not to be applied in meditation, since (the mantras of piercing etc. differ) in purport.

Because the matter (of certain Mantras), such as piercing, differs (from that of the nearby Vidyas), (the former are not to be combined with the latter).

### 3.3.26

<vt>Rejection and Reception of Merit</vt>  
ॐ हानौ तूपायनशब्दशेषात् कुशाच्छन्दस्स्तुत्युपगानवत् तदुक्तम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।२६॥

*<vt>Rejection and Reception of Merit</vt>  
oṃ hānau tūpāyanaśabdaśeṣāt kuśācchandasstutyupagānavat taduktam oṃ .. 3.3.26..*

Where only the rejection of virtue and vice is mentioned, their reception by others is to be inferred, since reception is the counterpart of rejection. This follows the analogy of kusas, metres, praise, and recitation, as (Jaimini) has explained.

Where only the getting rid (of good and evil) is mentioned, (their being obtained by others has to be added), since the statement of acceptance supplements (that of getting rid of them), as with the Kusas, metres, praise, and hymns or recitations. (Jaimini in the Purvamimamsa) has stated the reason for this.

### 3.3.27

<vt>Discarding Virtue and Vice at Death</vt>  
ॐ साम्पराये तर्तव्याभावात् तथा ह्यन्ये ॐ ॥ ३।३।२७॥

*<vt>Discarding Virtue and Vice at Death</vt>  
oṃ sāmparāye tartavyābhāvāt tathā hyanye oṃ .. 3.3.27..*

(A person of knowledge gets rid of virtue and vice) at death, since nothing remains to be attained. Others (the followers of other branches) state it this way.

(The one who attains knowledge gets rid of good and evil deeds) at death, since nothing remains to be attained (by them through those works on the way to Brahmaloka). So others (declare in their sacred texts).

### 3.3.28

ॐ छन्दत उभयाविरोधात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।२८॥

*oṃ chandata ubhayāvirodhāt oṃ .. 3.3.28..*

Since the two (two texts, or cause and effect) do not conflict once destruction is admitted to come from voluntary effort, (such effort must take place before death).

(The view that the individual soul practicing Yama-Niyama) at will (discards good and evil works while living is reasonable), for then the two (cause and effect, as well as the Chhandogya and another Sruti) are in harmony.

### 3.3.29

<vt>Paths of Those Who Know Or Do Not Know the</vt>  
ॐ गतेरर्थवत्त्वमुभयथाऽन्यथा हि विरोधः ॐ ॥ ३।३।२९॥

*<vt>Paths of Those Who Know Or Do Not Know the</vt>  
oṃ gaterarthavattvamubhayathā'nyathā hi virodhaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.29..*

The soul's course after death must have purpose in two ways; otherwise there is contradiction.

The (soul's) journey (on the path of the gods, Devayana) applies in a twofold way; otherwise (scripture) would be contradicted.

### 3.3.30

ॐ उपपन्नस्तल्लक्षणार्थोपलब्धेः लोकवत् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३०॥

*oṃ upapannastallakṣaṇārthopalabdheḥ lokavat oṃ .. 3.3.30..*

This (distinction) is reasonable, since signs of a soul's journey are found (only in meditation on the qualified Brahman), just as (such differences are) found in common life.

(The twofold view above) is justified, for a purpose marked by it (the purpose of the going) is observed, as in ordinary life.

### 3.3.31

<vt>The Path of Gods Is for All Worshippers of</vt>  
ॐ अनियमः सर्वेषामविरोधश्शब्दानुमानाभ्याम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३१॥

*<vt>The Path of Gods Is for All Worshippers of</vt>  
oṃ aniyamaḥ sarveṣāmavirodhaśśabdānumānābhyām oṃ .. 3.3.31..*

(The souls' journey on the path of the gods is) not restricted (to any one meditation). It applies to all meditations (on the qualified Brahman). There is no contradiction, as Upanishadic and Smriti texts (literally, direct text and inference) show.

There is no restriction (on going by the path of the gods for any Vidya). Sruti and Smriti show there is no contradiction.

### 3.3.32

<vt>People with a Mission</vt>  
ॐ यावदधिकारमवस्थितिराधिकारिकाणाम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३२॥

*<vt>People with a Mission</vt>  
oṃ yāvadadhikāramavasthitirādhikārikāṇām oṃ .. 3.3.32..*

Those with a mission to fulfill remain in the bodily state as long as the mission requires.

Those with a mission to fulfill have (bodily) existence until the mission is fulfilled.

### 3.3.33

<vt>Conception of the Immutable</vt>  
ॐ अक्षरधियां त्वविरोधः सामान्य तद्भावा भ्यामौपसदवत् तदुक्तम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३३॥

*<vt>Conception of the Immutable</vt>  
oṃ akṣaradhiyāṃ tvavirodhaḥ sāmānyatadbhāvābhyāmaupasadavattaduktam oṃ .. 3.3.33..*

All the (negative) conceptions of the Immutable are to be combined, since the manner of presentation is similar and the object is the same. This is as with the Upasad sacrifice, as Jaimini has shown.

But the conceptions of the (negative) attributes of the Imperishable (Brahman) are to be combined (from the different texts on It, as they form one Vidya), since (defining It through denials) is similar and the object (the Imperishable Brahman) is the same, as with the Upasad (offerings). (Jaimini in the Purvamimamsa) has explained this.

### 3.3.34

<vt>Same Conception in Mundaka and Katha</vt>  
ॐ इयदामननात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३४॥

*<vt>Same Conception in Mundaka and Katha</vt>  
oṃ iyadāmananāt oṃ .. 3.3.34..*

The conceptions (in the Mundaka and Svetasvatara on one side and the Katha on the other) are the same, since a particular limit is mentioned.

Because (the same thing) is described as such and such.

### 3.3.35

<vt>The Inmost Self in Brihadaranyaka</vt>  
ॐ अन्तरा भूतग्रामवदिति चेत् तदुक्तम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३५॥

*<vt>The Inmost Self in Brihadaranyaka</vt>  
oṃ antarā bhūtagrāmavaditi cet taduktam oṃ .. 3.3.35..*

(The conception of the Self is the same in Brihadaranyaka III-iv-1 and III-v-1, since) one's own Self is declared the inmost of all, as with the aggregate of elements. (Or: since one's own Self is declared the inmost of all, just as It is shown to be the Self of all in Svetasvatara VI-11).

Since the Self is within all, as with the aggregate of the elements, (the Vidya is one).

### 3.3.36

ॐ अन्यथा भेदानुपपत्तिरितिचेन्नोपदेशवत् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३६॥

*oṃ anyathā bhedānupapattiriticennopadeśavat oṃ .. 3.3.36..*

If it is argued that the separate statements make no sense unless difference is admitted, the reply is no: it can be like another instruction of this kind.

If it is said (that the two Vidyas are separate, since) the repetition cannot otherwise be explained, we reply no; (it is) like (the repetition) in another instruction (in the Chhandogya).

### 3.3.37

<vt>Reciprocity of Conceptions</vt>  
ॐ व्यतिहारो विशिंषन्ति हीतरव्रत् ॐ ॥ ३।३।३७॥

*<vt>Reciprocity of Conceptions</vt>  
oṃ vyatihāro viśiṃṣanti hītaravrat oṃ .. 3.3.37..*

There should be mutual interchange, as with other traits, for the readers (of the scriptures) recite it distinctly.

There is exchange (of meditation), since the texts distinguish (two meditations), as in other cases.

### 3.3.38

<vt>Satya-Brahman in Brihadaranyaka</vt>  
ॐ सैव हि सत्यादयः ॐ ॥ ३।३।३८॥

*<vt>Satya-Brahman in Brihadaranyaka</vt>  
oṃ saiva hi satyādayaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.38..*

Since the same Satya-Vidya is taught in both places (of the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad), traits like Satya are to be combined.

The same (Satya Vidya is taught in both places), since (attributes like) Satya (appear in both).

### 3.3.39

<vt>Combination of Traits in Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya</vt>  
ॐ कामादितरत्र तत्र च आयतनादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ ३।३।३९॥

*<vt>Combination of Traits in Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya</vt>  
oṃ kāmāditaratra tatra ca āyatanādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.39..*

Traits like (true) desire (mentioned in the Chandogya) are to be added to the other (the Brihadaranyaka), and those mentioned there added here, since the abode and so on (are the same).

(Qualities like true) desire (given in the Chhandogya Upanishad are to be inserted) in the other (the Brihadaranyaka), and (those in the Brihadaranyaka likewise in the Chhandogya), since the abode and so on (are the same in both).

### 3.3.40

<vt>Agnihotra to Prana</vt>  
ॐ आदारादलोपः ॐ ॥ ३।३।४०॥

*<vt>Agnihotra to Prana</vt>  
oṃ ādārādalopaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.40..*

(The Agnihotra to Prana) cannot be omitted, given the respect shown (in the Upanishad).

Given the respect (the Sruti shows to the Pranagnihotra), this (act) cannot be omitted (even when eating is omitted).

### 3.3.41

ॐ उपस्थितेस्तद्वचनात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।४१॥

*oṃ upasthitestadvacanāt oṃ .. 3.3.41..*

When food is present, the Agnihotra is to be performed from that very food, for so (the Upanishad) declares.

When eating takes place, (the Pranagnihotra is to be performed) from that (the food first eaten), for so (the Sruti) declares.

### 3.3.42

<vt>Meditations Connected with Rites Are Not Obligatory</vt>  
ॐ तन्निर्धारणार्थनियमस्तद्दृष्टेर्पृथग्ध्यप्रतिबन्धः फलम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।४२॥

*<vt>Meditations Connected with Rites Are Not Obligatory</vt>  
oṃ tannirdhāraṇārthaniyamastaddṛṣṭerpṛthagdhyapratibandhaḥ phalam oṃ .. 3.3.42..*

No binding rule applies to that (meditations always being tied to rites), as the Upanishad makes plain, since a meditation has its own result: removing what hinders a rite.

There is no rule making that (Upasanas tied to certain sacrifices) inviolable, as (the Sruti itself) shows, for (the Upasanas) have a separate fruit: non-obstruction (of the sacrifice's results).

### 3.3.43

<vt>Meditations on Prana and Vayu</vt>  
ॐ प्रदानवदेव हि तदुक्तम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।४३॥

*<vt>Meditations on Prana and Vayu</vt>  
oṃ pradānavadeva hi taduktam oṃ .. 3.3.43..*

(The meditations on Prana and Vayu or Air are to be kept apart), just as with offerings, as Jaimini has stated.

As with the offerings, (Vayu and Prana must be held apart). (The Purvamimamsa Sutra) has explained this.

### 3.3.44

<vt>Fires in Agni-Rahasya Not Parts of Sacrifice</vt>  
ॐ लिङ्गभूयस्त्वात्तद्धि बलीयस्तदपि ॐ ॥ ३।३।४४॥

*<vt>Fires in Agni-Rahasya Not Parts of Sacrifice</vt>  
oṃ liṅgabhūyastvāttaddhi balīyastadapi oṃ .. 3.3.44..*

The fires (of mind, speech, etc. in the Agni-rahasya) are not parts of any rite, given the many indicatory marks, which outweigh the context. Jaimini said this too.

Given the majority of indicatory marks, (the fires of mind, speech, etc. in the Agnirahasya of the Vajasaneyins are not part of the sacrifice), for the indicatory mark is stronger (than the context or general subject matter). (Jaimini in the Purvamimamsa Sutras) has also explained this.

### 3.3.45

ॐ पूर्वविकल्पः प्रकरणात्स्यात्क्रियामानसवत् ॐ ॥ ३।३।४५॥

*oṃ pūrvavikalpaḥ prakaraṇātsyātkriyāmānasavat oṃ .. 3.3.45..*

On the strength of the context, the conceptual fires are alternatives to the actual fire enjoined earlier. They make up a rite, like the imaginary drinking (of Soma juice).

(The fires of the previous Sutra are) alternative forms of the one mentioned first (the actual sacrificial fire), given the context. (They) should be part of the sacrifice, like the imaginary drink or the Manasa-cup.

### 3.3.46

ॐ अतिदेशाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।३।४६॥

*oṃ atideśācca oṃ .. 3.3.46..*

And (this conclusion is supported) by the extended application.

And because of the extension (of the actual fire's attributes to these imaginary fires).

### 3.3.47

ॐ विद्यैव तु निर्धारणात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।४७॥

*oṃ vidyaiva tu nirdhāraṇāt oṃ .. 3.3.47..*

The fires rather make up only a meditation, for so it is settled (in the Vedas).

But (the fires) rather make up the Vidya, for (the Sruti) asserts it.

### 3.3.48

ॐ दर्शनाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।३।४८॥

*oṃ darśanācca oṃ .. 3.3.48..*

And because of the indicatory mark found.

And because (indicatory marks of that are) seen (in the text).

### 3.3.49

ॐ श्रुत्यादिबलीयस्त्वाच्च न बाधः ॐ ॥ ३।३।४९॥

*oṃ śrutyādibalīyastvācca na bādhaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.49..*

Moreover, the view (that the fires make up a meditation) cannot be set aside, given the greater authority of express statement and the like.

(The view that the Agnis or fires form an independent Vidya) cannot be refuted, given the greater force of the Sruti and the rest.

### 3.3.50

ॐ अनुबन्धदिभ्यह् प्रज्नन्तरप्रिथक्त्ववत् द्रिस्ह्तस्छ तदुक्तम् ॥ ३।३।५०॥

*oṃ Anubandhadibhyah prajnantaraprithaktvavat drishtascha taduktam .. 3.3.50..*

Being linked with the mind, and for similar reasons, the mental fires are independent, as other meditations are. Sacrifices too are seen to be treated as independent (regardless of context), as Jaimini pointed out.

Because of the connection and so on, (the fires built of mind etc. form an independent Vidya), just as other Vidyas (like the Sandilya Vidya) are separate. And (a sacrifice is) seen (to be treated as independent despite the context). (The Purvamimamsa Sutras by Jaimini) have explained this.

### 3.3.51

ॐ न सामान्यादप्य् उपलब्धेर्मृत्यु वन्नहि लोकापत्तिः ॐ ॥ ३।३।५१॥

*oṃ na sāmānyādapyupalabdhermṛtyuvannahi lokāpattiḥ oṃ .. 3.3.51..*

Not even similarity can make the mental fires subservient to rites, since they are seen to serve human needs, as with death; for the world does not become fire merely by resembling it.

Despite the resemblance (of the fires to the imaginary drink, they do) not (form part of the sacrificial act), because it is seen (from the reasons given and from the Sruti that they form an independent Vidya), as with death; for the world does not become (fire because it resembles fire in some points).

### 3.3.52

ॐ परेण च शब्दस्य ताद्विध्यं भूयस् त्वात्त्वनुबन्धः ॐ ॥ ३।३।५२॥

*oṃ pareṇa ca śabdasya tādvidhyaṃ bhūyastvāttvanubandhaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.52..*

From the later Brahmana text too, it is known that the scripture intends that (prescription of a meditation). The link with fire arises from the many attributes of fire to be imagined here.

The later (Brahmana) also shows that the text (under discussion) is of this kind (enjoining an independent Vidya). But the connection (of the imagined Agnis or fires with actual fire arises) from the abundance (of the latter's attributes imagined in these fires).

### 3.3.53

<vt>The Self Distinct from Body</vt>  
ॐ एक आत्मनः शरीरे भावात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।५३॥

*<vt>The Self Distinct from Body</vt>  
oṃ eka ātmanaḥ śarīre bhāvāt oṃ .. 3.3.53..*

Some deny the existence of the soul, because its existence depends on the existence of the body.

Some (maintain there is no) separate self (besides the body), because (the self) exists only where a body is.

### 3.3.54

ॐ व्यतिरेकस्तद् भावभावित्वान्न तूपलब्धिवत् ॐ ॥ ३।३।५४॥

*oṃ vyatirekastadbhāvabhāvitvānna tūpalabdhivat oṃ .. 3.3.54..*

But this is not so. There is a distinction (between soul and body), since consciousness may be absent even when the body is present, as with perception.

But not (so). A self or soul separate (from the body does exist), because (Consciousness) is absent even when the body is there (after death), as with cognition or perceptive consciousness.

### 3.3.55

<vt>Meditations Connected with Accessories of Rites</vt>  
ॐ अङ्गावबद्धास्तु न शाखासु हि प्रतिवेदम् ॐ ॥ ३।३।५५॥

*<vt>Meditations Connected with Accessories of Rites</vt>  
oṃ aṅgāvabaddhāstu na śākhāsu hi prativedam oṃ .. 3.3.55..*

The meditations tied to the accessories of rites are not confined to the branches of the Vedas where they occur; they are to be adopted in all (branches of) the Vedas.

But (the Upasanas or meditations connected with parts of sacrificial acts are) not (restricted) to (particular) Sakhas of (their) Veda, (but apply to all its Sakhas, since the same Upasana is described in all).

### 3.3.56

ॐ मन्त्रादिवद्वाऽविरोधः ॐ ॥ ३।३।५६॥

*oṃ mantrādivadvā'virodhaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.56..*

Or rather (they are to be adopted in other branches) like the mantras; (so) there is no contradiction.

Or else there is no contradiction (here), as with Mantras and the like.

### 3.3.57

<vt>Meditation on Vaisvanara as a Whole</vt>  
ॐ भूम्नः क्रतुवज्ज्यायस्त्वं तथा च दर्शयति ॐ ॥ ३।३।५७॥

*<vt>Meditation on Vaisvanara as a Whole</vt>  
oṃ bhūmnaḥ kratuvajjyāyastvaṃ tathā ca darśayati oṃ .. 3.3.57..*

Meditation on the whole has greater importance, as with sacrifices. So the Upanishad shows.

Importance (is given to meditation) on the entire form (of Vaisvanara), as with sacrifice; so (the Sruti) shows.

### 3.3.58

<vt>When Meditations Differ</vt>  
ॐ नानाशब्दादिभेदात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।५८॥

*<vt>When Meditations Differ</vt>  
oṃ nānāśabdādibhedāt oṃ .. 3.3.58..*

Meditations differ when their terminology and the like differ.

(The Vidyas are) separate, since their words and the like differ.

### 3.3.59

<vt>Alternative Meditations</vt>  
ॐ विकल्पो विशिष्टफलत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।५९॥

*<vt>Alternative Meditations</vt>  
oṃ vikalpo viśiṣṭaphalatvāt oṃ .. 3.3.59..*

Any one meditation (may be taken as) an alternative to the others, since their result is the same.

There is a choice (among the several Vidyas), since (all of them) have the same result.

### 3.3.60

<vt>Meditations Yielding Worldly Results</vt>  
ॐ काम्यास्तु यथाकामं समुच्चीयेरन्न वा पूर्वहेत्वभावात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।६०॥

*<vt>Meditations Yielding Worldly Results</vt>  
oṃ kāmyāstu yathākāmaṃ samuccīyeranna vā pūrvahetvabhāvāt oṃ .. 3.3.60..*

Meditations (based on symbols and) undertaken to fulfill worldly desires may be combined or not, as one chooses, since the earlier reason (sameness of result) is absent.

But Vidyas for particular desires may be combined or not, according to one's wishes, since the reason (given in the previous Sutra) is absent.

### 3.3.61

<vt>Meditations Based on Subsidiaries</vt>  
ॐ अङ्गेषु यथाश्रयभावः ॐ ॥ ३।३।६१॥

*<vt>Meditations Based on Subsidiaries</vt>  
oṃ aṅgeṣu yathāśrayabhāvaḥ oṃ .. 3.3.61..*

Meditations based on the subsidiaries (of rites) have the same standing as their bases.

Meditations connected with members (of sacrificial acts) follow (the members) to which they are connected.

### 3.3.62

ॐ शिष्टेश्च ॐ ॥ ३।३।६२॥

*oṃ śiṣṭeśca oṃ .. 3.3.62..*

The Vedas enjoin them, so too the meditations are to be combined.

The Sruti also commands it.

### 3.3.63

ॐ समाहारात् ॐ ॥ ३।३।६३॥

*oṃ samāhārāt oṃ .. 3.3.63..*

One meditation is rectified by another; this indicatory mark shows they are to be combined.

Because of the rectification.

### 3.3.64

ॐ गुणसाधारण्यश्रुतेश्च ॐ ॥ ३।३।६४॥

*oṃ guṇasādhāraṇyaśruteśca oṃ .. 3.3.64..*

The Upanishads declare Om, an accessory of Vedic rites, common to all the Vedas; so the meditations based on it must co-exist.

The Sruti also declares 'OM,' a common feature (of the Udgitha Vidya), to be shared by all the Vedas.

### 3.3.65

ॐ न वा तत्सहभावश्रुतेः ॐ ॥ ३।३।६५॥

*oṃ na vā tatsahabhāvaśruteḥ oṃ .. 3.3.65..*

No: the meditations are not to be combined, since the Upanishads do not say so.

No: the Sruti does not state that (meditations connected with members of sacrificial acts) go together, so they are not combined.

### 3.3.66

ॐ दर्शनाच्च ॐ ॥ ३।३।६६॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु तृतीयाध्यायस्य तृतीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ darśanācca oṃ .. 3.3.66.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu tṛtīyādhyāyasya tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

The Upanishad shows the contrary, so combining is not obligatory.

The Sruti (scripture) also says so (shows it).

### 3.4.1

<vt>Knowledge not a Subsidiary of Rites</vt>  
ॐ पुरुषार्थोऽतः शब्दादिति बादरायणः ॐ ॥ ३.४.१॥

*<vt>Knowledge not a Subsidiary of Rites</vt>  
oṃ puruṣārtho'taḥ śabdāditi bādarāyaṇaḥ oṃ .. 3.4.1..*

Liberation comes from this (knowledge of the Self presented in the Upanishads), Badarayana holds, because the Vedic texts say so.

The sage Baadarayana holds that from this (Brahma Vidya or Brahma Jnana) comes man's purpose, the chief object of his pursuit, because the scriptures say so.

### 3.4.2

ॐ शेषत्वात्पुरुषार्थवादो यथाऽन्येष्विति जैमिनिः ॐ ॥ ३.४.२॥

*oṃ śeṣatvātpuruṣārthavādo yathā'nyeṣviti jaiminiḥ oṃ .. 3.4.2..*

Jaimini holds that, because the Self is subservient in rites and the like, stating the result of knowledge merely glorifies the agent, as happens elsewhere.

Jaimini's opinion: since (the self) is supplementary (to sacrificial acts), (the fruits of the Knowledge of the Self) only praise the agent, as in other cases.

### 3.4.3

ॐ आचारदर्शनात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.३॥

*oṃ ācāradarśanāt oṃ .. 3.4.3..*

What is revealed about the behaviour (of knowers of Brahman) confirms this.

The scriptures show such conduct (in men of realisation).

### 3.4.4

ॐ तच्छ्रुतेः ॐ ॥ ३.४.४॥

*oṃ tacchruteḥ oṃ .. 3.4.4..*

The Upanishad says so.

Scripture states it directly (that knowledge of Brahman is subordinate to sacrificial acts).

### 3.4.5

ॐ समन्वारम्भणात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.५॥

*oṃ samanvārambhaṇāt oṃ .. 3.4.5..*

Both knowledge and work follow the Self (when it transmigrates).

The two (knowledge and work) accompany (the departing soul to yield the fruits of actions).

### 3.4.6

ॐ तद्वतो विधानात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.६॥

*oṃ tadvato vidhānāt oṃ .. 3.4.6..*

Rites are enjoined for one who has that (knowledge of the Vedas).

(The scriptures) enjoin (works) only for such as grasp the purport of the Vedas.

### 3.4.7

ॐ नियमाच्च ॐ ॥ ३.४.७॥

*oṃ niyamācca oṃ .. 3.4.7..*

The restrictive texts also show this.

The prescribed rules also show this.

### 3.4.8

ॐ अधिकोपदेशात्तु बादरायणस्यैवं तद्दर्शनात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.८॥

*oṃ adhikopadeśāttu bādarāyaṇasyaivaṃ taddarśanāt oṃ .. 3.4.8..*

Badarayana's view stands firm, for the supreme Self is taught to be greater even (than the agent); the Upanishads reveal this.

Baadarayana's view is valid, because (the scriptures) teach (the Supreme Self to be) other (than the agent), as scriptural passages show.

### 3.4.9

ॐ तुल्यं तु दर्शनम् ॐ ॥ ३.४.९॥

*oṃ tulyaṃ tu darśanam oṃ .. 3.4.9..*

The Upanishadic statement (of conduct) is equal evidence (that knowledge is not subservient to religious acts).

The statements of the Sruti support both views equally.

### 3.4.10

ॐ असार्वत्रिकी ॐ ॥ ३.४.१०॥

*oṃ asārvatrikī oṃ .. 3.4.10..*

That declaration is not universal.

(The scriptural declaration cited in Sutra 4) does not apply to all cases.

### 3.4.11

ॐ विभागः शतवत् ॐ ॥ ३.४.११॥

*oṃ vibhāgaḥ śatavat oṃ .. 3.4.11..*

Knowledge and action are to be apportioned, like a hundred things.

Knowledge and work are divided, as when a hundred is shared (between two persons).

### 3.4.12

ॐ अध्ययनमात्रवतः ॐ ॥ ३.४.१२॥

*oṃ adhyayanamātravataḥ oṃ .. 3.4.12..*

(Engaging in religious actions is prescribed) only for one who has merely recited the Vedas.

(The scriptures enjoin work) on those who have only read the Vedas.

### 3.4.13

ॐ नाविशेषात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.१३॥

*oṃ nāviśeṣāt oṃ .. 3.4.13..*

(The restrictive texts) do not apply (to the man of knowledge), since the restriction specifies no one.

With nothing specified, (the rule does) not (specially apply to the one who knows, that is, a Jnani).

### 3.4.14

ॐ स्तुतयेऽनुमतिर्वा ॐ ॥ ३.४.१४॥

*oṃ stutaye'numatirvā oṃ .. 3.4.14..*

Or rather, the consent given to do religious acts serves to glorify knowledge.

Or rather, the permission (to work) exists to glorify (knowledge).

### 3.4.15

ॐ कामकारेण चैके ॐ ॥ ३.४.१५॥

*oṃ kāmakāreṇa caike oṃ .. 3.4.15..*

Some, moreover, refrain from (religious) work by personal choice.

And some, as they pleased, (have given up all works).

### 3.4.16

ॐ उपमर्दं च ॐ ॥ ३.४.१६॥

*oṃ upamardaṃ ca oṃ .. 3.4.16..*

(Knowledge), moreover, brings the destruction (of the whole world).

(Scripture also teaches that knowledge brings about) the destruction (of all qualifications for work).

### 3.4.17

ॐ ऊर्ध्वरेतस्सु च शब्दे हि ॐ ॥ ३.४.१७॥

*oṃ ūrdhvaretassu ca śabde hi oṃ .. 3.4.17..*

Knowledge belongs to monks too, for the Vedas mention them.

(Knowledge also belongs) to those vowed to lifelong celibacy, because scripture (mentions that stage of life).

### 3.4.18

<vt>Sannyasa Prescribed by Scriptures</vt>  
ॐ परामर्शं जैमिनिरचोदना चापवदिति हि ॐ ॥ ३.४.१८॥

*<vt>Sannyasa Prescribed by Scriptures</vt>  
oṃ parāmarśaṃ jaiminiracodanā cāpavaditi hi oṃ .. 3.4.18..*

Jaimini holds that this only alludes to other stages and is no injunction, since scripture condemns them.

Jaimini (considers that texts mentioning the stages of life with obligatory celibacy only) refer (to those stages) and are not injunctions, because other (scriptural texts) condemn (those stages).

### 3.4.19

ॐ अनुष्ठेयं बादरायणः साम्यश्रुतेः ॐ ॥ ३.४.१९॥

*oṃ anuṣṭheyaṃ bādarāyaṇaḥ sāmyaśruteḥ oṃ .. 3.4.19..*

Badarayana holds that the other orders of life must also be observed, since Vedic texts speak of all stages equally.

Baadarayana (holds that Sannyasa) too must be undergone, because the (quoted) text refers equally to all four Ashramas or stages of life.

### 3.4.20

ॐ विधिर्वा धारणवत् ॐ ॥ ३.४.२०॥

*oṃ vidhirvā dhāraṇavat oṃ .. 3.4.20..*

Or rather, it is an injunction, as with holding the sacrificial fuel.

Or rather, (this text contains an) injunction, as with carrying (the sacrificial wood).

### 3.4.21

<vt>Injunctions for Meditation Not Eulogistic</vt>  
ॐ स्तुतिमात्रमुपादानादिति चेन्नापूर्वत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.२१॥

*<vt>Injunctions for Meditation Not Eulogistic</vt>  
oṃ stutimātramupādānāditi cennāpūrvatvāt oṃ .. 3.4.21..*

If texts (on Udgitha and the like) are called merely eulogistic because they are accepted as subservient to ritual, we say no, because of their extraordinary character.

If (texts such as the one on the Udgitha) are called mere glorifications because they refer (to parts of sacrifices), (we say) no, because of the newness (of what they teach, if taken as injunctions).

### 3.4.22

ॐ भावशब्दाच्च ॐ ॥ ३.४.२२॥

*oṃ bhāvaśabdācca oṃ .. 3.4.22..*

Words with injunctive meaning occur as well, (so there must be injunctions).

There are also words that express injunction.

### 3.4.23

<vt>Upanishadic Stories</vt>  
ॐ पारिप्लवार्था इति चेन्न विशेषितत्वात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.२३॥

*<vt>Upanishadic Stories</vt>  
oṃ pāriplavārthā iti cenna viśeṣitatvāt oṃ .. 3.4.23..*

If (the Upanishadic stories) are said to serve (the ritual use called) Pariplava, we say no, for the stories used in Pariplava have been specified.

If (the Upanishadic stories) are said to serve Pariplava (only, we say) no, because (the Sruti) specifies (certain stories for this purpose).

### 3.4.24

ॐ तथा चैकवाक्योपबन्धात् ॐ ॥ ३.४.२४॥

*oṃ tathā caikavākyopabandhāt oṃ .. 3.4.24..*

Because (the stories) thus connect (with the meditations) through unity of idea, (they serve to illuminate the adjacent knowledge).

So (they serve to illustrate the nearest Vidyas), being joined as one coherent whole.

### 3.4.25

<vt>Sannyasins Free from Rituals</vt>  
ॐ अत एव चाग्नीन्धनाद्यनपेक्षा ॐ ॥ ३.४.२५॥

*<vt>Sannyasins Free from Rituals</vt>  
oṃ ata eva cāgnīndhanādyanapekṣā oṃ .. 3.4.25..*

For that same reason (the Sannyasin) needs no lighting of fire or other such rites.

So there is no need to light the fire and so on.

### 3.4.26

<vt>Rituals etc., Needed for Knowledge</vt>  
ॐ सर्वापेक्षा च यज्ञादिश्रुतेः अश्ववत् ॐ ॥ ३.४.२६॥

*<vt>Rituals etc., Needed for Knowledge</vt>  
oṃ sarvāpekṣā ca yajñādiśruteḥ aśvavat oṃ .. 3.4.26..*

Because the Upanishads sanction sacrifices and the like, all religious activities are also necessary, as with a horse (and what it is suited for).

### 4.2.3

<vt>Prana Merges into the soul</vt>  
ॐ तन्मनः प्राण उत्तरात् ॐ ॥ ४.२.३॥

*<vt>Prana Merges into the soul</vt>  
oṃ tanmanaḥ prāṇa uttarāt oṃ .. 4.2.3..*

That mind merges in the vital force, as the subsequent text reveals.

That mind merges in Prana, as is seen from the subsequent clause of the cited Sruti.

### 4.2.4

ॐ सोऽध्यक्षे तदुपगमादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ ४.२.४॥

*oṃ so'dhyakṣe tadupagamādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 4.2.4..*

The vital force is known to be withdrawn into the ruler (the individual Self), from such facts as its approaching that Self at death.

That Prana merges in the ruler (the individual soul or Jiva), because of statements such as those about the Pranas coming to it.

### 4.2.5

ॐ भूतेषु तच्छ्रुतेः ॐ ॥ ४.२.५॥

*oṃ bhūteṣu tacchruteḥ oṃ .. 4.2.5..*

The soul comes to stay among the elements, as the Upanishads declare.

The Jiva with the Pranas merges in the subtle elements, as is seen from the Sruti.

### 4.2.6

ॐ नैकस्मिन् दर्शयतो हि ॐ ॥ ४.२.६॥

*oṃ naikasmin darśayato hi oṃ .. 4.2.6..*

The soul does not come to stay in a single element, for both Upanishads and Smritis show otherwise.

The soul with Prana does not merge in one element only, as both Sruti and Smriti declare.

### 4.2.7

<vt>Departure of the Enlightened and the Unenlightened</vt>  
ॐ समाना चासृत्युपक्रमादमृतत्वं चानुपोष्य ॐ ॥ ४.२.७॥

*<vt>Departure of the Enlightened and the Unenlightened</vt>  
oṃ samānā cāsṛtyupakramādamṛtatvaṃ cānupoṣya oṃ .. 4.2.7..*

The mode of departure at death is the same for the knower of the qualified Brahman and the ignorant person, up to the start of the path of the gods. The immortality spoken of is attained without burning away ignorance.

The mode of departure at death is common to the knower of Saguna Brahman and the ignorant, up to the start of their paths. The immortality of the knower of Saguna Brahman is only relative, since ignorance has not been burnt.

### 4.2.8

<vt>Relative Merger of Fire etc.</vt>  
ॐ तदपीतेः संसारव्यपदेशात् ॐ ॥ ४.२.८॥

*<vt>Relative Merger of Fire etc.</vt>  
oṃ tadapīteḥ saṃsāravyapadeśāt oṃ .. 4.2.8..*

That group of elements, counting from fire, lasts until complete liberation, since the transmigratory state is declared to continue till then.

That fine body lasts until Brahman is attained through knowledge, because the scriptures declare the state of relative existence to continue till then.

### 4.2.9

ॐ सूक्ष्मं प्रमाणतश्च तथोपलब्धेः ॐ ॥ ४.२.९॥

*oṃ sūkṣmaṃ pramāṇataśca tathopalabdheḥ oṃ .. 4.2.9..*

That fire, like the other elements, is minute in nature and size, for it is observed to be so.

This fine body is subtle in nature and size, as is observed.

### 4.2.10

ॐ नोपमर्देनातः ॐ ॥ ४.२.१०॥

*oṃ nopamardenātaḥ oṃ .. 4.2.10..*

For this very reason, the subtle body is not destroyed even when the gross body is.

So this subtle body is not destroyed by the destruction of the gross body.

### 4.2.11

ॐ अस्यैव चोपपत्तेरूष्मा ॐ ॥ ४.२.११॥

*oṃ asyaiva copapatterūṣmā oṃ .. 4.2.11..*

This warmth certainly belongs to the subtle body; that is what makes sense.

Bodily heat belongs to this subtle body alone, for only this is possible.

### 4.2.12

<vt>No Departure for a Knower of Brahman</vt>  
ॐ प्रतिषेधादिति चेन्न शारीरात् ॐ ॥ ४.२.१२॥

*<vt>No Departure for a Knower of Brahman</vt>  
oṃ pratiṣedhāditi cenna śārīrāt oṃ .. 4.2.12..*

If it is argued that the organs of the knower do not leave the body because scripture denies it, the opponent replies: not so, since the denial concerns departure from the individual soul.

If it is said that the Pranas of one who knows Brahman do not depart because the Sruti denies it, we say no: the scripture denies their departure from the individual soul, not from the body.

### 4.2.13

ॐ स्पष्टो ह्येकेषाम् ॐ ॥ ४.२.१३॥

*oṃ spaṣṭo hyekeṣām oṃ .. 4.2.13..*

This is not so, for followers of one recension clearly deny the soul's departure.

The denial of the soul's departure is clear in the texts of some schools.

### 4.2.14

ॐ स्मर्यते च ॐ ॥ ४.२.१४॥

*oṃ smaryate ca oṃ .. 4.2.14..*

The Smriti says so too.

Smriti says the same.

### 4.2.15

<vt>the Organs of the Knower Merge in Brahman</vt>  
ॐ तानि परे तथा ह्याह ॐ ॥ ४.२.१५॥

*<vt>the Organs of the Knower Merge in Brahman</vt>  
oṃ tāni pare tathā hyāha oṃ .. 4.2.15..*

Those organs merge in the supreme Brahman, as the Upanishad declares.

Those Pranas and elements merge in the Supreme Brahman, as scripture says.

### 4.2.16

<vt>Absolute Absorption of the Constituents</vt>  
ॐ अविभागो वचनात् ॐ ॥ ४.२.१६॥

*<vt>Absolute Absorption of the Constituents</vt>  
oṃ avibhāgo vacanāt oṃ .. 4.2.16..*

Absolute non-distinction from Brahman comes about, on the authority of the scriptural declaration.

The merged parts become absolutely non-distinct from Brahman, according to the statement of the scriptures.

### 4.2.17

<vt>Departure of one who knows the Qualified Brahman</vt>  
ॐ तदोकोऽग्रज्वलनं तत्प्रकाशितद्वारो विद्यासामर्थ्यात्तच्छेषगत्यनुस्मृतियोगाच्च हार्दानुगृहीताः शताधिकया ॐ ॥ ४.२.१७॥

*<vt>Departure of one who knows the Qualified Brahman</vt>  
oṃ tadoko'grajvalanaṃ tatprakāśitadvāro vidyāsāmarthyāttaccheṣagatyanusmṛtiyogācca hārdānugṛhītāḥ śatādhikayā oṃ .. 4.2.17..*

When the soul of one who has realized the qualified Brahman is about to depart, the top of the heart lights up. With that door lit by this light, and by the favor of Him who dwells in the heart, the soul leaves through the hundred and first nerve, owing to the power of knowledge and the fitness of constantly thinking of the path that is part of that knowledge.

When the soul of a knower of Saguna Brahman is about to leave the body, the front of its abode, the heart, lights up, and the door of its exit is illumined. Owing to the power of knowledge and the application of meditation to the path that is part of that knowledge, the soul, favored by Him in the heart (Brahman), passes upward by the one that exceeds a hundred (the hundred and first Nadi).

### 4.2.18

<vt>The Soul follows the Rays of the Sun</vt>  
ॐ रश्म्यनुसारी ॐ ॥ ४.२.१८॥

*<vt>The Soul follows the Rays of the Sun</vt>  
oṃ raśmyanusārī oṃ .. 4.2.18..*

The soul of the knower proceeds by following the rays of the sun.

The soul of a knower of Saguna Brahman, at death, follows the rays of the sun.

### 4.2.19

ॐ निशि न इति चेत्, न, सम्बन्धस्य यावद्देहभावित्वाद्, दर्शयति च ॐ ॥ ४.२.१९॥

*oṃ niśi na iti cet, na, sambandhasya yāvaddehabhāvitvād, darśayati ca oṃ .. 4.2.19..*

If it is argued that a soul departing at night cannot travel along the rays, not so: the connection between nerve and rays lasts as long as the body does, as the Upanishad reveals.

If it is said that the soul does not follow the rays at night, we say no, because the connection of Nadis and rays continues as long as the body lasts; the Sruti also declares this.

### 4.2.20

<vt>Soul's Journey during the Sun's Southern Course</vt>  
ॐ अतश्चायनेऽपि दक्षिणे ॐ ॥ ४।२।२०॥

*<vt>Soul's Journey during the Sun's Southern Course</vt>  
oṃ ataścāyane'pi dakṣiṇe oṃ .. 4.2.20..*

For this same reason, the soul gets the result of knowledge even when departing during the sun's southern course.

For the same reason, the departed soul follows the rays during the sun's southern course as well.

### 4.2.21

ॐ योगिनः प्रति स्मर्यते स्मार्ते चैते ॐ ॥ ४।२।२१॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु चतुर्थाध्यायस्य द्वितीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ yoginaḥ prati smaryate smārte caite oṃ .. 4.2.21.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu caturthādhyāyasya dvitīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

These times and so on are mentioned in the Smriti for Yogins; and these paths of Samkhya and Yoga are mentioned in the Smritis, not the Vedas.

Smriti records these times or details with reference to the Yogins, and these two, Yoga and Sankhya, are classed as Smritis only.

### 4.3.1

<vt>Only One Path to the World of Brahman</vt>  
ॐ अर्चिरादिना तत्प्रथितेः ॐ ॥ ४.३.१॥

*<vt>Only One Path to the World of Brahman</vt>  
oṃ arcirādinā tatprathiteḥ oṃ .. 4.3.1..*

The soul travels along the path beginning with flame, as is well known.

After death, the departed soul of the knower of Saguna Brahman travels to Brahmaloka on the path connected with light, as is well known from the Sruti.

### 4.3.2

<vt>The Departing Soul Reaches Air after Year</vt>  
ॐ वायुशब्दादविशेषविशेषाभ्याम् ॐ ॥ ४.३.२॥

*<vt>The Departing Soul Reaches Air after Year</vt>  
oṃ vāyuśabdādaviśeṣaviśeṣābhyām oṃ .. 4.3.2..*

The soul of the knower of the qualified Brahman goes from the year to air, because of the absence and presence of specification.

The departed soul of a knower of Saguna Brahman goes from the deity of the year to the deity of the air, because of the absence and presence of specification.

### 4.3.3

<vt>The Soul proceeds from Lightning to Varuna</vt>  
ॐ तटितोऽधि वरुणः संबन्धात् ॐ ॥ ४.३.३॥

*<vt>The Soul proceeds from Lightning to Varuna</vt>  
oṃ taṭito'dhi varuṇaḥ saṃbandhāt oṃ .. 4.3.3..*

Varuna is placed after lightning, since both are connected with water (cloud).

After reaching the deity of lightning, the soul reaches Varuna, because the two are connected.

### 4.3.4

<vt>Guiding Deities</vt>  
ॐ आतिवाहिकस्तल्लिङ्गात् ॐ ॥ ४.३.४॥

*<vt>Guiding Deities</vt>  
oṃ ātivāhikastalliṅgāt oṃ .. 4.3.4..*

Flame and the rest are conducting deities, as the indicative mark shows.

These are deities who conduct the soul on the path of the gods, as indicatory marks show.

### 4.3.5

ॐ उभयव्यामोहात्तत्सिद्धेः ॐ ॥ ४.३.५॥

*oṃ ubhayavyāmohāttatsiddheḥ oṃ .. 4.3.5..*

That is established, since both traveler and path are then unconscious.

That these texts mean deities or divine guides, personal conductors, is established, because both the path and the traveler become unconscious.

### 4.3.6

ॐ वैद्युतेनैव ततस्तच्छ्रुतेः ॐ ॥ ४.३.६॥

*oṃ vaidyutenaiva tatastacchruteḥ oṃ .. 4.3.6..*

From there, they are guided by the same being who comes to lightning, for the Upanishad speaks of him.

From there the souls are guided by the very same superhuman person who comes to lightning, as is known from the Sruti.

### 4.3.7

<vt>The Path Leads to the Conditioned Brahman</vt>  
ॐ कार्यं बादरिरस्य गत्युपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ ४.३.७॥

*<vt>The Path Leads to the Conditioned Brahman</vt>  
oṃ kāryaṃ bādarirasya gatyupapatteḥ oṃ .. 4.3.7..*

Badari holds that souls are led to the conditioned Brahman, since it alone can reasonably be the goal.

The sage Baadari holds that the departed souls are led to the Karya Brahman, or Hiranyagarbha, or Saguna Brahman, since that can be the goal of their journey.

### 4.3.8

ॐ विशेषितत्वाच्च ॐ ॥ ४.३.८॥

*oṃ viśeṣitatvācca oṃ .. 4.3.8..*

The conditioned Brahman must also be the goal because it is specifically mentioned.

Also because of the qualification concerning this Brahman in another text.

### 4.3.9

ॐ सामीप्यात्तु तद्व्यपदेशः ॐ ॥ ४.३.९॥

*oṃ sāmīpyāttu tadvyapadeśaḥ oṃ .. 4.3.9..*

But the conditioned Brahman bears that name because of its nearness to the absolute Brahman.

But because Saguna Brahman is near to the Supreme Brahman, it is called by that name.

### 4.3.10

ॐ कार्यात्यये तदध्यक्षेण सहातः परमभिधानात् ॐ ॥ ४.३.१०॥

*oṃ kāryātyaye tadadhyakṣeṇa sahātaḥ paramabhidhānāt oṃ .. 4.3.10..*

When the world of the conditioned Brahman finally dissolves, they attain, along with that world's lord, what is higher than the conditioned Brahman, as the Upanishadic declaration shows.

On the dissolution of Brahmaloka, the souls, along with the ruler of that world, attain what is higher, the Supreme Brahman, as the Sruti declares.

### 4.3.11

ॐ स्मृतेश्च ॐ ॥ ४.३.११॥

*oṃ smṛteśca oṃ .. 4.3.11..*

Smriti confirms this as well.

Smriti texts also support this view.

### 4.3.12

ॐ परं जैमिनिर्मुख्यत्वात् ॐ ॥ ४.३.१२॥

*oṃ paraṃ jaiminirmukhyatvāt oṃ .. 4.3.12..*

Jaimini holds that they are led to the supreme Brahman, that being the primary meaning of the word Brahman.

Jaimini holds that the souls are led to the highest Brahman, since that is the primary meaning of the word "Brahman."

### 4.3.13

ॐ दर्शनाच्च ॐ ॥ ४.३.१३॥

*oṃ darśanācca oṃ .. 4.3.13..*

This is so because the Upanishad reveals it.

The Sruti too declares that.

### 4.3.14

ॐ न च कार्ये प्रतिपत्त्यभिसन्धिः ॐ ॥ ४.३.१४॥

*oṃ na ca kārye pratipattyabhisandhiḥ oṃ .. 4.3.14..*

Moreover, the firm resolve to attain is not about the conditioned Brahman.

The desire to attain Brahman cannot concern the Saguna Brahman.

### 4.3.15

<vt>Worship with and without Symbols</vt>  
ॐ अप्रतीकालम्बनान्नयतीति बादरायणरुभयथा च दोषात् तत्क्रतुश्च ॐ ॥ ४.३.१५॥

*<vt>Worship with and without Symbols</vt>  
oṃ apratīkālambanānnayatīti bādarāyaṇarubhayathā ca doṣāt tatkratuśca oṃ .. 4.3.15..*

Badarayana says the superhuman being leads to Brahman only those who do not use symbols in meditation, for this twofold division involves no contradiction, and one becomes what one resolves to be.

Baadarayana holds that the superhuman being leads to Brahmaloka only those who do not resort to a symbol of Brahman in meditation. The twofold relation this implies is no fault, and it rests on the doctrine that as one meditates on Brahman, so one becomes.

### 4.3.16

ॐ विशेषं च दर्शयति ॐ ॥ ४.३.१६॥ इति श्रीमत्कृष्णद्वैपायनकृत ब्रह्मसूत्रेषु चतुर्थाध्यायस्य तृतीयः पादः समाप्तः

*oṃ viśeṣaṃ ca darśayati oṃ .. 4.3.16.. iti śrīmat kṛṣṇadvaipāyanakṛta brahmasūtreṣu caturthādhyāyasya tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ samāptaḥ*

The Upanishad also reveals a distinction in the results of meditations with symbols.

The scripture declares a difference in the case of meditation on symbols.

### 4.4.1

<vt>Nature of Freedom</vt>  
ॐ सम्पद्याविहाय स्वेनशब्दात् ॐ ॥ ४.४.१॥

*<vt>Nature of Freedom</vt>  
oṃ sampadyāvihāya svenaśabdāt oṃ .. 4.4.1..*

On reaching the "highest Light," the soul becomes manifest in its own real nature, as the Upanishad's term "in its own" shows.

When the Jiva, the individual soul, has attained the highest light, its own real nature becomes manifest, as we infer from the word "own."

### 4.4.2

ॐ मुक्तः प्रतिज्ञानात् ॐ ॥ ४.४.२॥

*oṃ muktaḥ pratijñānāt oṃ .. 4.4.2..*

The soul then attains liberation, as the Upanishad declares.

The self whose true nature has become manifest is released, according to the promise of scripture.

### 4.4.3

ॐ आत्मा प्रकरणात् ॐ ॥ ४.४.३॥

*oṃ ātmā prakaraṇāt oṃ .. 4.4.3..*

The Light is the Self, as the context makes obvious.

The light the individual soul enters is the Supreme Self, given the subject matter of the chapter.

### 4.4.4

<vt>Liberated Soul Inseparable from Brahman</vt>  
ॐ अविभागेन दृष्टत्वात् ॐ ॥ ४.४.४॥

*<vt>Liberated Soul Inseparable from Brahman</vt>  
oṃ avibhāgena dṛṣṭatvāt oṃ .. 4.4.4..*

In liberation, the soul exists inseparable from the supreme Self, as the Upanishad shows.

The Jiva in the state of release exists as inseparable from Brahman, as is seen from the scriptures.

### 4.4.5

<vt>Characteristics of the Liberated Soul</vt>  
ॐ ब्राह्मेण जैमिनिरुपन्यासादिभ्यः ॐ ॥ ४.४.५॥

*<vt>Characteristics of the Liberated Soul</vt>  
oṃ brāhmeṇa jaiminirupanyāsādibhyaḥ oṃ .. 4.4.5..*

Jaimini says that references and the like in the Upanishads make it evident that the liberated soul becomes established in the attributes of Brahman.

Jaimini holds that the released soul exists as possessing the attributes of Brahman, on account of the reference and so on.

### 4.4.6

ॐ चितिमात्रेण तदात्मकत्वादित्यौडुलोमिः ॐ ॥ ४.४.६॥

*oṃ citimātreṇa tadātmakatvādityauḍulomiḥ oṃ .. 4.4.6..*

Audulomi says the liberated soul becomes established in consciousness as consciousness itself, that being its true nature.

Audulomi holds that the released soul exists solely as pure consciousness or Intelligence, that being its true nature or essence.

### 4.4.7

ॐ एवमप्युपन्यासात्पूर्वभावादविरोधं बादरायणः ॐ ॥ ४.४.७॥

*oṃ evamapyupanyāsātpūrvabhāvādavirodhaṃ bādarāyaṇaḥ oṃ .. 4.4.7..*

Badarayana says that even so there is no contradiction, since the earlier nature exists, according to Upanishadic reference.

Baadarayana holds that even so there is no contradiction between the two, since the former qualities are admitted to exist on account of reference and so on.

### 4.4.8

<vt>Fulfilment of Desire through Will</vt>  
ॐ सङ्कल्पादेव च तच्छ्रुतेः ॐ ॥ ४.४.८॥

*<vt>Fulfilment of Desire through Will</vt>  
oṃ saṅkalpādeva ca tacchruteḥ oṃ .. 4.4.8..*

The fathers and others come through will alone, for the Upanishad says so.

But liberated souls attain their purpose by mere will, as the scriptures say.

### 4.4.9

ॐ अत एव चानन्याधिपतिः ॐ ॥ ४.४.९॥

*oṃ ata eva cānanyādhipatiḥ oṃ .. 4.4.9..*

For that very reason, no other lord rules a man of knowledge.

For this same reason the released soul has no other Lord.

### 4.4.10

<vt>Body after Reaching Brahma-loka</vt>  
ॐ अभावं बादरिराह ह्येवम् ॐ ॥ ४.४.१०॥

*<vt>Body after Reaching Brahma-loka</vt>  
oṃ abhāvaṃ bādarirāha hyevam oṃ .. 4.4.10..*

Badari holds that one who reaches the Brahma-loka (the world of Brahman) has no body or organs, for the Upanishad says so.

Baadari asserts that liberated souls have no body or organs, for so scripture says.

### 4.4.11

ॐ भावं जैमिनिर्विकल्पाम्नानात् ॐ ॥ ४.४.११॥

*oṃ bhāvaṃ jaiminirvikalpāmnānāt oṃ .. 4.4.11..*

Jaimini holds that body and sense-organs exist after realizing the qualified Brahman, since the Upanishad speaks of option.

Jaimini asserts that the liberated soul has a body and organs, since the scriptures declare such a soul able to assume various forms.

### 4.4.12

ॐ द्वादशाहवदुभयविधं बादरायणोऽतः ॐ ॥ ४.४.१२॥

*oṃ dvādaśāhavadubhayavidhaṃ bādarāyaṇo'taḥ oṃ .. 4.4.12..*

Badarayana therefore considers released souls to be of both kinds, with or without bodies and senses, as with the Dvadasaha (twelve-day) sacrifice.

For this reason Baadarayana holds that the released person is of both kinds, as with the twelve days' sacrifice.

### 4.4.13

ॐ तन्वभावे सन्ध्यवदुपपत्तेः ॐ ॥ ४.४.१३॥

*oṃ tanvabhāve sandhyavadupapatteḥ oṃ .. 4.4.13..*

Without a body, desires can reasonably be fulfilled, as in dreams.

Without a body, desires can be fulfilled as in dreams, this being reasonable.

### 4.4.14

ॐ भावे जाग्रद्वत् ॐ ॥ ४.४.१४॥

*oṃ bhāve jāgradvat oṃ .. 4.4.14..*

With a body, desires are fulfilled just as in the waking state.

Where the body exists, desires are fulfilled as in waking.

### 4.4.15

<vt>Entry into many Bodies</vt>  
ॐ प्रदीपवदावेशः तथा हि दर्शयति ॐ ॥ ४.४.१५॥

*<vt>Entry into many Bodies</vt>  
oṃ pradīpavadāveśaḥ tathā hi darśayati oṃ .. 4.4.15..*

The released soul can animate different bodies, like a lamp, as scripture shows.

The released soul enters several bodies, as a lamp's flame multiplies, for so scripture declares.

### 4.4.16

ॐ स्वाप्ययसम्पत्त्योरन्यतरापेक्षमाविष्कृतं हि ॐ ॥ ४.४.१६॥

*oṃ svāpyayasampattyoranyatarāpekṣamāviṣkṛtaṃ hi oṃ .. 4.4.16..*

The absence of particularized knowledge is declared from one of two standpoints, deep sleep or absolute union; the Upanishad makes this clear.

The absence of all cognition is declared with one of two states in view, deep sleep or absolute union with Brahman, as the scriptures make clear.

### 4.4.17

<vt>Acquisition of Divine Powers</vt>  
ॐ जगद्व्यापारवर्जम् ॐ ॥ ४.४.१७॥

*<vt>Acquisition of Divine Powers</vt>  
oṃ jagadvyāpāravarjam prakaraṇādasannihitatvācca oṃ .. 4.4.17..*

The released soul gains all divine powers except that of running the universe (its creation, continuance, and dissolution), as the context dealing with God and the non-proximity of the individual soul show.

The liberated soul attains all lordly powers except creation and the like, since the Lord is the subject of every text on creation and liberated souls are not mentioned there.

### 4.4.18

ॐ प्रत्यक्षोपदेशादिति चेन्नाधिकारिक मण्डलस्थोक्तेः ॐ ॥ ४।४।१८॥

*oṃ pratyakṣopadeśāditi cennādhikārikamaṇḍalasthokteḥ oṃ .. 4.4.18..*

If it is held that the liberated soul's powers are unlimited because scripture directly declares it, no; the Upanishad speaks of attaining Him, God, who appoints others as lords of the spheres and resides in those spheres.

If it is said that the liberated soul attains absolute powers through the direct teaching of the scriptures, we say no; they declare that it attains Him who entrusts the sun and others with their offices and abides in those spheres.

### 4.4.19

ॐ विकारावर्ति च तथा हि दर्शयति ॐ ॥ ४।४।१९॥

*oṃ vikārāvarti ca tathā hi darśayati oṃ .. 4.4.19..*

The supreme Lord also has another form that does not abide in the effect, as the Upanishad has declared.

The Supreme Lord also has a form beyond all created things, for scripture declares His existence in a twofold form, unmanifest and manifest.

### 4.4.20

ॐ स्थितिमाह दर्शयतश्चैवं प्रत्यक्षानुमाने ॐ ॥ ४।४।२०॥

*oṃ sthitimāha darśayataścaivaṃ pratyakṣānumāne oṃ .. 4.4.20..*

Both Upanishadic and Smriti texts show that the supreme Light is beyond all changing things.

Perception and inference show this too.

### 4.4.21

ॐ भोगमात्रसाम्यलिङ्गाच्च ॐ ॥ ४।४।२१॥

*oṃ bhogamātrasāmyaliṅgācca oṃ .. 4.4.21..*

The Upanishadic indication that experience alone is equal also shows that liberated souls do not gain unfettered powers.

Also because scripture indicates that the liberated soul equals the Lord only with respect to enjoyment.

### 4.4.22

*oṃ anāvṛttiḥ śabdādanāvṛttiḥ śabdāt oṃ .. 4.4.22..*

Released souls do not return, on the strength of the Upanishadic declaration; released souls do not return, on the strength of the Upanishadic declaration.

These liberated souls do not return, as scripture states.
