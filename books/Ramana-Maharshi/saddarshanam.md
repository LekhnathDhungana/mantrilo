# Saddarshanam

### 0

मङ्गलम्  
सत्प्रत्ययाः किं नु विहाय सन्तं  
हृद्येष चिन्तारहितो हृदाख्यः  
कथं स्मरामस्तममेयमेकं  
तस्य स्मृतिस्तत्र दृढैव निष्ठा ॥१॥

*maṅgalam  
satpratyayāḥ kiṃ nu vihāya santaṃ  
hṛdyeṣa cintārahito hṛdākhyaḥ  
kathaṃ smarāmastamameyamekaṃ  
tasya smṛtistatra dṛḍhaiva niṣṭhā ..1..*

Benedictory Verses - Mangalam
Could anyone hold the belief (faith, firm conviction, idea) that something exists if there were nothing that exists?
That which is in the Heart, free of (empty of) thoughts, is what we call the Heart.
How can one think of (remember) that which cannot be measured?
To remember it, then, is simply to abide in it firmly. (Or: Remembering it is nothing but firm abidance in it.)   ..1..

Could a thought of Reality come up if Reality were not there? Reality, without any thought, lives within as the Heart, so how is the Reality we call the Heart to be known? Knowing That is nothing more than being That in the Heart.

### 1

मृत्युञ्जयं मृत्युभियाश्रिताना-  
महंमतिर्मृत्युमुपैति पूर्वम्  
अथ स्वभावादमृतेषु तेषु  
कथं पुनर्मृत्युधियोऽवकाशः ॥२॥

*mṛtyuñjayaṃ mṛtyubhiyāśritānā-  
mahaṃmatirmṛtyumupaiti pūrvam  
atha svabhāvādamṛteṣu teṣu  
kathaṃ punarmṛtyudhiyo'vakāśaḥ ..2..*

When people, afraid of dying, take refuge in the Conqueror of death,
the “I” thought (notion) is the first thing to die.
After that, since they are by their very nature immortal,
what room could there be for any thought of death?   ..2..

Those who dread death and seek shelter at the feet of the deathless, birthless Lord Supreme find that their ego and attachments die. Now deathless themselves, they no longer think about death.

### 2

ग्रन्थारम्भः  
सर्वैर्निदानं जगतोऽहमश्च  
वाच्यः प्रभुः कश्चिदपारशक्तिः  
चित्रेऽत्र लोक्यं च विलोकिता च  
पटः प्रकाशोऽप्यभवत्स एकः ॥१॥

*granthārambhaḥ  
sarvairnidānaṃ jagato'hamaśca  
vācyaḥ prabhuḥ kaścidapāraśaktiḥ  
citre'tra lokyaṃ ca vilokitā ca  
paṭaḥ prakāśo'pyabhavatsa ekaḥ ..1..*

The Text
Everyone must grant that the universe (world) and the “I” have a first cause (original essence),
and that this is the Lord (the powerful Master), one whose power has no limit.
In this varied picture here, the one who sees (the seer), the seen,
the screen and the Light: He, the One, became all of these.  ..1..

Because we know the world, we have to accept one common Source for both, a single Source with the power of seeming many. The picture of names and forms, the person watching, the screen, and the light that makes it visible are all truly He.

### 3

तत्त्वग्रन्थमाला  
आरभ्यते जीवजगत्परात्म-  
तत्त्वाभिधानेन मतं समस्तम्  
इदं त्रयं यावदहंमति स्यात्  
सर्वोत्तमाऽहंमतिशून्यनिष्ठा ॥२॥

*tattvagranthamālā  
ārabhyate jīvajagatparātma-  
tattvābhidhānena mataṃ samastam  
idaṃ trayaṃ yāvadahaṃmati syāt  
sarvottamā'haṃmatiśūnyaniṣṭhā ..2..*

All suppositions (doctrines) start by naming as principles (verities)
the individual, the world and the Supreme Self.
(Or: Naming the individual, the world and the Supreme Self is built into suppositions.)
This triad lasts only as long as the “I”-notion will exist.
Highest of all is abidance empty of the “I”-notion (“I”-assumption).   ..2..

Every creed rests on three entities: the individual, God and the world. Claims such as ‘the One becomes the three’ and ‘the three always stay three’ are made only for as long as the ego survives. The State Supreme is to lose the ‘I’ and remain in the Self.

### 4

सत्यं मृषा वा चिदिदं जडं वा  
दुःखं सुखं वेति मुधा विवादः  
अदृष्टलोका निरहंप्रतीति-  
र्निष्ठाऽविकल्पा परमाऽखिलेष्टा ॥३॥

*satyaṃ mṛṣā vā cididaṃ jaḍaṃ vā  
duḥkhaṃ sukhaṃ veti mudhā vivādaḥ  
adṛṣṭalokā nirahaṃpratīti-  
rniṣṭhā'vikalpā paramā'khileṣṭā ..3..*

Whether this is true or false, conscious or inert,
suffering or happiness: such dispute (quarrelling) serves no purpose (is vain).
The world unseen, no “I”-belief (no going toward the “I”; clear ascertainment of no “I”),
abidance without differentiation (without doubt), the Absolute (the Supreme): this pleases (is dear to) everyone.   ..3..

‘The World is true.’ ‘No, it is a false appearance.’ ‘The World is Mind.’ ‘No, it is not.’ ‘The World is pleasant.’ ‘No, it is not.’ What good does such talk do? Leaving the world alone, knowing the Self, and going past every thought of ‘One’ and ‘Two’: this condition without ego is the goal shared by all.

### 5

सरूपबुद्धिर्जगतीश्वरे च  
सरूपधीरात्मनि यावदस्ति  
अरूपकात्मा यदि कः प्रपश्येत्  
सा दृष्टिरेकाऽनवधिर्हि पूर्णा ॥४॥

*sarūpabuddhirjagatīśvare ca  
sarūpadhīrātmani yāvadasti  
arūpakātmā yadi kaḥ prapaśyet  
sā dṛṣṭirekā'navadhirhi pūrṇā ..4..*

The world and the Lord are ideas (beliefs) of having form
for as long as the idea of having form belongs to the Self (is in oneself).
If the Self has no form, who is there to see?
That vision (eye, wisdom, beholding) is single, without limit, and certainly perfectly full.   ..4..

If the Self has form, then the world and God have form too. If the Self is formless, who could see the form of world and God, and how? Can there be sight or anything to look at without the eye? The Self, the real Eye, is infinite.

### 6

यत्पञ्चकोशात्मकमस्ति देहं  
तदन्तरा किं भुवनं चकास्ति  
देहं विना पञ्चविधं तदेतत्  
पश्यन्ति के वा भुवनं भणन्तु ॥५॥

*yatpañcakośātmakamasti dehaṃ  
tadantarā kiṃ bhuvanaṃ cakāsti  
dehaṃ vinā pañcavidhaṃ tadetat  
paśyanti ke vā bhuvanaṃ bhaṇantu ..5..*

The body has the nature of the five-fold sheaths (lit.: the five-fold sheaths form the nature of the body).
Does the world shine without it?
Without that five-fold body, here and now,
who has ever seen the world? Let them say.  ..5..

The five sheaths make up the body; [3] the word body covers all five. Without the body there is no world. Has anyone without a body ever seen the world?

### 7

शब्दादिरूपं भुवनं समस्तं  
शब्दादिसत्तेन्द्रियवृत्तिभास्या  
सत्तेन्द्रियाणां मनसो वशे स्यात्  
मनोमयं तद्भुवनं वदामः ॥६॥

*śabdādirūpaṃ bhuvanaṃ samastaṃ  
śabdādisattendriyavṛttibhāsyā  
sattendriyāṇāṃ manaso vaśe syāt  
manomayaṃ tadbhuvanaṃ vadāmaḥ ..6..*

The entire world has the form of sound and the rest.
The existence of sound and the rest is a mode of the senses, brought to light.
The existence of the senses lies in the power (control) of the mind.
So that world is full of (composed of) the mind, we say.   ..6..

The world consists of the five kinds of sense perception and nothing besides. The five senses feel those perceptions as objects. And since the mind alone perceives the world through the senses, is the world anything other than the mind?

### 8

धिया सहोदेति धियास्तमेति  
लोकस्ततो धीप्रविभास्य एषः  
धीलोकजन्मक्षयधाम पूर्णं  
सद्वस्तु जन्मक्षयशून्यमेकम् ॥७॥

*dhiyā sahodeti dhiyāstameti  
lokastato dhīpravibhāsya eṣaḥ  
dhīlokajanmakṣayadhāma pūrṇaṃ  
sadvastu janmakṣayaśūnyamekam ..7..*

The world rises with the idea (thought, intelligence, mind) and sets with the thought (mind).
This, therefore, belongs to the light of the mind (thought).
The abode (glory, splendor, light) in which mind and world are born and decay (are destroyed, perish) is the perfect fullness,
the real thing (true Reality), with no birth and no decay (destruction, perishing), the One.  ..7..

World and mind rise and fade together, yet it is by the light of the mind that the world shines. The ground from which both arise and into which both set is that Perfection, which neither rises nor sets but always shines. That is Reality.

### 9

भवन्तु सद्दर्शनसाधनानि  
परस्य नामाकृतिभिः सपर्या  
सद्वस्तुनि प्राप्ततदात्मभावा  
निष्ठैव सद्दर्शनमित्यवेहि ॥८॥

*bhavantu saddarśanasādhanāni  
parasya nāmākṛtibhiḥ saparyā  
sadvastuni prāptatadātmabhāvā  
niṣṭhaiva saddarśanamityavehi ..8..*

Let worship (in all its kinds) of the Supreme with name and form
serve as spiritual practices for the vision (darshanam) of Truth.
Abidance in the true Reality, the attainment of that Self-Being (or: abidance in the True Reality, the attainment of the state (conviction) of That, is the Self),
and only this, is the vision of Truth (true vision). Know (understand) this.   ..8..

Whatever name or form we use in worshipping It, It carries us toward knowledge of the nameless, formless Absolute. Still, true Knowledge of the Truth is to see one's real Self in the Absolute, to sink into It and be one with It.

### 10

द्वन्द्वानि सर्वाण्यखिलास्त्रिपुट्यः  
किञ्चित्समाश्रित्य विभान्ति वस्तु  
तन्मार्गणे स्याद्गलितं समस्तं  
न पश्यतां सच्चलनं कदापि ॥९॥

*dvandvāni sarvāṇyakhilāstripuṭyaḥ  
kiñcitsamāśritya vibhānti vastu  
tanmārgaṇe syādgalitaṃ samastaṃ  
na paśyatāṃ saccalanaṃ kadāpi ..9..*

All dualities and all triads shine by leaning on some thing (Reality) for support.
(All dualities and triads shine for some thing (some reality) that supports them, on which they depend.)
When that is traced (sought), they all come loose and drop away.
For those who see the Truth (Being), there is never (at any time) any movement.   ..9..

‘Twos’ and ‘threes’ rest on a single thing, the ego. Ask in your Heart, ‘What is this ego?’ and find it, and they slip off. Only those who have found this know the Truth, and they will never be confused. [4]

### 11

विद्या कथं भाति न चेदविद्या  
विद्यां विना किं प्रविभात्यविद्या  
द्वयं च कस्येति विचार्य मूल-  
स्वरूपनिष्ठा परमार्थविद्या ॥१०॥

*vidyā kathaṃ bhāti na cedavidyā  
vidyāṃ vinā kiṃ pravibhātyavidyā  
dvayaṃ ca kasyeti vicārya mūla-  
svarūpaniṣṭhā paramārthavidyā ..10..*

How could knowledge shine where there is no ignorance?
And does ignorance shine where there is no knowledge?
“To whom do these two belong?” Inquiring in this way into the source (root),
abiding in one's own (true) nature is the Knowledge of the Supreme Truth.  ..10..

Knowledge does not exist without ignorance, and ignorance cannot exist without knowledge. To ask, ‘Whose is this knowledge? Whose is this ignorance?’ and so come to know the primal Self: this alone is Knowledge.

### 12

बोद्धारमात्मानमजानतो यो  
बोधः स किं स्यात्परमार्थबोधः  
बोधस्य बोध्यस्य च संश्रयं स्वं  
विजानतस्तद्द्वितयं विनश्येत् ॥११॥

*boddhāramātmānamajānato yo  
bodhaḥ sa kiṃ syātparamārthabodhaḥ  
bodhasya bodhyasya ca saṃśrayaṃ svaṃ  
vijānatastaddvitayaṃ vinaśyet ..11..*

Can knowledge that does not know the one who knows oneself (the Self)
be Knowledge of the Supreme Truth?
(or: Can that knowledge belonging to someone who does not know the Self (oneself) be so?)
(or: Can knowledge that does not know the Self, the one who knows, be so?)
Oneself is the support of knowing and of knowledge (or: of what is to be known);
the one who knows that shall destroy the two.  ..11..

To know every object without knowing the Self that knows is not knowledge; it is merely ignorance. Once the Self, the ground of knowledge, and the non-Self are known, knowledge and ignorance both drop away.

### 13

निद्रा न विद्या ग्रहणं न विद्या  
गृह्णाति किञ्चिन्न यथार्थबोधे  
निद्रापदार्थग्रहणेतरा स्यात्  
चिदेव विद्या विलसन्त्यशून्या ॥१२॥

*nidrā na vidyā grahaṇaṃ na vidyā  
gṛhṇāti kiñcinna yathārthabodhe  
nidrāpadārthagrahaṇetarā syāt  
cideva vidyā vilasantyaśūnyā ..12..*

Sleep is not knowledge. Perceiving (sensing, seizing, grasping, taking into the mind) is not knowledge.
In true Knowledge one grasps (seizes, perceives) nothing at all.
Different from the categories of sleep and perceiving
is Consciousness alone (indeed); Knowledge shines forth and is no void.   ..12..

True Knowledge is the absence of both knowledge and ignorance of objects. To know objects is not true knowledge. The Self shines by its own light, with nothing else for It to know and nothing else to know It, so the Self is Knowledge. It is not Nescience.

### 14

सत्यश्चिदात्मा विविधाकृतिश्चित्  
सिध्येत्पृथक्सत्यचितो न भिन्ना  
भूषाविकाराः किमु सन्ति सत्यं  
विना सुवर्णं पृथगत्र लोके ॥१३॥

*satyaścidātmā vividhākṛtiścit  
sidhyetpṛthaksatyacito na bhinnā  
bhūṣāvikārāḥ kimu santi satyaṃ  
vinā suvarṇaṃ pṛthagatra loke ..13..*

The Truth is the Consciousness-Self. Various (manifold) forms of Consciousness
are not established (accomplished) as separate (different). True Consciousness has no divisions.
(lit., The Truth is the Consciousness-Self. It is various (manifold) forms of Consciousness that are established as apart (different), yet true Consciousness is not divided.)
(or: The Truth is the Consciousness-Self. Various (manifold) forms of Consciousness are not established as apart or divided from the true Consciousness.)
Could changing ornaments (their transformation, modification) truly exist
here in this world, separately (apart), without the gold?  ..13..

The Self that is Awareness alone is true. Knowledge of many things is ignorance, and even ignorance, false as it is, cannot exist apart from the Self. The many jewels are false, because they cannot exist apart from the gold, and only the gold is true.

### 15

तद्युष्मदोरस्मदि सम्प्रतिष्ठा  
तस्मिन् विनष्टेऽस्मदि मूलबोधात्  
तद्युष्मदस्मन्मतिवर्जितैका  
स्थितिर्ज्वलन्ती सहजात्मनः स्यात् ॥१४॥

*tadyuṣmadorasmadi sampratiṣṭhā  
tasmin vinaṣṭe'smadi mūlabodhāt  
tadyuṣmadasmanmativarjitaikā  
sthitirjvalantī sahajātmanaḥ syāt ..14..*

“That” and “you” have “us” as their firm basis.
When that “us” is destroyed through (by means of) knowledge of the root (source),
with no “that,” “you” or “us” notion left, the One
State (Abidance, Stand) shines; it shall be the innate Self.   ..14..

‘You’ and ‘he’ turn up only when ‘I’ does. But when the nature of the ‘I’ is investigated and the ego is destroyed, ‘you’ and ‘he’ come to an end. What then shines as the One alone is the true Self.

### 16

भूतं भविष्यच्च भवत्स्वकाले  
तद्वर्तमानस्य विहाय तत्त्वम्  
हास्या न किं स्याद्गतभाविचर्चा  
विनैकसङ्ख्यां गणनेव लोके ॥१५॥

*bhūtaṃ bhaviṣyacca bhavatsvakāle  
tadvartamānasya vihāya tattvam  
hāsyā na kiṃ syādgatabhāvicarcā  
vinaikasaṅkhyāṃ gaṇaneva loke ..15..*

The past (what has been, the gone) and the future (what is to become) are each the present (being) in their own time.
To set aside the Truth of what exists at present (the present),
and keep thinking over (discussing) what is gone and what will be: will that not be laughable,
like counting in this world without the number one?   ..15..

Past and future depend on the present. The past was the present in its day, and the future will be the present too. The present is always present. Trying to know the future and the past without knowing the truth of time today is like trying to count without the number ‘One’.

### 17

क्व भाति दिक्कालकथा विनाऽस्मान्  
दिक्काललीलेह वपुर्वयं चेत्  
न क्वापि भामो न कदापि भामो  
वयं तु सर्वत्र सदा च भामः ॥१६॥

*kva bhāti dikkālakathā vinā'smān  
dikkālalīleha vapurvayaṃ cet  
na kvāpi bhāmo na kadāpi bhāmo  
vayaṃ tu sarvatra sadā ca bhāmaḥ ..16..*

Without us, where does any talk of space (direction) and time shine (appear)?
The play of space and time exists here only if we are the body.
We shine (appear) nowhere and at no time,
yet we shine everywhere and always.   ..16..

There is neither time nor space without us. If we are nothing but bodies, we are caught up in time and space. But are we bodies? Now, then and always, here, there and everywhere, we are the same. We exist beyond time and space.

### 18

देहात्मभावे ज्ञजडौ समाना-  
वेकस्य देहे हृदि दीप्त आत्मा  
आक्रम्य देहं च जगच्च पूर्णः  
परस्य मेयं तनुमात्रमात्मा ॥१७॥

*dehātmabhāve jñajaḍau samānā-  
vekasya dehe hṛdi dīpta ātmā  
ākramya dehaṃ ca jagacca pūrṇaḥ  
parasya meyaṃ tanumātramātmā ..17..*

In the body being the Self, the wise (the knower) and the ignorant (the unintelligent) are alike (the same).
(or: In the state of the body-self, the wise and the ignorant are the same.)
For the one, the Self illumines within the body, in the heart,
encompassing (spreading over) the body and the universe, the Perfect Fullness.
For the other, the Self is discernible only as the body
(the Self is only the measure of the body).  ..17..

For those who do not know the Self and for those who do, the body is the ‘I’. But for those who do not know the Self, the ‘I’ is confined by the body, while for those who know the Self within the body, the ‘I’ shines without bounds. That is how they differ.

### 19

अज्ञस्य विज्ञस्य च विश्वमस्ति  
पूर्वस्य दृश्यं जगदेव सत्यम्  
परस्य दृश्याश्रयभूतमेकं  
सत्यं प्रपूर्णं प्रविभात्यरूपम् ॥१८॥

*ajñasya vijñasya ca viśvamasti  
pūrvasya dṛśyaṃ jagadeva satyam  
parasya dṛśyāśrayabhūtamekaṃ  
satyaṃ prapūrṇaṃ pravibhātyarūpam ..18..*

The universe is (exists) for the ignorant and for the wise alike.
For the former, the seen universe alone (indeed) is the real (the true).
For the other, the seen has become a resting place; the One,
the Real (the Truth), the completely Full, shines without form.
(or: For the other, the One that has become the resting place of the seen,
the Real, the completely Full, the Formless, shines.)  ..18..

For those who do not know and for those who do, the world is real. But for those who do not know, Reality is confined by the world, while for those who know, Reality shines formless as the ground of the world. That is how they differ.

### 20

विधेः प्रयत्नस्य च कोऽपि वाद-  
स्तयोर्द्वयोर्मूलमजानतां स्यात्  
विधेः प्रयत्नस्य च मूलवस्तु  
सञ्जानतां नैव विधिर्न यत्नः ॥१९॥

*vidheḥ prayatnasya ca ko'pi vāda-  
stayordvayormūlamajānatāṃ syāt  
vidheḥ prayatnasya ca mūlavastu  
sañjānatāṃ naiva vidhirna yatnaḥ ..19..*

The talk (controversy) about fate and effort (striving) belongs to
those who do not know the root of these two.
For those who know well the root of fate and effort,
there is, indeed, neither fate nor one's own activity (free will).  ..19..

The debate over whether free will or fate prevails is only for those who do not know the root of both. Those who have known the Self, the common source of free will and fate, have gone beyond the two and will not come back to them.

### 21

यदीशितुर्वीक्षणमीक्षितार-  
मवीक्ष्य तन्मानसिकेक्षणं स्यात्  
न द्रष्टुरन्यः परमो हि तस्य  
वीक्षा स्वमूले प्रविलीय निष्ठा ॥२०॥

*yadīśiturvīkṣaṇamīkṣitāra-  
mavīkṣya tanmānasikekṣaṇaṃ syāt  
na draṣṭuranyaḥ paramo hi tasya  
vīkṣā svamūle pravilīya niṣṭhā ..20..*

A seeing of the Lord in which the seer
is not seen will be only a mental seeing.
The Supreme is, indeed, not other than the seer. To see Him
is to be absorbed (lit., having been absorbed, dissolved) in one's own source (root) and to abide there.   ..20..

Seeing God without seeing the Self that sees is seeing only a projection of the mind. It is said that God is seen only by the one who sees the Self; but whoever has lost the ego and seen the Self is none other than God.

### 22

आत्मानमीक्षेत परं प्रपश्ये-  
दित्यागमोक्तेः सुलभो न भावः  
नात्मैव दृश्यो यदि का कथेशे  
स्वयं तदन्नीभवनं तदीक्षा ॥२१॥

*ātmānamīkṣeta paraṃ prapaśye-  
dityāgamokteḥ sulabho na bhāvaḥ  
nātmaiva dṛśyo yadi kā katheśe  
svayaṃ tadannībhavanaṃ tadīkṣā ..21..*

See the Self, see the Supreme:
such is the Agama's (the scripture's) declaration. This is no easy bhava (state, meditation, contemplation). (or: This is easy, not a way of thinking.)
If the Self cannot, in fact, be seen, how can there be talk of the Lord? (how to seek the Lord?)
To become His food oneself is that seeing (is seeing Him).  ..21..

When the scriptures talk of ‘seeing the Self’ and ‘seeing God’, what truth do they intend? How is the Self to be seen? Being one without a second, it cannot be seen. How is God to be seen? To see Him is to be devoured by Him.

### 23

धिये प्रकाशं परमो वितीर्य  
स्वयं धियोऽन्तः प्रविभाति गुप्तः  
धियं परावर्त्य धियोन्तरेऽत्र  
संयोजनान्नेश्वरदृष्टिरन्या ॥२२॥

*dhiye prakāśaṃ paramo vitīrya  
svayaṃ dhiyo'ntaḥ pravibhāti guptaḥ  
dhiyaṃ parāvartya dhiyontare'tra  
saṃyojanānneśvaradṛṣṭiranyā ..22..*

Having given Light to the mind, the Supreme,
Himself within the mind (or: interior to the mind), shines hidden.
Here the mind must be turned back within (interior to) the mind.
From that union (absorption) comes the seeing (wisdom) of the Lord, and in no other way.  ..22..

His is the light that shines within the mind and gives it all the light it has. Without turning inward and merging in the Lord, how can we know the Light of lights with the mind's borrowed light?

### 24

न वक्ति देहोऽहमिति प्रसुप्तौ  
न कोऽपि नाभूवमिति प्रवक्ति  
यत्रोदिते सर्वमुदेति तस्य  
धियाऽहमः शोधय जन्मदेशम् ॥२३॥

*na vakti deho'hamiti prasuptau  
na ko'pi nābhūvamiti pravakti  
yatrodite sarvamudeti tasya  
dhiyā'hamaḥ śodhaya janmadeśam ..23..*

The body does not say “I.” In deep sleep,
no one says, “I did not exist.”
When that arises, everything arises. Of that
“I,” investigate (make clear) the birthplace with the mind (intelligence).   ..23..

The body does not claim to be ‘I’. And no one says, “There is no ‘I’ in sleep.” When ‘I’ comes up, all other things come up. Search with a keen mind for where this ‘I’ comes from.

### 25

देहो न जानाति सतो न जन्म  
देहप्रमाणोऽन्य उदेति मध्ये  
अहङ्कृतिग्रन्थिविबन्धसूक्ष्म-  
शरीरचेतोभवजीवनामा ॥२४॥

*deho na jānāti sato na janma  
dehapramāṇo'nya udeti madhye  
ahaṅkṛtigranthivibandhasūkṣma-  
śarīracetobhavajīvanāmā ..24..*

The body does not know. Being (Existence) is not born.
Something else, limited to the measure of the body, arises in between:
ego, knot, bondage, subtle body,
mind, bhava (mundane existence, samsara), jiva (the individual) it is named.  ..24..

The body, being matter, does not say ‘I’. Eternal Awareness neither rises nor sets. Between these two, tied to the body, the thought of ‘I’ rises. This is the knot of matter and Awareness. This is bondage, jiva, subtle body, ego. This is samsara, this is the mind.

### 26

रूपोद्भवो रूपततिप्रतिष्ठो  
रूपाशनो धूतगृहीतरूपः  
स्वयं विरूपः स्वविचारकाले  
धावत्यहङ्कारपिशाच एषः ॥२५॥

*rūpodbhavo rūpatatipratiṣṭho  
rūpāśano dhūtagṛhītarūpaḥ  
svayaṃ virūpaḥ svavicārakāle  
dhāvatyahaṅkārapiśāca eṣaḥ ..25..*

Its birth (springing up) is of form, resting on a mass of form.
It feeds on form, it gives up one form and grasps another,
and it has no form of its own. When it is inquired into,
it runs away, this ego-ghost.  ..25..

It rises by holding onto a form, stays by holding onto a form, and thrives by holding onto and feeding on a form. Dropping one form, it grabs another. When someone looks for it, it flees. Such is the ego-ghost, with no form of its own.

### 27

भावेऽहमः सर्वमिदं विभाति  
लयेऽहमो नैव विभाति किञ्चित्  
तस्मादहंरूपमिदं समस्तं  
तन्मार्गणं सर्वजयाय मार्गः ॥२६॥

*bhāve'hamaḥ sarvamidaṃ vibhāti  
laye'hamo naiva vibhāti kiñcit  
tasmādahaṃrūpamidaṃ samastaṃ  
tanmārgaṇaṃ sarvajayāya mārgaḥ ..26..*

When the “I” comes to be, all this shines (appears).
When the “I” dissolves, nothing whatever shines (appears).
So all this is completely of the form of “I.”
That search is the way (path) (or: the search for That is the way) to victory over all.   ..26..

When the ego rises, everything rises with it. When there is no ego, nothing else exists. Since the ego is thus everything, asking ‘What is this thing?’ is the extinction of all things.

### 28

सत्या स्थितिर्नाहमुदेति यत्र  
तच्चोदयस्थानगवेषणेन  
विना न नश्येद्यदि तन्न नश्येत्  
स्वात्मैक्यरूपा कथमस्तु निष्ठा ॥२७॥

*satyā sthitirnāhamudeti yatra  
taccodayasthānagaveṣaṇena  
vinā na naśyedyadi tanna naśyet  
svātmaikyarūpā kathamastu niṣṭhā ..27..*

The Real State (True Abidance) is where “I” does not arise,
and without the fervent search (inquiry) for the place from which it emerges,
that “I” is not destroyed (will not perish). If it is not destroyed (does not perish),
how can there be abidance whose nature is Oneness with the True (own) Self?  ..27..

We are ‘That’ when ‘I’ has not arisen. Without searching for where the ‘I’ comes from, how can one reach the self-extinction in which no ‘I’ arises? And without reaching self-extinction, how can one stay in one's true state, where the Self is ‘That’?

### 29

कूपे यथा गाढजले तथान्त-  
र्निमज्ज्य बुद्ध्या शितया नितान्तम्  
प्राणं च वाचं च नियम्य चिन्वन्  
विन्देन्निजाहङ्कृतिमूलरूपम् ॥२८॥

*kūpe yathā gāḍhajale tathānta-  
rnimajjya buddhyā śitayā nitāntam  
prāṇaṃ ca vācaṃ ca niyamya cinvan  
vindennijāhaṅkṛtimūlarūpam ..28..*

Just as one dives deep into a well of water, so within
one must dive (plunge) with an extraordinarily sharp intellect,
controlling (lit., having controlled) Prana and speech, and inquiring (reflecting);
thus one shall find the nature of the root of one's own ego.   ..28..

Keeping speech and breath under control and diving deep inside oneself, the way someone dives to find a thing that has dropped into water, one must seek out the source from which the aspiring ego springs.

### 30

मौनेन मज्जन्मनसा स्वमूल-  
चर्चैव सत्यात्मविचारणं स्यात्  
एषोऽहमेतन्न मम स्वरूप-  
मिति प्रमा सत्यविचारणाङ्गम् ॥२९॥

*maunena majjanmanasā svamūla-  
carcaiva satyātmavicāraṇaṃ syāt  
eṣo'hametanna mama svarūpa-  
miti pramā satyavicāraṇāṅgam ..29..*

Diving silently with the mind into one's true (own) root:
this consideration (inquiry) alone is true Self-inquiry.
“This I am; this is not my true (own) nature”:
such a correct idea (understanding, knowledge) is only a limb of (unessential to, secondary to, inferior to) true inquiry.   ..29..

Stop all talk of ‘I’ and, with a mind diving inward, search for where the thought of ‘I’ springs up. This is the way of wisdom. Thinking instead, ‘I am not this, but That I am,’ helps the search, but it is not the search itself.

### 31

गवेषणात्प्राप्य हृदन्तरं तत्  
पतेदहन्ता परिभुग्नशीर्षा  
अथाहमन्यत्स्फुरति प्रकृष्टं  
नाहङ्कृतिस्तत्परमेव पूर्णम् ॥३०॥

*gaveṣaṇātprāpya hṛdantaraṃ tat  
patedahantā paribhugnaśīrṣā  
athāhamanyatsphurati prakṛṣṭaṃ  
nāhaṅkṛtistatparameva pūrṇam ..30..*

By the search one reaches (lit., having attained, having reached) That within the Heart.
The “I” consciousness shall then fall, head bowed.
Then another “I” shines forth, preeminent (superior).
It is not the ego. It is the Supreme alone (indeed), the Perfect Fullness.  ..30..

When the mind turns inward asking ‘Who am I?’ and merges in the Heart, the ‘I’ hangs his head in shame and the One ‘I’ appears as Itself. Though it appears as ‘I-I’, it is not the ego. It is Reality, Perfection, the Substance of the Self.

### 32

अहङ्कृतिं यो लसति ग्रसित्वा  
किं तस्य कार्यं परिशिष्टमस्ति  
किञ्चिद्विजानाति स नात्मनोऽन्यत्  
तस्य स्थितिं भावयितुं क्षमः कः ॥३१॥

*ahaṅkṛtiṃ yo lasati grasitvā  
kiṃ tasya kāryaṃ pariśiṣṭamasti  
kiñcidvijānāti sa nātmano'nyat  
tasya sthitiṃ bhāvayituṃ kṣamaḥ kaḥ ..31..*

For the one who shines, having swallowed (consumed, caused to disappear) the ego,
what is left to be done?
He knows nothing other than the Self.
Who can conceive of his state?  ..31..

For one who is the Bliss of Self that comes from the extinction of the ego, what is there to do? He knows nothing besides this Self. How could his state be imagined?

### 33

आह स्फुटं तत्त्वमसीति वेद-  
स्तथाप्यसम्प्राप्य परात्मनिष्ठाम्  
भूयो विचारो मतिदुर्बलत्वं  
तत्सर्वदा स्वात्मतया हि भाति ॥३२॥

*āha sphuṭaṃ tattvamasīti veda-  
stathāpyasamprāpya parātmaniṣṭhām  
bhūyo vicāro matidurbalatvaṃ  
tatsarvadā svātmatayā hi bhāti ..32..*

Aha. The Veda has made clear (clearly opened) “That you are.”
And still, abidance as the Supreme Self is not fully attained.
Further inquiry is weakness (feebleness) of the mind (weakness of conviction).
That always shines, indeed, as one's own Self.   ..32..

The Vedas have declared, ‘Thou art That’. To think ‘I am That, not This’ instead of seeking and finding the nature of the Self and abiding in It shows a lack of strength. For That remains always and forever as the Self.

### 34

न वेद्म्यहं मामुत वेद्म्यहं मा-  
मिति प्रवादो मनुजस्य हास्यः  
दृग्दृश्यभेदात् किमयं द्विधात्मा  
स्वात्मैकतायां हि धियां न भेदाः ॥३३॥

*na vedmyahaṃ māmuta vedmyahaṃ mā-  
miti pravādo manujasya hāsyaḥ  
dṛgdṛśyabhedāt kimayaṃ dvidhātmā  
svātmaikatāyāṃ hi dhiyāṃ na bhedāḥ ..33..*

“I do not know myself” or “I know myself”:
such human expressions deserve to be laughed at.
Does the difference between seer and seen split this Self into two (twofold)?
The Oneness of oneself (the true Self), indeed, is the understanding (knowledge) that there are no differences.  ..33..

To say ‘I do not know myself’ or ‘I have known myself’ is laughable. What? Are there two selves, one to be known by the other? There is only One, the Truth of everyone's experience.

### 35

हृत्प्राप्य सद्धाम निजस्वरूपे  
स्वभावसिद्धेऽनुपलभ्य निष्ठाम्  
मायाविलासः सदसत्सरूप-  
विरूपनानैकमुखप्रवादाः ॥३४॥

*hṛtprāpya saddhāma nijasvarūpe  
svabhāvasiddhe'nupalabhya niṣṭhām  
māyāvilāsaḥ sadasatsarūpa-  
virūpanānaikamukhapravādāḥ ..34..*

Not having attained (obtained) abidance in the established Self (true) Existence
(or: not having attained (obtained) abidance in the accomplishment of the true (Self) state),
the reaching of the Heart, the true abode, in the innate True Nature (one's own innate nature),
“real, unreal, with form, without form, many (separate, different), one”
are a play (echo) of maya (illusion); they are proclamations of the mouth.   ..34..

The natural and true Reality lives forever in the Heart of all. Failing to realise It there and stay in It, and instead quarrelling, ‘It is’, ‘It is not’, ‘It has form’, ‘It has no form’, ‘It is one’, ‘It is two’, ‘It is neither’: this is the mischief of maya.

### 36

सिद्धस्य वित्तिः सत एव सिद्धिः  
स्वप्नोपमानाः खलु सिद्धयोऽन्याः  
स्वप्नः प्रबुद्धस्य कथं नु सत्यः  
सति स्थितः किं पुनरेति मायाम् ॥३५॥

*siddhasya vittiḥ sata eva siddhiḥ  
svapnopamānāḥ khalu siddhayo'nyāḥ  
svapnaḥ prabuddhasya kathaṃ nu satyaḥ  
sati sthitaḥ kiṃ punareti māyām ..35..*

Knowledge of the established Truth (Being) is the only accomplishment.
Other accomplishments are, indeed, comparable to (resemble) a dream.
How can the dream of someone now awake be true?
Does one who abides in Truth (Being) go near illusion again?  ..35..

True attainment is to discern and abide in the ever-present Reality. All other attainments are like powers enjoyed in a dream. When the sleeper wakes, are they real? Will those who stay in the state of Truth, having thrown off the unreal, ever be deluded?

### 37

सोऽहंविचारो वपुरात्मभावे  
साहाय्यकारी परमार्गणस्य  
स्वात्मैक्यसिद्धौ स पुनर्निरर्थो  
यथा नरत्वप्रमितिर्नरस्य ॥३६॥

*so'haṃvicāro vapurātmabhāve  
sāhāyyakārī paramārgaṇasya  
svātmaikyasiddhau sa punarnirartho  
yathā naratvapramitirnarasya ..36..*

The “He am I” inquiry, while in the “body is the Self” state (conception),
is helpful for the search for (the path to) the Supreme.
Once Oneness with the true (one's own) Self is accomplished, it is purposeless (useless, meaningless),
like a man's correct notion (conception) of his own man-ness (manhood).   ..36..

If we think we are the body, telling ourselves ‘No, I am That’ helps us abide as That. But since we always abide as That, why should we keep thinking ‘I am That’? Does anyone keep thinking ‘I am a man’?

### 38

द्वैतं विचारे परमार्थबोधे  
त्वद्वैतमित्येष न साधुवादः  
गवेषणात्प्राग्दशमे विनष्टे  
पश्चाच्च लब्धे दशमत्वमेकम् ॥३७॥

*dvaitaṃ vicāre paramārthabodhe  
tvadvaitamityeṣa na sādhuvādaḥ  
gaveṣaṇātprāgdaśame vinaṣṭe  
paścācca labdhe daśamatvamekam ..37..*

“Duality in inquiry, but nonduality
in the Knowledge of the Supreme Truth”: this is not good (holy) speech (doctrine, advice, proposition).
Before the search, when the tenth man was lost,
and afterwards, when he was found, the tenth-ness is one (that one only, the same).  ..37..

‘Duality during the search; unity upon attainment’: this doctrine is false as well. Both when he was eagerly seeking himself and later when he found himself, the tenth man in the story was the tenth man and no one else. (Ten men crossed a stream and wanted to be sure all were safe. Each one, in counting, left himself out and found only nine. A passer-by gave each a blow and had them count the ten blows.)

### 39

करोमि कर्मेति नरो विजानन्  
बाध्यो भवेत्कर्मफलं च भोक्तुम्  
विचारधूता हृदि कर्तृता चेत्  
कर्मत्रयं नश्यति सैव मुक्तिः ॥३८॥

*karomi karmeti naro vijānan  
bādhyo bhavetkarmaphalaṃ ca bhoktum  
vicāradhūtā hṛdi kartṛtā cet  
karmatrayaṃ naśyati saiva muktiḥ ..38..*

A man who regards himself with the thought “I do karma (action)”
becomes bound and enjoys (experiences) the result (fruit) of karma.
If inquiry in the heart removes (casts off) doership,
the triple karma perishes (is destroyed). That alone is Liberation.   ..38..

If we are the doers of deeds, we ought to reap their fruits. But when we ask, ‘Who am I, the doer of this deed?’ and realise the Self, the sense of agency is lost and the three karmas slip away. And this Liberation is Eternal.

### 40

बद्धत्वभावे सति मोक्षचिन्ता  
बन्धस्तु कस्येति विचारणेन  
सिद्धे स्वयं स्वात्मनि नित्यमुक्ते  
क्व बन्धचिन्ता क्व च मोक्षचिन्ता ॥३९॥

*baddhatvabhāve sati mokṣacintā  
bandhastu kasyeti vicāraṇena  
siddhe svayaṃ svātmani nityamukte  
kva bandhacintā kva ca mokṣacintā ..39..*

The anxious thought of Liberation lasts as long as the conception of bondage.
But bondage of whom? Through such inquiry,
in the accomplishment of himself (of itself), in one's own (true) Self, one is ever-liberated.
(or: The ever-liberated is established of itself, in one's own Self.)
Where then is the anxious thought of bondage, and where the anxious thought of Liberation?  ..39..

Thoughts of bondage and freedom last only while one feels, ‘I am bound’. When one asks oneself, ‘Who am I, the bound one?’ the Self, Eternal and ever free, remains. The thought of bondage leaves, and the thought of freedom leaves with it.

### 41

रूपिण्यरूपिण्युभयात्मिका च  
मुक्तिस्त्रिरूपेति विदो वदन्ति  
इदं त्रयं या विविनक्त्यहन्धी-  
स्तस्याः प्रणाशः परमार्थमुक्तिः ॥४०॥

*rūpiṇyarūpiṇyubhayātmikā ca  
muktistrirūpeti vido vadanti  
idaṃ trayaṃ yā vivinaktyahandhī-  
stasyāḥ praṇāśaḥ paramārthamuktiḥ ..40..*

With form, without form, and of the nature of both:
Liberation has these three forms, the knowers say.
The complete destruction of that “I”-notion (conception of individuality) which examines (ponders) this triad
is the Supreme Truth of Liberation.   ..40..

If someone asks, ‘Which of these three is final liberation: with form, without form, or with-and-without-form?’ I say that Liberation is the extinction of the ego that asks ‘With form, without form, or with-and-without-form?’

### 42

सद्दर्शनं द्राविडवाङ्निबद्धं  
महर्षिणा श्रीरमणेन शुद्धम्  
प्रबन्धमुत्कृष्टममर्त्यवाण्या-  
मनूद्य वासिष्ठमुनिर्व्यतानीत् ॥४१॥

*saddarśanaṃ drāviḍavāṅnibaddhaṃ  
maharṣiṇā śrīramaṇena śuddham  
prabandhamutkṛṣṭamamartyavāṇyā-  
manūdya vāsiṣṭhamunirvyatānīt ..41..*

The Saddarsanam, composed in the Dravida language
by the pure Maharshi Sri Ramana:
this excellent (highest, best) composition, afterward spoken
in the language of the immortals, was composed by Vasishta Muni.  ..41..

### 43

सत्तत्त्वसारं सरलं दधाना  
मुमुक्षुलोकाय मुदं ददाना  
अमानुषश्रीरमणीयवाणी-  
मयूखभित्तिर्मुनिवाग् विभाति ॥४२॥

*sattattvasāraṃ saralaṃ dadhānā  
mumukṣulokāya mudaṃ dadānā  
amānuṣaśrīramaṇīyavāṇī-  
mayūkhabhittirmunivāg vibhāti ..42..*
